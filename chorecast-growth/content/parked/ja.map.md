# Japanese: course map

The whole path from first words to speaking on your own (around JLPT N3), planned before the lessons are written.
Stages 1 and 2 cover hiragana, katakana and the start of N5; stages 3 and 4 the rest of N5 and N4; stages 5 to 8 reach N3.
Each stage is 10 units of 8 lessons, plus a story and a challenge per unit.

**For the fluent speaker reviewing this:** standard Japanese in the polite (です・ます) style first, with the casual
style kids use with friends alongside it. Stage 1 is written in hiragana with spaces between words, as in early readers,
and rōmaji is shown above the kana for 7 to 9-year-olds always and for everyone in stages 1 and 2. Katakana shows up
in loanwords (バナナ, ドア) from stage 1 and is taught in full in stage 2. Kanji start in stage 2. Kids trace each
lesson's kana over a faint copy (without stroke order yet). Please check that the phrases are natural for children, and
the rōmaji. Religion and politics stay out. いただきます and ごちそうさま are taught as table manners.

## Stage 2 (N5, part 2)

1. Katakana in full, with loanwords from everyday life.
2. First kanji: numbers, days, 人, 日, 月, 山, 川, 木.
3. Likes: すきです, だいすきです, きらいです.
4. My home: rooms and things; あります and います.
5. My day: telling the time; おきます, たべます, いきます.
6. Verbs in the polite form, and their negative (〜ません).
7. Clothes and the weather.
8. The body; あたまが いたい.
9. School subjects.
10. Describing people: たかい, やさしい, おもしろい.

## Stage 3 (N5 to N4)

1. The past: 〜ました, 〜ませんでした.
2. Adjectives in the past (たのしかった).
3. Plans: 〜たい, 〜に いきます.
4. Shopping: prices, counters and change.
5. Directions in town.
6. Food and cooking.
7. The て form: asking, joining actions.
8. Writing a short message.
9. A short story read aloud.
10. About 100 kanji read.

## Stage 4 (N4)

1. The casual (plain) form, for friends and family.
2. Giving and receiving: あげる, もらう, くれる.
3. Comparing: 〜より, いちばん.
4. Nature, animals and caring for the planet.
5. Travel and transport.
6. Money and work: jobs, pay and saving (built on Chorecast's jars).
7. Reasons: から, ので.
8. Festivals and seasons as culture, not religion.
9. Reading short texts for young readers.
10. About 300 kanji read.

## Stages 5 to 8 (towards N3)

- **Stage 5:** potential and conditional forms; telling events in order.
- **Stage 6:** the passive and causative; advice; longer conversations.
- **Stage 7:** keigo (polite speech) to recognise and use; summarising; everyday idioms.
- **Stage 8:** discussion with respect; adapting language for different audiences; a final talk on a topic they choose. About 650 kanji read and 3,000 words known.
