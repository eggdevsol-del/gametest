@lesson 1.1 | Kia ora | 👋 | home
hook: People all over Aotearoa say one friendly word every day. Let’s learn it!
word: kia ora | hello, thanks | 👋 | wave
say: «Kia ora, e Ari!» | Hello, Ari!
say: «Kia ora, e Mere!» | Hello, Mere!
learn: «Kia ora» is said all over «Aotearoa». It means hello and thanks, and it wishes someone well.
learn: When you say hello to someone with a short name, put «e» in front of it: «Kia ora, e Ari!»
play: hear | Which one is «kia ora»? | 👋* | 🌙 | 🍎
check: hear | What does «kia ora» mean? | hello and thanks* | good night | see you later
check: mcq | Ari says «Kia ora, e Mere!» What is Ari doing? | Saying hello to Mere* | Saying goodbye to Mere | Asking Mere a question
home: Say «kia ora» to everyone in your family today.
skill: mi-greet

@lesson 1.2 | Tēnā koe | ☝️ | home
hook: Te reo has a greeting for one person, for two people, and for a whole group.
word: tēnā koe | hello (to one person) | ☝️ | hold up one finger
word: tēnā kōrua | hello (to two people) | ✌️ | hold up two fingers
word: tēnā koutou | hello (to three or more) | 🙌 | wave both arms
rule: «Koe» is one person, «kōrua» is two people, and «koutou» is three or more.
learn: «Tēnā koe» is a little more formal than «kia ora». It’s a good way to greet a teacher or a kuia.
chant: «Tēnā koe»; «Tēnā kōrua»; «Tēnā koutou»
play: match | Match each greeting to who it’s for | «tēnā koe»=one person; «tēnā kōrua»=two people; «tēnā koutou»=three or more
check: hear | Who is «tēnā kōrua» for? | ✌️* | ☝️ | 🙌
check: mcq | You greet your whole class. You say… | «Tēnā koutou»* | «Tēnā koe» | «Tēnā kōrua»
home: Greet two people at once with «tēnā kōrua».
skill: mi-greet

@lesson 1.4 | Haere rā | 🚶 | home
hook: There are two ways to say goodbye. Which one you use depends on who is leaving.
word: haere rā | goodbye (to someone leaving) | 🚶 | wave as they walk away
word: e noho rā | goodbye (to someone staying) | 🏠 | wave as you walk away
learn: «Haere» means go and «noho» means stay. «Haere rā» is like “go well”, and «e noho rā» is like “stay well”.
rule: If you’re staying, say «haere rā» to the person leaving. If you’re leaving, say «e noho rā» to the people staying.
say: «Haere rā, e Ari!» | Goodbye, Ari! (Ari is leaving)
play: mcq | Mere is going home. Ari is staying. What does Mere say to Ari? | «E noho rā!»* | «Haere rā!» | «Mōrena!»
check: mcq | Your friend leaves your house. You say… | «Haere rā!»* | «E noho rā!» | «Pō mārie!»
check: tf | You say «e noho rā» when you are the one leaving. | true | The one leaving says «e noho rā» to the people staying.
home: When someone leaves today, say «haere rā».
skill: mi-farewell

@lesson 2.4 | Tēnā, tērā | 👉 | stage
hook: “This” and “that” come in three kinds in te reo.
word: tēnā | that (near you) | 👉 | point to the person you’re talking to
word: tērā | that (over there) | 🔭 | point far away
rule: «Tēnei» is near me, «tēnā» is near you, and «tērā» is far from us both.
learn: «Tēnā» is also in «tēnā koe». Same word, a different job!
say: «Ko wai tērā?» | Who is that (over there)?
play: sort | Near me, Near you, Over there | «tēnei»=Near me; «tēnā»=Near you; «tērā»=Over there | Where is it?
check: mcq | The boat is far out at sea. You call it… | «tērā»* | «tēnei» | «tēnā»
check: hear | Which one is «tēnā»? | 👉* | 👇 | 🔭
home: Point and say «tēnei», «tēnā» and «tērā» around your house.
skill: mi-this-that
