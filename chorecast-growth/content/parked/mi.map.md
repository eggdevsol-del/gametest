# Te reo Māori: course map

The whole path from first words to speaking on your own, planned before the lessons are written. Each stage is one
level of Te Aho Arataki Marau mō te Ako i Te Reo Māori (the Ministry of Education's curriculum guidelines for
English-medium schools), in its four pairs: **te whakatōtanga** (levels 1–2, beginning to use te reo), **te
tupuranga** (3–4, developing communication), **te puāwaitanga** (5–6, social competence) and **te pakaritanga**
(7–8, personal independence).

**For the kaiako reviewing this:**
- The level topics below follow the guidelines' structure as I understand it. Please check them against the
  guidelines themselves; I couldn't open the Ministry's copy, which is a download.
- Language is standard written te reo with macrons, following Te Taura Whiri i te Reo Māori's conventions. Please note
  anything your rohe says differently, and anything that should be said another way for children.
- Kept out, as for all Chorecast lessons: karakia and anything religious or tapu, the Treaty and history, and
  politics. Whether and how kids write a pepeha is your call.
- Every line is recorded by a fluent speaker before any kid hears it. There's no device voice for te reo, and it's
  never read by an English voice.

Each stage is 10 units of 8 lessons, plus a story and a challenge per unit: 80 lessons, and about 300 to 400 new words
and phrases. Early units are built on what kids do in Chorecast: chores, money, the island, family and payday.

## Level 2: Ko au tēnei (to write after Level 1 is reviewed)

1. **Tōku kāinga:** rooms and things at home (kāuta, moenga, tēpu, tūru, wharepaku), using kei hea and kei roto.
2. **Ngā mea e pai ana ki a au:** likes and dislikes with reasons (He pai ki a au te … nā te mea …; Kāore au i te pai ki …).
3. **Āku mea, ōku mea:** possessions. The a and o categories (tāku / tōku, āku / ōku), taught a few at a time.
4. **Te huarere me ngā kaupeka:** weather and the seasons (raumati, ngahuru, takurua, kōanga).
5. **Te tinana:** the body (upoko, kanohi, ihu, waha, taringa, ringa, waewae, makawe), and feeling sick or well.
6. **Ngā kākahu:** clothes and what to wear for the weather.
7. **Te wā:** telling the time, and what happens when (i te ata, i te ahiahi; He aha te wā?).
8. **Kei te aha koe?:** actions now (Kei te …), with the everyday verbs of chores and play.
9. **Kāore:** saying no and not (Kāore au i te …; Kāore he …).
10. **Ngā tāngata:** describing people: tall, short, kind, funny, and personality.

## Level 3: Kōrero ake (te tupuranga)

1. Past actions with i and kua (I haere au …; Kua horoia ngā rīhi).
2. Future plans with ka and ā (Ka haere au āpōpō; ā te Rāhoroi).
3. Asking and giving directions on the island and in town (ki mauī, ki matau, haere tōtika).
4. At the shop and the market: prices, quantities, paying and change (He aha te utu o …?).
5. Sports, games and hobbies; inviting and accepting.
6. The marae and everyday tikanga for visitors: what to expect, greeting with a hongi or a hariru, and manners (kept practical; the kaiako decides how far to go).
7. Food and cooking: a hāngī, a recipe, and sharing kai.
8. School and learning: subjects, the timetable, classroom talk.
9. Telling a simple story: beginning, middle and end, with ka … ka …
10. Writing a card, a message and a short email.

## Level 4: Taku ao (te tupuranga)

1. The passive in instructions and reports (Kua whāngaia te kurī e au).
2. Comparing (nui ake, iti iho, te mea nui rawa).
3. Health, feelings and looking after each other.
4. Plants and animals of Aotearoa, and caring for the environment (kaitiakitanga in practice: rubbish, water, native birds).
5. Places and travel around Aotearoa; Māori place names and what they mean.
6. Money and work: jobs, pay, saving and spending (built on Chorecast's jars).
7. Media: reading a notice, a poster and a short article.
8. Giving opinions, agreeing and disagreeing politely.
9. Conditions: if … then (Mēnā …, ka …).
10. A longer story read and retold.

## Level 5: Te ao pāpori (te puāwaitanga)

1. Talking about the past in more detail: when, where, with whom, why.
2. Plans, hopes and intentions (E hiahia ana au ki …; Ko taku whāinga …).
3. Social life: arranging to meet, phone and message talk, apologising and explaining.
4. Describing events: a game, a trip, a celebration such as Matariki (as a season and a time to gather, not a religious lesson).
5. Problems and solutions at home and school.
6. Reported speech (I kī ia …).
7. Reading short texts written for young readers of te reo.
8. Pronunciation and fluency: long vowels, the wh and ng sounds, and natural speed.
9. Explaining how to do something step by step.
10. Speaking to a group: a short talk.

## Level 6: Te ao o tōku hapori (te puāwaitanga)

1. Community: who does what, helping out, volunteering.
2. Sport and performance: describing a match or a show.
3. Persuading: giving reasons and examples.
4. Giving and responding to advice.
5. Jobs and the future: what I'd like to do.
6. Te ao tūroa: the natural world in more depth.
7. Stories from te ao Māori chosen by the kaiako (traditional stories are their call).
8. More complex sentences: because, although, so that.
9. Writing a letter or an opinion piece.
10. Conversation practice: longer exchanges on everyday topics.

## Level 7: Taku reo, taku tuakiri (te pakaritanga)

1. Expressing feelings and ideas with nuance.
2. Debating everyday topics with respect.
3. Understanding speakers at natural speed: interviews and conversations.
4. Formal and informal te reo: when to use which.
5. Idiom and whakataukī (proverbs) in everyday speech, chosen by the kaiako.
6. Summarising what was read or heard.
7. Describing changes over time.
8. Negotiating and solving problems in te reo.
9. Creative writing: a short story or poem.
10. A longer spoken presentation.

## Level 8: Kia māia (te pakaritanga)

1. Complex arguments and counter-arguments.
2. Speculating and hypothesising.
3. Analysing a spoken or written text.
4. Adapting language for different audiences.
5. Te reo in the community and media today.
6. Planning and running a discussion.
7. Explaining an issue that matters to the speaker.
8. Extended storytelling.
9. Reflecting on one's own learning.
10. A final kōrero: speaking confidently on their own, on a topic they choose.
