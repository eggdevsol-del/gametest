@lesson 4.3 | Structure a submission | 🧱 | townhall
hook: What does a good submission actually look like?
learn: Say who you are and why it matters to you: "I'm a 17-year-old who works part-time."
learn: Say whether you support or oppose it, or which parts.
learn: Give reasons, then clear recommendations. Short and specific beats long and vague.

@lesson 4.4 | Specific recommendations | 🎯 | townhall
hook: "Make it better" won't change a law. What will?
learn: Recommend a clear change: "Change clause 7 so the training wage ends at 18."
learn: Explain what will happen if they accept it.
learn: If you support something, say so too. Committees count support as well as opposition.

@lesson 4.5 | Speaking to a committee | 🎤 | townhall
hook: You've been invited to speak about your submission for 5 minutes. Scary?
learn: Committees often hear from people in person or online. You can usually ask to speak.
learn: Don't read your whole submission. Pick your two strongest points and a real example.
learn: Leave time for questions. It's OK to say "I don't know, but I can find out."
