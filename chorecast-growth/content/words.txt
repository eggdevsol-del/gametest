@lesson 1.1 | Capital letters and full stops | ✍️ | stage
hook: How do you know where a sentence starts and stops?
learn: A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop.
learn: Names of people, places and days start with a capital letter too: Ari, Napier, Monday.
learn: A sentence makes sense on its own: "The hen laid an egg."
try: "my dog is big." needs a capital M :: mcq | Which sentence is written correctly? | We fed the ducks.* | we fed the ducks. | We fed the ducks
play: sort | Needs a capital, Fine as it is | ari=Needs a capital; tuesday=Needs a capital; apple=Fine as it is; wellington=Needs a capital; chair=Fine as it is | Does it need a capital letter?
check: mcq | Which word needs a capital letter? | sydney* | river | house
check: tf | A sentence can end with a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark. | true
check: mcq | Where does the full stop go? | At the end of the sentence* | At the start | After every word
home: Write two sentences about your day. Check each one has a capital letter and a full stop.
skill: capitals-full-stops

@lesson 1.2 | Asking questions | ❓ | stage
hook: "Where is my hat?" How can you tell this is a question?
learn: A question asks something. It ends with a question mark (?).
learn: Questions often start with who, what, where, when, why or how.
learn: An exclamation mark (!) shows a strong feeling: "Watch out!"
try: "Can I help?" ends with a question mark :: mcq | Which one needs a question mark? | Where are the eggs* | The eggs are here | Here are the eggs
play: sort | Question mark, Full stop | What time is it=Question mark; I like apples=Full stop; How old are you=Question mark; The sky is blue=Full stop; Who is at the door=Question mark | Which mark goes at the end?

check: mcq | Which mark fits best at the end of "Look out, a wave"? | !* | ? | ,
check: tf | "How many eggs are there." is punctuated correctly. | false | It asks something, so it needs a question mark.
home: Ask a grown-up three questions about when they were your age. Write one down with its question mark.
skill: question-marks

@lesson 1.3 | Joining words | 🔗 | stage
hook: "I was hungry. I ate an apple." Can you join these into one sentence?
learn: Joining words link ideas: and, but, because and so.
learn: "and" adds more: I fed the hens and collected the eggs.
learn: "but" shows a change: I wanted to play, but it rained. "because" gives a reason: I ate an apple because I was hungry.
try: I was tired, so I went to bed :: mcq | Pick the best joining word: I wanted a swim … the pool was closed. | but* | and | because
play: match | Match the two halves | I like apples=and I like pears; I was cold=so I put on a jumper; I stayed inside=because it was raining; I tried hard=but I didn't win
check: mcq | I fed the dog … it was hungry. | because* | but | or
check: mcq | Which joining word gives a reason? | because* | and | but
check: tf | "but" can show that something changed. | true
home: Tell a grown-up about your day using and, but, because and so.
skill: conjunctions

@lesson 3.2 | ck or k? | 🦆 | island

learn: At the end of a short word with a short vowel, we usually write ck: duck, sock, back.
learn: After a long vowel sound or another consonant, we write k: cake, milk, park.
learn: Say the word slowly and listen to the vowel.
