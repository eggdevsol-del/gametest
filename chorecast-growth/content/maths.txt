@lesson 1.1 | Adding money | ➕ | stall
hook: An apple is 50c and a banana is 40c. Do you have enough with $1?
learn: To add money, add the cents, then the dollars.
learn: 100 cents makes $1.
try: 50c + 40c = 90c :: gap | 30c + 50c = _c | 80
play: gen | add | 4
check: gap | 60c + 40c = _c | 100
check: mcq | 100 cents is the same as… | $1* | $10 | 10c
check: tf | 50c + 50c = $1 | true
home: Add up the prices of two things on a shopping list.
skill: adding

@lesson 1.1 | What is a percentage? | 💯 | stall

check: gap | 20% of $40 = $_ | 8

home: Find a "% off" sign and work out the saving.
skill: percent

@lesson 1.1 | Ratios | 🧃 | kitchen
hook: Cordial says 1 part syrup to 4 parts water. How much water for 50 ml of syrup?
learn: A ratio compares amounts. 1:4 means for every 1 of this, 4 of that.
learn: Multiply both sides by the same number to scale up.
try: 1:4 with 50 ml syrup means 200 ml water :: gap | 1:5 with 40 ml syrup needs _ ml water | 200
play: match | Match the ratio to the mix | 1:1=Equal amounts; 1:3=One part to three parts; 2:1=Twice as much of the first; 1:10=A tiny bit of the first
check: gap | Paint mixes 2:3 blue to white. 4 cups of blue needs _ cups of white | 6
check: tf | 1:4 and 2:8 are the same ratio. | true
check: mcq | 3 boys to 2 girls in a group of 10. How many girls? | 4* | 2 | 6
home: Find a ratio on a packet at home.
skill: ratio

@lesson 1.1 | Simple vs compound | 🔁 | bank
hook: $1,000 at 5% for 10 years. Simple interest gives $1,500. Compound gives more. How much more?
learn: Simple interest: interest only on the original amount.
learn: Compound interest: interest on the original plus past interest.
learn: Compound value = start × (1 + rate)^years.
try: $1,000 at 10% for 2 years compound = $1,210 :: gap | $1,000 at 10% for 2 years simple = $_ | 1200
play: compound | Compare simple and compound growth | 1000 | 5 | 10
check: mcq | What does compound interest earn interest on? | The original plus past interest* | Only the original | Nothing
check: gap | $2,000 at 5% simple for 3 years = $_ | 2300
check: tf | Over long periods, compound interest grows faster than simple. | true
home: Use a compound interest calculator with a grown-up.
skill: compound

@lesson 1.2 | The real cost of a loan | 💳 | bank

home: Check a real payment plan and work out its total cost.

@lesson 1.5 | Expected value | 🎰 | stage
hook: A $5 raffle ticket. 1 in 100 chance to win $300. Worth it?
learn: Expected value = chance × prize, minus the cost.
learn: 1/100 × $300 = $3. You pay $5, so on average you lose $2 a ticket.
learn: Raffles for a good cause can still be worth it. Just know it's giving, not investing.
try: 1 in 10 chance at $20 = $2 expected :: gap | 1 in 50 chance at $100 = $_ expected | 2
play: debate | Is buying a lottery ticket every week a smart money plan? | No. The expected value is negative, so it's entertainment at best* | Yes. Someone has to win | Some people enjoy the dream for a small cost. Keeping it tiny and fun, not a plan, is the key.
check: gap | 1 in 4 chance to win $8, ticket $3. On average you lose $_ a ticket | 1
check: mcq | Negative expected value means… | You lose on average over time* | You always win | It's free
check: tf | Lotteries usually have negative expected value. | true
home: Work out the expected value of a school raffle.
skill: probability
