Privacy policy

Chorecast · last updated 4 November 2026

This is a draft for legal review. The highlighted blanks are still to be filled in.

Chorecast is a chores, pocket money and lessons app for families with kids aged 7 to 15. A parent or guardian sets it up. Kids use it on a paired iPad or tablet and never need an email address.

This policy explains what Chorecast keeps about your family, why, for how long, and who can see it. We follow the New Zealand Privacy Act 2020 and the Australian Privacy Act 1988.

The short version

  • We keep only what the app needs to work for your family.
  • No advertising, no third-party analytics, and no tracking across other apps or websites. We never sell your family’s information.
  • Kids’ job photos are deleted 60 days after you review the job. You can choose anything from 1 month to 1 year.
  • You can delete your account, or your whole family, at any time in Grown-ups, then Family. Everything goes straight away.

Chorecast is run by [company name and NZBN], [address]. For anything about privacy, email [privacy email].

What we keep

About grown-ups

  • Your name and email address.
  • Your password and recovery code. We store only a salted, one-way hash of each, so nobody at Chorecast can read them.
  • That you confirmed you are the parent or guardian, and the date of the terms you agreed to.
  • The devices you sign in on: the name you give each one, the kind of browser or device, and when it was last used.

About kids

  • First name, age, the avatar and colour they pick, and an optional passcode (stored as a salted hash).
  • Chores and jobs: what was sent, done, approved or sent back, star scores and pay.
  • Pretend money: their jars, statement, savings goals, interest and pretend investments. Chorecast never holds or moves real money.
  • Habits, their island and the items on it.
  • Lesson progress and their answers to lesson questions, so you can see how they’re going. For a language course, that includes each word they’ve learned and how they did when it came up for practice.
  • Job photos: the photos kids take to show a finished job, such as a tidy room or a clean bench. The app asks for a photo of the job, not of the kid.

About how the app is used

  • An activity log of what happens in the app, such as “job sent”, “pay approved” or “lesson finished”, with the time, the device and the kid it was for. It helps us fix problems and see which parts of the app help families.
  • Your internet address is held in memory for up to 15 minutes to slow down repeated wrong passwords and PINs. We don’t store it. Our hosting provider’s logs may record it for up to [hosting log period].

Things we don’t collect

We don’t collect location, contacts, kids’ email addresses or phone numbers, or audio. Read-aloud uses the voice built into the device, so lesson text isn’t sent anywhere to be spoken.

Speaking practice in language lessons uses the microphone: a kid hears a word, records themselves saying it and hears both. The recording stays on the device for that moment and is then thrown away. It’s never saved or sent anywhere. You can turn Speaking practice off in Grown-ups, then Character Growth.

Why we keep it

To run Chorecast for your family: to show the same chores, pay and progress on every device in your family, to keep kids’ balances right, to let you approve jobs from a photo, and to show you reports on their lessons. We also use the activity log, in totals, to find bugs and improve the app.

Who can see it

  • The grown-ups in your family, on their own sign-ins.
  • Your paired kids’ iPads, which show your family’s chores and kids. Grown-up settings on a kid’s iPad need your PIN.
  • A small number of Chorecast people, only when needed to help you, fix a problem or keep the service safe.
  • Companies that run parts of the service for us, under contract and only for that purpose: [hosting provider, e.g. Railway] for the servers and database, and [email provider] for account emails.
  • If you buy a plan in the app, Apple handles the payment. We get a receipt that says which plan your family has, not your card details.
  • Anyone the law requires us to tell, such as the police with a valid order.

Where it’s stored

Chorecast’s servers and database are in [country]. The app also keeps a copy of your family’s data on each signed-in device, so it opens without an internet connection. Signing out removes that copy.

Keeping it safe

Everything travels over HTTPS. Passwords, PINs and kids’ passcodes are stored only as salted hashes. Grown-up powers on a kid’s iPad need the PIN, and the server checks it. The server also checks kids’ pretend money so it can only change in ways the app allows. If there is a privacy breach likely to cause serious harm, we’ll tell you and the Privacy Commissioner as the law requires.

Kids’ privacy

Chorecast is used by kids under a parent or guardian’s account. The grown-up who signs up confirms they are the parent or guardian of the kids they add and agrees to this policy. Kids can’t make an account, buy anything or follow links out of the app without a grown-up. There are no ads and no chat with strangers.

Your choices

  • See or correct it. Most of it is in the app. For a copy of everything, email us.
  • Delete it. Remove a kid in Grown-ups, then Kids. Delete your sign-in or the whole family in Grown-ups, then Family, then Delete account.
  • Photos. Turn off Photo proof in Grown-ups, then Money rules, or keep photos for a shorter time.
  • Complain. Email us first. You can also go to the Privacy Commissioner in New Zealand or the OAIC in Australia.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we’ll tell grown-ups in the app or by email before it takes effect.
