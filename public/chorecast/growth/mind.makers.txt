@lesson 1.1 | Goals that work | 🎯 | stage
hook: "Get fitter", or "Run 2 km without stopping by March". Which goal are you more likely to reach?
learn: Good goals are specific (what exactly), measurable (how you'll know) and have a deadline.
learn: They should be challenging but realistic.
learn: Writing a goal down helps you remember it and commit to it.
try: "Read 10 books by December" is specific and measurable :: mcq | Which goal is best? | Save $100 for a bike by the end of term* | Have more money | Be rich
play: sort | Strong goal, Vague goal | Practise piano for 15 minutes, 5 days a week=Strong goal; Be better at piano=Vague goal; Learn 20 Spanish words by Friday=Strong goal; Get good at Spanish=Vague goal; Swim 10 lengths by the end of summer=Strong goal | Strong or vague?
check: mcq | What does "measurable" mean? | You can tell when you've done it* | It's long | It's secret
check: tf | A good goal has a deadline. | true
check: mcq | Which goal is realistic for most 14-year-olds? | Learn to cook five dinners this term* | Win the Olympics next week | Never make a mistake
home: Write one specific, measurable goal with a deadline and put it where you'll see it.
skill: goal-setting

@lesson 1.2 | Systems beat motivation | 🛠️ | workshop
hook: Motivation comes and goes. So how do people keep training on rainy, tired days?
learn: Motivation is a feeling, and feelings change. A system is what you do anyway.
learn: A system is a plan for when, where and how: "Every school day at 4 pm, I do 20 minutes of maths at my desk."
learn: Discipline is following the system even when you don't feel like it.
try: A set time and place are part of a system :: mcq | Which is a system? | Practise guitar every day after dinner* | Practise when I feel inspired | Practise someday
play: order | Build a study system | Choose a time; Choose a place; Remove distractions; Start, even if you don't feel like it
check: tf | You have to feel motivated before you can start. | false | Often starting comes first, and the motivation follows.
check: mcq | Discipline means… | doing what you planned even when you don't feel like it* | never having fun | being strict with other people
check: mcq | Why do systems help? | They don't depend on how you feel that day* | They're fun | They're complicated
home: Write a simple system for one goal: when, where and how.
skill: systems

@lesson 1.3 | Planning the steps | 🗺️ | stage
hook: A big assignment is due in three weeks. When do you start?
learn: Break big goals into smaller milestones, each with its own deadline.
learn: Work backwards from the due date: what must be done by when?
learn: Put the steps in a calendar so they're harder to forget.
try: The draft is due one week before the final :: mcq | An assignment is due in 3 weeks. When should you start? | This week, with a plan* | The night before | Never
play: order | Plan a 3-week assignment | Research in week 1; Write a draft in week 2; Edit and finish in week 3; Hand it in on time
check: mcq | Working backwards from a deadline helps you… | see what's due by when* | relax until the last day | avoid the work
check: tf | Breaking a big goal into milestones makes it easier. | true
check: mcq | Where's the best place to keep milestones? | A calendar or planner* | Nowhere | Only in your head
home: Use a calendar to plan the steps for your next big task.
skill: planning

@lesson 1.4 | Bouncing back from slips | 🔄 | island
hook: You planned to train five days but only managed two. Is the goal dead?
learn: Everyone slips. What matters is how quickly you get back on track.
learn: Look at what got in the way and adjust the plan, without beating yourself up.
learn: Progress isn't a straight line. It goes up and down, but it trends upward if you keep going.
try: Ask "What got in the way?" :: mcq | You missed your study goal this week. What's the best response? | Work out why and adjust the plan* | Quit the goal | Call yourself lazy
play: sort | Helpful response, Unhelpful response | Start again tomorrow=Helpful response; I always fail=Unhelpful response; Make the goal a bit smaller=Helpful response; It's pointless now=Unhelpful response; Ask someone to check in with me=Helpful response | Helpful or not?
check: tf | Progress usually goes up and down rather than in a straight line. | true
check: mcq | Talking to yourself kindly after a slip helps you… | get back on track sooner* | give up faster | forget the goal
check: mcq | Which is an adjustment? | Training 3 days a week instead of 5* | Giving up forever | Pretending it didn't happen
home: Think of a goal you slipped on. Write one change that would make it easier to restart.
skill: recovering

@lesson 2.1 | Stress and your body | 😮‍💨 | garden
hook: Before an exam your palms sweat and your stomach flips. Why?
learn: Stress is your body's alarm system. It releases chemicals like adrenaline that speed up your heart and breathing.
learn: A bit of stress helps you focus and perform. Too much for too long wears you down.
learn: Sleep, exercise, time outdoors and talking with people you trust all help your body handle stress.
try: A racing heart before a race is your stress response :: mcq | Which chemical speeds up your heart when you're stressed? | Adrenaline* | Vitamin C | Saliva
play: sort | Helps with stress, Adds to stress | A good night's sleep=Helps with stress; Scrolling late at night=Adds to stress; A walk outside=Helps with stress; Leaving everything to the last minute=Adds to stress; Talking with a parent=Helps with stress | Which is it?
check: tf | A little stress can help you focus. | true
check: mcq | Which helps your body recover from stress? | Exercise and sleep* | Energy drinks | Skipping meals
check: mcq | Who's a good person to talk to when stress builds up? | A parent or another trusted adult* | Nobody | A stranger online
home: Notice what your body does when you're stressed, and try one recovery habit this week.
skill: stress

@lesson 2.2 | Reframing | 🖼️ | stage
hook: You didn't make the team. Is that the end, or is it information?
learn: Reframing means looking at a situation from a different, more useful angle.
learn: "I failed" can become "I found out what I need to work on."
learn: Reframing isn't pretending everything's fine. It's finding the most useful true way to see it.
try: "I lost" becomes "I learned what the winners do" :: mcq | Which is a reframe of "I bombed my speech"? | "Now I know to practise out loud more"* | "I'm terrible at everything" | "Speeches are stupid"
play: match | Match each thought to a useful reframe | I didn't get the job=I got interview practice; My friend didn't text back=They might just be busy; I got a C=Now I know which topics to revise; It rained on the trip=A good test of our planning
check: tf | Reframing means pretending bad things are good. | false | It means finding a more useful true way to look at it.
check: mcq | Reframing helps you… | learn and move forward* | blame others | avoid trying
check: mcq | Which question helps you reframe? | "What can I learn from this?"* | "Why does this always happen to me?" | "Who can I blame?"
home: Reframe one disappointment from this week, in writing.
skill: reframing

@lesson 2.3 | Control what you can | 🎛️ | stage
hook: The weather, the referee, the other team. What can you actually control in a big game?
learn: Some things are in your control: your effort, attitude, preparation and how you treat people.
learn: Some things aren't: the weather, other people's choices, luck.
learn: Putting your energy into what you can control makes you calmer and more effective.
try: Your effort is in your control :: mcq | Which can you control? | How much you prepare* | The weather | What others think
play: sort | In my control, Not in my control | My effort=In my control; The referee's call=Not in my control; My attitude=In my control; Traffic on the way=Not in my control; How I speak to others=In my control | Can you control it?
check: tf | Worrying about what you can't control often makes you more stressed. | true
check: mcq | Your bus is late. What's in your control? | Calmly letting your teacher know* | Making the bus faster | The traffic
check: mcq | Focusing on what you can control makes you… | calmer and more effective* | lazier | angrier
home: Before your next big event, write two lists: what you can control and what you can't.
skill: control

@lesson 2.4 | Mental toughness | 🏔️ | island
hook: What makes someone mentally tough? Never feeling scared?
learn: Mental toughness isn't never feeling fear or sadness. It's feeling them and still doing what matters.
learn: It's built by doing hard things on purpose: a tough training session, speaking up in class, finishing what you start.
learn: Tough people also know when to rest and when to ask for help. That's strength too.
try: Speaking up in class even when you're nervous :: mcq | Which shows mental toughness? | Finishing a run even though it's hard* | Never feeling nervous | Refusing all help
play: sort | Builds toughness, Doesn't | Trying out for a team you might not get into=Builds toughness; Always picking the easiest option=Doesn't; Asking for feedback=Builds toughness; Quitting at the first mistake=Doesn't; Keeping a promise when it's hard=Builds toughness | Which is it?
check: tf | Mentally tough people never feel scared. | false | They feel fear and act anyway.
check: mcq | Asking for help when you need it is… | a sign of strength* | a sign of weakness | cheating
check: mcq | One way to build toughness is to… | do small hard things on purpose* | avoid anything difficult | wait until you feel brave
home: Choose one small hard thing to do on purpose this week.
skill: mental-toughness
