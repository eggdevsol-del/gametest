@lesson 1.2 | Peer pressure | 🫂 | garden
hook: Your friends want to skip class. You don't. What do you say?
learn: Peer pressure is feeling pushed to do what others do.
learn: Have a few lines ready: "Nah, I'm good." "I've got stuff on." Real friends respect your no.
play: chat | Friends want you to skip class | them: Come on, let's skip last period. Everyone's going. | me: Nah, I'm good. I've got a test to study for*/Fine, whatever you say/I'll do anything you want | them: Don't be boring. | me: Have fun. Catch you after school*/OK, I'll come/You're all losers
check: mcq | What's peer pressure? | Feeling pushed to do what others do* | A type of exercise | A school rule
check: tf | Real friends respect your choices. | true
check: mcq | A good way to say no? | Short and confident: "Nah, I'm good"* | A long angry speech | Saying yes anyway
home: Practise one "no thanks" line out loud.

@lesson 1.4 | Emotional spending | 🛍️ | stall
hook: Bad day, so you buy something to feel better. Does it work?
learn: Emotional spending is buying to change your mood. The good feeling usually fades fast.
learn: Try a pause: wait 24 hours before non-essential buys.
play: path | You've had a rough day and see a $60 hoodie online. | Add it to a wishlist and wait 24 hours => good: Tomorrow you decide you don't really need it. $60 saved. | Buy it straight away => ok: It feels good for a bit, but your goal fund shrinks. | Buy two to feel extra better => bad: Double the cost, and the feeling still fades.
check: mcq | What is emotional spending? | Buying things to change your mood* | Buying food | Saving
check: tf | Waiting 24 hours can reduce impulse buys. | true
check: mcq | A non-spending way to feel better? | A walk or talking to a friend* | Buying more | Scrolling shops
home: Try the 24-hour rule on your next want.
skill: spending

@lesson 2.1 | Kinds of pressure | 🧲 | garden
hook: Nobody said "do it", but everyone's doing it. Is that still pressure?
learn: Direct pressure is when someone asks or pushes you to do something.
learn: Indirect pressure is feeling you should, because everyone else is.
learn: Inside pressure is your own worry about missing out or not fitting in.
play: sort | Direct, Indirect, Inside | "Come on, just try it"=Direct; Everyone at the party is vaping=Indirect; "I'll look lame if I don't"=Inside; "Do it or you're out of the group"=Direct; All your friends have the same shoes=Indirect; Worrying you'll miss out=Inside | What kind of pressure is it?
check: mcq | Feeling you should do something because everyone else is. That's… | Indirect pressure* | Direct pressure | No pressure
check: tf | Pressure can come from inside your own head. | true
check: mcq | "Do it or you're out of the group." What kind of pressure is that? | Direct* | Inside | Indirect
home: Think of a time you felt indirect pressure. What did you do?
skill: peers

@lesson 2.2 | Exit plans | 🚪 | home
hook: You're at a party and things feel wrong. How do you leave without a scene?
learn: Have an exit line ready: "I've got to go, my mum needs me home."
learn: Agree a code word with your family. Text it, and they'll call or pick you up, no questions on the spot.
learn: Blaming a parent is fine. It gives you an easy way out.
play: chat | You're at a party that's getting out of hand | them: Hey, where are you going? It's just getting fun! | me: My dad's picking me up. I've got an early start*/I'm telling everyone's parents/Fine, I'll stay | them: Come on, five more minutes. | me: Can't, he's outside. See you Monday*/OK, whatever you want/You're all idiots
check: mcq | What's a family code word for? | Getting picked up, no questions on the spot* | A secret handshake | Your wi-fi password
check: tf | It's OK to blame a parent to get out of a situation. | true
check: mcq | A good exit line is… | I've got to go, my mum needs me home* | A long argument | Saying nothing and staying
home: Agree a code word with your family for when you need a pickup.
skill: peers

@lesson 2.3 | Online pressure | 📲 | online
hook: The group chat dares everyone to post a mean meme about a classmate. Everyone's doing it. Do you?
learn: Pressure online can feel stronger, because it's fast and lots of people are watching.
learn: You can leave a chat, mute it, or simply not reply.
learn: If a chat asks for pics or turns cruel, screenshot it and tell a trusted adult.
play: path | The group chat dares everyone to post a mean meme about a classmate. | Don't post, and message the classmate something kind => good: You didn't join in, and you helped someone feel less alone. | Post it so you fit in => bad: Posts spread and stick. It could really hurt them, and come back on you. | Post it, then delete it => bad: Screenshots last forever. Deleting doesn't undo the hurt.
check: mcq | A group chat turns cruel. What can you do? | Leave or mute it, and tell a trusted adult* | Join in | Share it more widely
check: tf | Deleting a post means nobody can ever see it again. | false | Screenshots can last forever.
check: mcq | Someone in a chat pressures you for a photo. You… | Say no, screenshot it and tell a trusted adult* | Send it to keep them happy | Keep it secret
home: Find how to mute or leave a group chat on an app you use.
skill: peers

@lesson 2.5 | Speaking up | 📣 | stage
hook: Your friends are teasing someone. You feel bad but stay quiet. Is staying quiet a choice?

learn: You can also check on the person later, or tell a trusted adult.

check: mcq | What's an upstander? | Someone who does something to help* | Someone standing on a chair | Someone who joins in
check: tf | A short line like "That's not funny" can make a difference. | true
check: mcq | You didn't feel safe speaking up. What else can you do? | Check on them later or tell a trusted adult* | Nothing at all | Join in next time
