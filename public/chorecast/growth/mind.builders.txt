@lesson 1.1 | How habits work | 🔁 | home
hook: Why do you reach for a snack the moment you walk into the kitchen after school?
learn: Habits have three parts: a cue (what starts it), a routine (what you do) and a reward (what you get).
learn: Cue: you get home. Routine: you grab a snack. Reward: you feel full and happy.
learn: Knowing the parts helps you build good habits and change unhelpful ones.
try: Cue: the alarm rings. Routine: get up. Reward: breakfast time :: mcq | "I see my running shoes, I go for a run, I feel great." What's the cue? | Seeing my running shoes* | Going for a run | Feeling great
play: match | Match each part of the habit | Cue=The bell rings at school; Routine=Pack up and line up; Reward=Lunchtime with friends
check: mcq | What starts a habit? | A cue* | A reward | A routine
check: tf | Changing the cue can help you change a habit. | true
check: mcq | Which is a reward? | Feeling proud after finishing homework* | The homework itself | The pencil
home: Pick one habit you have and work out its cue, routine and reward.
skill: habit-loop

@lesson 1.2 | Make it easy | 🎒 | home
hook: Why is it easier to practise guitar when it's out on a stand, not shut in a cupboard?
learn: Make good habits easy to start: put your book on your pillow, your water bottle on your desk.
learn: Make unhelpful habits harder: put the tablet in another room during homework.
learn: Stack a new habit onto an old one: "After I brush my teeth, I'll read for 5 minutes."
try: "After I eat breakfast, I'll pack my bag" :: mcq | Which makes reading more likely? | Leaving a book on your pillow* | Hiding books in a box | Never getting books
play: sort | Helps good habits, Gets in the way | Book by your bed=Helps good habits; Tablet next to your homework=Gets in the way; Clothes laid out the night before=Helps good habits; Phone pinging while you study=Gets in the way; Sports gear packed by the door=Helps good habits | Which is it?
check: mcq | "After I brush my teeth, I'll stretch for 2 minutes" is called… | habit stacking* | habit stopping | habit hiding
check: tf | Making a good habit easy to start helps you stick to it. | true
check: mcq | How could you make screen time before bed less likely? | Charge devices outside your bedroom* | Keep the tablet under your pillow | Turn the brightness up
home: Set something up tonight that makes a good habit easier tomorrow.
skill: habit-design

@lesson 1.3 | Tracking progress | ✅ | home
hook: How do you know if you're really sticking to a habit?
learn: A habit tracker is a simple chart where you tick each day you do your habit.
learn: Seeing a chain of ticks makes you want to keep it going.
learn: If you miss a day, it's no big deal. Just try not to miss two in a row.
try: Tick each day you practise :: mcq | You missed a day of your habit. What's the best move? | Get back on track tomorrow* | Give up completely | Pretend you didn't miss it
play: order | Set up a habit tracker | Pick one habit; Draw a grid for the month; Tick each day you do it; Look back at how you went each week
check: tf | Missing one day means your habit is ruined. | false | Everyone misses sometimes. Just try not to miss two days in a row.
check: mcq | Why does a tracker help? | You can see your progress* | It does the habit for you | It's homework
check: mcq | A chart of ticks on the fridge is a… | habit tracker* | shopping list | timetable
home: Draw a habit tracker for one habit and stick it where you'll see it.
skill: habit-tracking

@lesson 1.4 | Sticking with it | 🧗 | island
hook: Why do some people keep going when things get hard?
learn: Most things worth doing get hard in the middle: learning an instrument, a big project, getting fitter.
learn: Sticking with it means pushing through the hard middle instead of quitting at the first bump.
learn: Tips: break it into smaller goals, remember why you started, and celebrate small wins.
try: Remember why you started :: mcq | Halfway through a big puzzle, you're stuck. What helps? | Take a short break, then try a new section* | Throw it out | Decide puzzles are dumb
play: sort | Keeps you going, Makes you quit | Celebrating small wins=Keeps you going; Comparing yourself to experts=Makes you quit; Remembering why you started=Keeps you going; Expecting it to be easy=Makes you quit; Breaking it into steps=Keeps you going | Which is it?
check: tf | It's normal for things to get hard in the middle. | true
check: mcq | What's a small win worth celebrating? | Finishing the first chapter of a long book* | Nothing until the very end | Only first place
check: mcq | What's a good reason to keep going? | You'll learn and feel proud* | Nobody will notice | It's easy
home: Think of something you nearly quit. Plan three small steps to keep going.
skill: persistence

@lesson 2.1 | Your attention | 🎯 | stage
hook: Can you really do homework and watch a video at the same time?
learn: Your brain can only focus on one hard thing at a time. Switching back and forth slows you down and causes mistakes.
learn: Distractions like notifications, noise and screens steal your attention.
learn: Focus grows with practice, like a muscle.
try: Doing one thing at a time is faster :: mcq | Which helps you focus on homework? | A quiet desk with devices put away* | Music videos playing | Chatting online
play: sort | Helps focus, Steals focus | Phone in another room=Helps focus; Notifications pinging=Steals focus; A clear desk=Helps focus; TV on in the background=Steals focus; One task at a time=Helps focus | Which is it?
check: tf | Switching between tasks can make you slower and cause mistakes. | true
check: mcq | Focus is a bit like… | a muscle that grows with practice* | a light that's always on | something you're born with or not
check: mcq | What's a good first step before homework? | Put devices away* | Open a game | Turn on the TV
home: Do 15 minutes of homework with every device in another room. How did it feel?
skill: attention

@lesson 2.2 | The focus timer | ⏲️ | stage
hook: How long can you really concentrate before your mind wanders?
learn: A focus timer splits work into short blocks: work for 15 to 25 minutes, then take a 5-minute break.
learn: Knowing a break is coming makes it easier to keep going.
learn: In your breaks, move your body: stretch, walk, get a drink.
try: 20 minutes of work, then a 5-minute break :: mcq | What's a good break? | Stretch and get a drink* | Start a long video | Play a game for an hour
play: order | Do a focus session | Choose one task; Set a timer for 20 minutes; Work until it rings; Take a 5-minute break
check: mcq | Why use a timer? | Short blocks are easier to focus on* | It makes work longer | It's a rule
check: tf | Moving your body in a break can help you focus again. | true
check: mcq | After the break, you… | start another focus block* | check every app first | give up for the day
home: Try one 20-minute focus block this week with a timer, then a 5-minute stretch break.
skill: focus-timer

@lesson 2.3 | Thoughts aren't facts | 💭 | stage
hook: "I'm going to fail this test." Is that a fact, or a thought?
learn: Our brains often jump to worried thoughts, especially before something new or hard.
learn: A thought isn't always true. You can check it: "What's the evidence? What would I tell a friend?"
learn: Swap an unhelpful thought for a helpful one: "I've practised. I'll do my best."
try: "I'll never get this" becomes "I don't get it yet" :: mcq | Which is a helpful thought before a test? | "I've practised, and I'll do my best"* | "I'm going to fail" | "Everyone's smarter than me"
play: match | Match each unhelpful thought to a helpful swap | Everyone will laugh at me=Most people are busy thinking about themselves; I'm bad at maths=I'm still learning maths; It has to be perfect=Done well is good enough; I can't do it=I can't do it yet
check: tf | Every thought you have is a fact. | false | Thoughts can be checked, and some of them aren't true.
check: mcq | A good question to check a worried thought is… | "What would I tell a friend?"* | "Why am I so bad?" | "What's the worst thing ever?"
check: mcq | Swapping "I can't" for "I can't yet" is… | helpful self-talk* | giving up | lying
home: Notice one unhelpful thought this week and write a helpful swap for it.
skill: helpful-thinking

@lesson 2.4 | Handling worry | 🌊 | garden
hook: Your heart races before a speech. Is something wrong with you?
learn: Feeling nervous before something big is normal. It's your body getting ready.
learn: Calm your body with box breathing: in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4.
learn: Plan for the worry: "What could go wrong? What would I do?" Then talk it over with a parent or trusted grown-up.
try: Box breathing goes in, hold, out, hold, for 4 counts each :: mcq | Feeling nervous before a big game means… | your body is getting ready* | you'll definitely lose | you're sick
play: order | Do box breathing | Breathe in for 4; Hold for 4; Breathe out for 4; Hold for 4
check: tf | Feeling nervous before something new is normal. | true
check: mcq | If a worry won't go away, the best step is to… | talk to a parent or trusted grown-up* | keep it to yourself | look for scary stories online
check: mcq | Which helps a nervous body? | Slow breathing* | Running away | Rushing faster
home: Teach box breathing to someone in your family.
skill: handling-worry
