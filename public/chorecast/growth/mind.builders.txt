@lesson 2.2 | The marshmallow study | 🍬 | museum
hook: In the 1960s, researchers offered kids one treat now, or two if they waited. What did they find?
learn: In studies at Stanford University, kids were left alone with a treat. If they waited about 15 minutes, they got two.
learn: Some waited and some didn't. Kids who waited often used tricks, like covering their eyes or singing.
learn: Later research found waiting is easier when kids trust the grown-ups around them and feel secure. It's a skill that can grow.
play: mystery | Kids were offered one treat now or two later; Researchers left them alone with the treat; It began at Stanford University in the 1960s; It's named after a squishy sweet | The marshmallow study* | The lolly lottery | The cookie contest
check: mcq | How did some kids manage to wait? | Covering their eyes or singing* | Staring at the treat | Licking it a little
check: tf | Later research found waiting is easier when kids trust the grown-ups around them. | true
check: mcq | What does the study teach us? | Waiting is a skill you can practise* | Marshmallows are bad | Everyone waits the same
home: Try a mini waiting test with a grown-up, using any small treat.
