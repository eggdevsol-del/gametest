@lesson 1.1 | Living or not? | 🐌 | garden
hook: A rock, a tree and a snail. Which ones are alive?
learn: Living things grow, need food and water, breathe, and make more of their own kind.
learn: Plants and animals are living things. Rocks, water and plastic are not.
learn: Some things were once alive, like a dry leaf or a wooden table.
try: A snail eats, grows and breathes, so it's alive :: mcq | Which is a living thing? | A worm* | A rock | A cup
play: sort | Living, Not living | Tree=Living; Stone=Not living; Fish=Living; Cloud=Not living; Mushroom=Living; Spoon=Not living | Living or not?
check: mcq | Which shows that something is alive? | It grows* | It's shiny | It's heavy
check: tf | Plants are living things. | true
check: mcq | A wooden chair… | was once part of a living tree* | is alive | was never alive
home: Go outside with a grown-up and find five living things and five things that aren't alive.
skill: living-things

@lesson 1.2 | What plants need | 🪴 | garden
hook: Why does a plant left in a dark cupboard go pale and droopy?
learn: Plants need light, water and air to grow.
learn: Roots take in water from the soil. Leaves use sunlight to make the plant's food.
learn: Without light, a plant can't make enough food, so it gets weak.
try: A plant on a sunny windowsill grows well :: mcq | What do leaves use to make food? | Sunlight* | Moonlight | Music
play: match | Match each plant part to its job | Roots=Take in water; Leaves=Make food from sunlight; Stem=Holds the plant up; Flower=Makes seeds
check: mcq | Which does a plant NOT need to grow? | Toys* | Water | Light
check: tf | Roots take in water from the soil. | true
check: mcq | A plant left in the dark will probably… | go pale and weak* | grow faster | turn blue
home: Grow a bean seed in a cup of damp cotton wool and watch it each day.
skill: plant-needs

@lesson 1.3 | Animal groups | 🐾 | island
hook: A kiwi, a gecko and a dog. Are they in the same group?
learn: Scientists sort animals into groups by what they're like.
learn: Mammals have hair or fur and feed their babies milk. Birds have feathers and lay eggs.
learn: Reptiles have dry, scaly skin. Fish have fins and gills and live in water.
try: A dog has fur, so it's a mammal :: mcq | A kiwi has feathers. It's a… | bird* | mammal | fish
play: sort | Mammal, Bird, Fish, Reptile | Cow=Mammal; Tūī=Bird; Snapper=Fish; Gecko=Reptile; Dolphin=Mammal; Kererū=Bird | Which group is it in?
check: mcq | Which group has feathers? | Birds* | Fish | Mammals
check: tf | A dolphin is a fish. | false | Dolphins breathe air and feed their babies milk, so they're mammals.
check: mcq | Which animal has dry, scaly skin? | A lizard* | A sheep | A sparrow
home: Sort the animals in a picture book into mammals, birds, fish and reptiles.
skill: animal-groups

@lesson 1.4 | Life cycles | 🦋 | garden
hook: How does a caterpillar turn into a butterfly?
learn: A life cycle is how a living thing starts life, grows and makes new young.
learn: A butterfly's life cycle goes egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly.
learn: A frog's life cycle goes egg, tadpole, froglet, frog.
try: A caterpillar hatches from an egg :: mcq | What comes after the caterpillar? | Chrysalis* | Egg | Tadpole
play: order | Put the butterfly's life cycle in order | Egg; Caterpillar; Chrysalis; Butterfly
check: mcq | A young frog with a tail and no legs is called a… | tadpole* | caterpillar | chick
check: tf | A butterfly lays eggs. | true
check: mcq | What does a caterpillar spend most of its time doing? | Eating and growing* | Flying | Swimming
home: Look for caterpillars or eggs on plants with a grown-up. Swan plants are great for monarchs.
skill: life-cycles

@lesson 2.1 | What is it made of? | 🧱 | workshop
hook: Why are windows made of glass and not wood?
learn: Materials are what things are made of: wood, metal, glass, plastic, wool.
learn: Each material has properties: hard or soft, see-through or not, waterproof or not, bendy or stiff.
learn: We choose materials for their properties. Glass is see-through, so we can look out of windows.
try: Rubber is waterproof, so it's good for gumboots :: mcq | Why are windows made of glass? | Glass is see-through* | Glass is soft | Glass is bendy
play: match | Match each thing to a good material for it | Window=Glass; Spoon=Metal; Jumper=Wool; Table=Wood
check: mcq | Which material is waterproof? | Plastic* | Paper | Cotton
check: tf | Metal is a good material for a pot. | true
check: mcq | Which property makes rubber good for gumboots? | It's waterproof* | It's see-through | It's crunchy
home: Find five things at home and say what each is made of, and why.
skill: materials

@lesson 2.2 | Float or sink? | 🛥️ | island
hook: A huge ship floats but a small stone sinks. How?
learn: Some things float on water and some sink.
learn: It isn't only about weight. Things that are packed tight for their size, like a stone, usually sink.
learn: Shape matters too. A ball of foil sinks, but a foil boat can float.
try: A cork floats :: mcq | Which will probably float? | A leaf* | A coin | A key
play: sort | Floats, Sinks | Cork=Floats; Stone=Sinks; Apple=Floats; Marble=Sinks; Plastic duck=Floats; Key=Sinks | Float or sink?
check: mcq | A flat foil boat floats, but a scrunched foil ball sinks. What changed? | The shape* | The material | The colour
check: tf | Heavy things always sink. | false | A big ship is heavy, but it floats because of its shape.
check: mcq | Which will probably sink? | A metal spoon* | A ping-pong ball | A cork
home: Test five things in a sink of water with a grown-up. Guess first, then test.
skill: float-sink

@lesson 2.3 | Solids and liquids | 🧊 | kitchen
hook: Ice and water are made of the same stuff. Why are they so different?
learn: A solid keeps its shape: a rock, an ice cube, a pencil.
learn: A liquid flows and takes the shape of its container: water, milk, juice.
learn: Water can be a solid (ice), a liquid (water) or a gas (water vapour, which we can't see).
try: Milk is a liquid :: mcq | Which is a solid? | A brick* | Juice | Rain
play: sort | Solid, Liquid | Ice cube=Solid; Honey=Liquid; Spoon=Solid; Orange juice=Liquid; Book=Solid; Rain=Liquid | Solid or liquid?
check: mcq | Which takes the shape of its container? | A liquid* | A solid | A rock
check: tf | Ice is solid water. | true
check: mcq | Water vapour is water as a… | gas* | solid | liquid
home: Pour water into three different shaped containers with a grown-up. Does it keep its shape?
skill: solids-liquids

@lesson 2.4 | Melting and freezing | 🍦 | kitchen
hook: Why does an ice block drip on a hot day?
learn: Heating can melt a solid into a liquid: ice melts into water, butter melts in a warm pan.
learn: Cooling can freeze a liquid into a solid: water freezes into ice in the freezer.
learn: Melting and freezing can go back and forth. Melt an ice cube, and you can freeze the water again.
try: Chocolate melts in a warm hand :: mcq | Water left in the freezer will… | freeze into ice* | boil | disappear
play: sort | Melting, Freezing | Ice cream on a hot day=Melting; Water in an ice tray in the freezer=Freezing; Butter in a warm pan=Melting; A puddle on a frosty night=Freezing; A snowman in the sun=Melting | Melting or freezing?
check: mcq | What makes ice melt? | Heat* | Cold | Darkness
check: tf | Melted ice can be frozen again. | true
check: mcq | Which will melt fastest? | An ice cube in the sun* | An ice cube in the freezer | An ice cube in the fridge
home: Race two ice cubes with a grown-up: one in the sun and one in the shade. Which melts first?
skill: melting-freezing

@lesson 3.1 | Day and night | 🌍 | island
hook: Where does the sun go at night?
learn: The Earth spins around like a top. One full spin takes about 24 hours: one day.
learn: The side of the Earth facing the sun has day. The side facing away has night.
learn: The sun doesn't travel around us. It only looks like it moves because the Earth is spinning.
try: When it's day in {nz:New Zealand~au:Australia}, it's night on the other side of the world :: mcq | What causes day and night? | The Earth spinning* | The sun switching off | Clouds covering the sun
play: order | Put the day in order | Sunrise; Midday; Sunset; Night
check: mcq | How long does the Earth take to spin around once? | About 24 hours* | About 1 hour | About 1 year
check: tf | It's night on the side of the Earth facing away from the sun. | true
check: mcq | The sun seems to move across the sky because… | the Earth is spinning* | the sun is walking | the wind blows it
home: In a dark room, shine a torch on a ball with a grown-up. Turn the ball to see day and night.
skill: day-night

@lesson 3.2 | The Moon | 🌙 | island
hook: Why does the Moon look like a banana some nights and a round plate on others?
learn: The Moon doesn't make its own light. It reflects light from the sun.
learn: The Moon travels around the Earth. It takes about a month.
learn: As it travels, we see different amounts of its sunlit side. These shapes are called phases.
try: At a full moon we see the whole lit-up side :: mcq | Where does moonlight come from? | The sun* | The Moon makes it | Stars
play: match | Match each phase to how it looks | Full moon=A whole circle; New moon=Almost nothing; Half moon=Half a circle; Crescent=A thin curve
check: mcq | About how long does the Moon take to go around the Earth? | A month* | A day | A year
check: tf | The Moon changes shape because it breaks into pieces. | false | We just see different amounts of its sunlit side.
check: mcq | The Moon goes around… | the Earth* | Mars | the stars
home: Look at the Moon each clear night for a week and draw its shape.
skill: moon-phases

@lesson 3.3 | Seasons | 🍂 | garden
hook: Why is December warm here but cold in many other countries?
learn: There are four seasons: summer, autumn, winter and spring.
learn: The Earth is tilted. For part of the year our half of the world leans towards the sun, and that's summer. Later it leans away, and that's winter.
learn: {nz:New Zealand~au:Australia} is in the bottom half of the world, so our summer is in December, January and February.
try: When our half of the world leans towards the sun, it's summer :: mcq | Here, December is in… | summer* | winter | autumn
play: order | Put the seasons in order, starting with summer | Summer; Autumn; Winter; Spring
check: mcq | How long does the Earth take to go around the sun? | One year* | One day | One month
check: tf | When it's summer here, it's winter in places like England. | true
check: mcq | In autumn, many trees… | drop their leaves* | grow new flowers | turn into ice
home: Draw what your family does in each season, with a grown-up.
skill: seasons

@lesson 3.4 | Weather and the water cycle | 🌧️ | garden
hook: Where does rain come from?
learn: The sun warms water in seas, lakes and puddles. Some of it turns into water vapour, a gas we can't see. This is evaporation.
learn: High up, the vapour cools and turns into tiny drops that make clouds. This is condensation.
learn: When the drops get big and heavy, they fall as rain. Then it all starts again. This is the water cycle.
try: Puddles dry up because of evaporation :: mcq | What are clouds made of? | Tiny drops of water* | Cotton | Smoke
play: order | Put the water cycle in order | The sun warms the water; Water evaporates into the air; The vapour cools into clouds; Rain falls
check: mcq | When water turns into a gas we can't see, it's called… | evaporation* | freezing | melting
check: tf | The same water goes round and round in the water cycle. | true
check: mcq | Why do puddles disappear on a sunny day? | The water evaporates* | Birds drink it all | It turns into sand
home: Draw a chalk line around a puddle with a grown-up, and check it again later in the day.
skill: water-cycle
