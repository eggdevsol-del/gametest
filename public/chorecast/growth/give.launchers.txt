@lesson 1.2 | Governance | 🏛️ | townhall
hook: Who checks that a charity spends money honestly?
learn: Boards oversee charities and companies. They set direction and hold leaders to account.
learn: Audits, annual reports and regulators add checks.
learn: When governance fails, money can go missing and trust collapses.
play: match | Match each check to what it does | Board=Oversees leaders and strategy; Audit=Independent check of the accounts; Annual report=Public account of money and results; Regulator=Government body that enforces the rules
check: mcq | Who oversees a charity's leaders? | The board* | The customers | Nobody
check: tf | Independent audits help keep organisations honest. | true
check: mcq | What happens when governance fails? | Money can go missing and trust collapses* | Nothing | Everyone gets a bonus
home: Read the board page of a charity or company you know.
skill: governance

@lesson 1.3 | Andrew Carnegie | 📚 | museum
hook: One of history's richest men said dying rich was a disgrace. What did he do?
learn: Andrew Carnegie made a fortune in steel in the 1800s.
learn: He gave much of it away, funding more than 2,500 public libraries.
learn: Critics point out his steel workers worked long hours for low pay. Both parts of the story matter.
play: debate | Does giving a fortune away make up for how it was made? | Both matter: how money is made and how it's given* | Yes, giving cancels out everything | Carnegie's libraries helped millions of people learn for free, which is a real and lasting good.
check: mcq | What did Carnegie fund? | More than 2,500 public libraries* | Space rockets | Video games
check: tf | Carnegie's story includes both generosity and criticism. | true
check: mcq | Where did his fortune come from? | Steel* | Software | Sport
home: Visit or look up the history of your local library.
skill: philanthropy

@lesson 1.4 | Mission, model, money | 🧭 | stall
hook: You want to start a social enterprise. What three things must line up?
learn: Mission: the problem you're solving.
learn: Model: how you'll earn money doing it.
learn: Money: enough to survive. A great mission with no income won't last.
play: match | Match each to an example from a coffee cart that trains young people | Mission=Help young people into work; Model=Sell coffee at events; Money=Prices that cover wages, beans and training; Impact=Number of trainees who get jobs
check: mcq | Why does the model matter? | Without income the mission can't last* | It doesn't | Only the logo matters
check: tf | A social enterprise needs to cover its costs. | true
check: mcq | How might you measure this coffee cart's impact? | Trainees who get jobs* | Cups of coffee only | Number of likes
home: Sketch mission, model and money for a social enterprise idea.
skill: impact

@lesson 1.5 | Changing systems | 🗳️ | townhall
hook: Food banks help hungry families. What would mean fewer families needed them?
learn: Charity helps people now. Policy change can fix causes, like wages or housing.
learn: Voting, submissions on laws and joining campaigns are ways to change systems.
learn: {nz:You can enrol to vote at 17 and vote from 18.~au:You can enrol at 16 and must enrol and vote from 18.}
play: sort | Helps now, Fixes causes | Donating to a food bank=Helps now; Making a submission on a housing law=Fixes causes; Volunteering at a soup kitchen=Helps now; Voting in an election=Fixes causes; Giving a coat to a coat drive=Helps now; Campaigning for a fairer minimum wage=Fixes causes | Which is which?
check: mcq | What can fix the causes of a problem? | Policy change* | Ignoring it | Only buying things
check: tf | Charity and policy change can both matter. | true
check: mcq | {nz:At what age can you enrol to vote in NZ?~au:At what age can you enrol to vote in Australia?} | {nz:17~au:16}* | 12 | 25
home: Check how to enrol to vote at {nz:vote.nz~au:aec.gov.au}.
skill: civics
