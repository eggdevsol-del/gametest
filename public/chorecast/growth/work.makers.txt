@lesson 1.1 | Your first CV | 📄 | workshop
hook: You've never had a job. What goes on your CV?
learn: A CV lists your contact details, skills, experience and references.
learn: Experience can include volunteering, school leadership, sport and helping at home or for neighbours.
learn: Keep it to one or two pages and check the spelling.
play: sort | Put it on, Leave it off | Volunteering at the food bank=Put it on; Your favourite pizza=Leave it off; School sports captain=Put it on; Lawn-mowing for neighbours=Put it on; Your password=Leave it off; A reference from your coach=Put it on | What belongs on a first CV?
check: mcq | What counts as experience on a first CV? | Volunteering and school roles* | Only paid jobs | Nothing
check: tf | Spelling mistakes on a CV don't matter. | false
check: mcq | How long should a first CV be? | One or two pages* | Ten pages | One line
home: Start a CV draft with three things you've done that show responsibility.
skill: cv

@lesson 1.2 | Applying | 📮 | online
hook: A cafe posts "Help wanted". What's your next move?
learn: Read the ad carefully. Note what they ask for.
learn: Write a short cover message about why you'd be good, using their words.
learn: Apply on time and follow their instructions exactly.
play: diff | The job ad, and a cover message. Tap the lines that DON'T match what the ad asks for. | Must be available weekends; Friendly with customers; Apply by Friday with your CV | I'm free on weekends; I love chatting with customers; I'll send my CV next month
check: mcq | What should a cover message do? | Show how you match what they asked for* | Repeat your whole CV | Talk about your holidays
check: tf | Applying after the deadline is fine. | false
check: mcq | The ad says "apply by email". You should… | Apply by email* | Turn up and shout | Call at midnight
home: Find a real part-time job ad and list what it asks for.
skill: applying

@lesson 1.3 | Interviews | 🎤 | stage
hook: The interviewer asks, "Tell me about yourself." What do you say?
learn: Prepare 3 short stories that show your skills, like a time you solved a problem.
learn: Arrive early, dress neatly, make eye contact and ask a question at the end.
play: chat | Your first interview at a supermarket | them: Thanks for coming in. Tell me a bit about yourself. | me: I'm in Year 11, I play netball and I help run the school canteen on Fridays*/Um, I dunno/I need money | them: Great. Tell me about a time you dealt with a problem. | me: Once the canteen ran out of change, so I got smaller notes from the office and kept the line moving*/I never have problems/My friend did it | them: Any questions for us? | me: What does a typical shift look like?*/No/When do I get paid more?
check: mcq | What helps most in an interview? | Prepared examples of your skills* | Wearing sunglasses | Talking about money only
check: tf | Asking a question at the end shows interest. | true
check: mcq | When should you arrive? | A few minutes early* | Right on time or a bit late | Whenever
home: Practise answering "Tell me about yourself" with a grown-up.
skill: interview

@lesson 1.4 | Your rights at work | ⚖️ | townhall
hook: Your boss says teens don't get breaks. True?
learn: {nz:In New Zealand, employees get rest and meal breaks, a written employment agreement and at least the minimum wage that applies to them.~au:In Australia, most employees are covered by an award or agreement setting minimum pay, breaks and conditions.}
learn: {nz:Employment New Zealand has free information and help.~au:The Fair Work Ombudsman has free information and help.}
learn: Young workers have the same right to a safe workplace as everyone else.
play: tf | Young workers don't have a right to a safe workplace. | false | Everyone has a right to a safe workplace.
check: mcq | {nz:What should every NZ employee have?~au:What sets minimum pay and conditions for most Australian employees?} | {nz:A written employment agreement~au:An award or agreement}* | {nz:A company car~au:Their boss's mood} | {nz:Nothing~au:Their friends}
check: mcq | Where can you get free help about work rights? | {nz:Employment New Zealand~au:The Fair Work Ombudsman}* | A gaming forum | Nowhere
check: tf | You should be paid for all the hours you work. | true
home: Look up "{nz:minimum wage NZ~au:minimum wage Australia}" on the official site and note today's rate.
skill: workrights

@lesson 1.5 | Pro communication | 📧 | online
hook: You're sick and can't make your shift. How do you tell your boss?
learn: Tell them as early as you can, the way they asked you to.
learn: Be clear, polite and brief. Say what you'll do next.
play: mcq | Which message is the most professional? | Hi Sam, I'm sick today and can't make my 4pm shift. Sorry for the short notice. I'll let you know about tomorrow by 6pm.* | cant come lol | Not coming. Sick.
check: tf | Telling your boss early is better than not showing up. | true
check: mcq | A good work message is… | Clear, polite and brief* | Long and full of emojis | Rude but honest
check: mcq | If you're running late, you should… | Tell your boss before your shift starts* | Hope they don't notice | Go home
home: Write a practice "I'm sick" message to a pretend boss.
skill: communication

@lesson 1.6 | Solving problems at work | 🧩 | workshop
hook: The till is $10 short at the end of your shift. What do you do?
learn: Stay calm. Check the facts first.
learn: Tell your supervisor honestly, even if it might be your mistake.
play: path | The till is $10 short at the end of your shift. | Count again and tell your supervisor => good: Honesty builds trust, and it was a miscounted $10 note. | Put in $10 of your own and say nothing => ok: Kind, but the business needs to know about mistakes so they can fix the cause. | Change the numbers so it looks right => bad: That's dishonest and could cost you your job.
check: mcq | Something goes wrong at work. First step? | Stay calm and check the facts* | Hide it | Blame someone else
check: tf | Being honest about mistakes builds trust. | true
check: mcq | Who should you tell about a till error? | Your supervisor* | Nobody | Customers
home: Think of a mistake you made and how you'd handle it now.
skill: problemsolving
