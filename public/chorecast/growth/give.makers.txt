@lesson 2.2 | Before and after | 📏 | townhall
hook: After a tutoring programme, 70% of kids read at their level. Is that good?
learn: You can't tell without a baseline: a measure of how things were before.
learn: If 40% read at their level before, 70% is a big jump. If 68% did, it's tiny.
learn: Measure the same way before and after, or the comparison isn't fair.
try: 40% before, 70% after: up 30 points :: gap | 55% before, 80% after: up _ points | 25
play: slider | A beach had 300 bits of rubbish per 100 m before a campaign, and 120 after. How many fewer is that? | 0 | 300 | 180 | pieces | 5 | 5
check: mcq | What is a baseline? | A measurement from before you started* | The final result | A type of graph
check: gap | 200 people came before, 260 after. That's _ more | 60
check: tf | Measuring in different ways before and after makes the comparison unfair. | true
home: Pick a habit and measure it for a week before you try to change it.
skill: impact

@lesson 2.3 | Would it have happened anyway? | 🔬 | townhall
hook: Reading scores rose 10% at a school with a new programme. They rose 8% at nearby schools without it. What did the programme really do?
learn: Some change might have happened anyway. Good measurement asks what would have happened without you.
learn: Comparing with a similar group that didn't get the programme shows the real difference.
learn: Here, the programme probably added about 2%, not 10%.
try: Up 10% with it, up 8% without: about 2% from the programme :: gap | Up 15% with it, up 5% without: about _% from the programme | 10
play: path | Your group gave out free bike helmets, and head injuries in town fell. How do you check it was the helmets? | Compare with a similar town that didn't get helmets => good: If injuries fell there too, something else is going on. That's a fair test. | Assume the helmets did it => bad: Maybe a new bike lane or a rainy year did it. You can't be sure. | Only count the kids who liked their helmets => bad: Liking a helmet tells you nothing about injuries.
check: mcq | Why use a comparison group? | To see what would have happened anyway* | To make the numbers bigger | To save paper
check: tf | If things improved after your project, your project must have caused it. | false | Something else might explain it.
check: gap | Up 12% with the project, up 9% without. The project probably added about _% | 3
home: Think of something that improved recently. What else might have caused it?

@lesson 3.1 | Cost per outcome | 🧮 | bank
hook: Charity A tutors a child for a year for $500. Charity B does it for $250 with similar results. Does it matter?
learn: Cost per outcome = total cost ÷ number of outcomes.
learn: If two charities get similar results, the one with lower cost per outcome helps more people per dollar.
learn: Check the outcomes are really similar, not just the activities.
try: $10,000 helping 20 kids = $500 per child :: gap | $10,000 helping 40 kids = $_ per child | 250
play: price | Which charity helps a child read for less? | Charity A:10000:20:children; Charity B:10000:40:children
check: gap | $6,000 for 30 outcomes = $_ per outcome | 200
check: mcq | What does a lower cost per outcome mean? | More good per dollar, if the outcomes are similar* | The charity is lazy | It always means lower quality
check: tf | You should check the outcomes are really comparable. | true
home: Find one charity's cost per person helped in its annual report, if it shares one.
