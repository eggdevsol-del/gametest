@lesson 1.1 | What's a social enterprise? | 🌱 | garden
hook: Can a business exist mainly to fix a problem?
learn: A social enterprise trades like a business but puts its mission first.
learn: Profits are reinvested in the mission, not mainly paid to owners.
play: sort | Social enterprise, Charity, Regular business | Cafe employing young people leaving care, profits fund training=Social enterprise; Gets donations to run a free helpline=Charity; Sells phone cases for profit=Regular business; Sells soap and funds clean water projects=Social enterprise; Animal shelter funded by donations=Charity | Which is it?
check: mcq | How is a social enterprise different from a regular business? | Its mission comes first* | It never sells anything | It can't make money
check: tf | Social enterprises sell products or services. | true
check: mcq | Where do social enterprise profits mostly go? | Back into the mission* | Only to owners | Nowhere
home: Find a social enterprise and describe its mission in one line.
skill: impact

@lesson 1.2 | Measuring impact | 📏 | townhall
hook: A charity says it "raised awareness". How do you know it helped?
learn: Impact is the real change that happens, not just activity.
learn: Good organisations measure outcomes, like more kids reading, not just outputs, like books handed out.
play: sort | Output, Outcome | 1,000 books handed out=Output; Kids' reading levels went up=Outcome; 50 workshops held=Output; Fewer people sleeping rough=Outcome; 500 posters printed=Output; More families with steady food=Outcome | Is it an output or an outcome?
check: mcq | Which is an outcome? | More students passing exams* | Number of flyers printed | Hours spent in meetings
check: tf | Counting activities is the same as measuring impact. | false
check: mcq | Why measure outcomes? | To check real change happened* | To look busy | It doesn't matter
home: Pick a charity and find one outcome it reports.
skill: impact

@lesson 1.3 | Effective giving | 🎯 | bank
hook: Two charities. One helps 10 people with $1,000. One helps 100. Does it matter which?
learn: Effective giving means looking at how much good each dollar does.
learn: It's not the only thing that matters. Causes close to your heart count too.
play: debate | Should you always give to the charity that helps the most people per dollar? | It's a strong guide, balanced with causes you care about* | Only give to causes near you, no matter what | Giving locally builds your own community and lets you see the impact, which matters too.
check: mcq | What does effective giving look at? | How much good each dollar does* | Which logo is nicest | Which charity has the most ads
check: tf | There can be more than one good reason to choose a charity. | true
check: mcq | Charity A helps 10 people with $1,000. Charity B helps 50. Per dollar, B helps… | 5 times as many people* | The same | Fewer
home: Compare two charities' annual reports with a grown-up.
skill: giving

@lesson 1.4 | Speaking up | 📣 | stage
hook: You think your school should have a recycling system. How do you make it happen?
learn: Advocacy is speaking up to change rules or decisions.
learn: Facts, a clear ask and respectful persistence work better than shouting.
play: chat | Asking your principal for recycling bins | them: Hi! You wanted to talk about recycling? | me: Yes. Our class counted 200 plastic bottles in the bins last week. Could we trial recycling bins for a term?*/Recycling is obviously right and you're wrong/Whatever, forget it | them: That's useful data. Who would empty them? | me: Our enviro group would roster it, and we'd report back each month*/Not my problem/The cleaners, obviously
check: mcq | What makes advocacy work? | Facts and a clear ask* | Yelling | Giving up fast
check: tf | Being respectful helps persuade decision makers. | true
check: mcq | What's a good first step? | Gather some facts* | Start a fight | Complain online anonymously
home: Pick a small change at school and write a two-sentence pitch with a fact.
skill: advocacy

@lesson 1.5 | Patagonia | 🏔️ | garden
hook: A company owner gave his whole company away to fight climate change. Why?
learn: Yvon Chouinard founded the outdoor clothing company Patagonia.
learn: For years it pushed repairing clothes instead of buying new.
learn: In 2022 his family transferred ownership so that profits not reinvested in the business go to fighting climate change.
play: mystery | He was a rock climber who started making gear; His company makes outdoor clothing; It encourages people to repair clothes; In 2022 his family gave the company's future profits to climate causes | Yvon Chouinard* | Henry Ford | Rod Drury
check: mcq | What did Patagonia encourage customers to do? | Repair clothes instead of buying new* | Buy more every month | Throw clothes away
check: tf | A business can choose to put the planet before profits. | true
check: mcq | What did the 2022 change do? | Sent future profits to climate causes* | Closed the company | Made clothes free
home: Fix or reuse one thing instead of replacing it.
skill: sustainability

@lesson 1.6 | Who made my clothes? | 👕 | stall
hook: A t-shirt costs $5. Who got paid what to make it?
learn: A supply chain is every step from raw material to the shop.
learn: Very cheap prices can mean very low pay for workers somewhere in the chain.
learn: Fair trade labels show producers met certain standards for pay and conditions.
play: order | Put the t-shirt's supply chain in order | Cotton is grown on a farm; Cotton is spun into thread; Fabric is woven and dyed; Workers sew the t-shirt; It's shipped to a shop; You buy it
check: mcq | What is a supply chain? | Every step from materials to the shop* | A chain for bikes | A shop's checkout
check: tf | Very cheap clothes can mean very low pay for workers. | true
check: mcq | What do fair trade labels show? | Producers met standards for pay and conditions* | The clothes are free | The clothes are stolen
home: Check the label of a piece of clothing and find where it was made.
skill: fairtrade
