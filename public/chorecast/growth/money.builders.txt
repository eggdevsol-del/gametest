@lesson 1.1 | What is a budget? | 📋 | bank
hook: Two kids get the same pocket money. One always runs out. One never does. What's the difference?
learn: A budget is a plan for your money before you spend it.
learn: It splits money into needs, wants and savings.
learn: A plan beats hoping there is some left at the end of the week.
play: sort | Need, Want, Save | Lunch at school=Need; New game skin=Want; Bus fare=Need; Lollies=Want; Money for a bike=Save; Rain jacket=Need; Cinema ticket=Want; Birthday present fund=Save | Sort each spend
check: mcq | What is a budget? | A plan for money before you spend it* | A list of things you bought | A type of bank account
check: tf | A budget only matters if you have lots of money. | false | Budgets help most when money is tight.
check: mcq | Which is usually a need? | Food* | A new phone case | Concert tickets
home: Before your next Chorecast payday, write a three-line plan: needs, wants and save.
skill: budget

@lesson 1.2 | Pay yourself first | 🥇 | bank
hook: Most people try to save what's left over. Usually nothing is left. Is there a better way?
learn: Paying yourself first means saving the moment money arrives.
learn: Then you spend what's left, without touching the savings.
learn: Your Chorecast budget split does this for you every payday.
play: order | Put payday in the best order | Money arrives; Move your savings first; Pay for needs; Spend some on wants; Check what's left at the end of the week
check: mcq | When should savings come out? | As soon as the money arrives* | At the end of the week | Only when you feel like it
check: tf | Paying yourself first means buying yourself a treat first. | false | It means saving first.
check: mcq | Why does saving first work? | You can't spend what you've already put away* | Banks charge less | Saving is more fun than spending
home: Watch your next Chorecast payday. See how your split puts savings away before you spend.
skill: saving

@lesson 2.3 | Falling behind | 🐢 | bank
hook: You planned to have $60 by now, but you only have $40. Is the goal ruined?
learn: Falling behind is normal. What matters is what you do next.
learn: You can save a bit more each week, earn extra or move the deadline.
learn: Don't give up on the whole goal because of one bad week.
play: path | You're $20 behind on your saving goal. | Do two extra jobs and move the date a week later => good: Small changes get you back on track. | Give up on the goal => bad: You'd lose all the progress you made. | Spend the rest because it's "ruined" => bad: It's not ruined, it's just behind.
check: mcq | You're behind on a goal. What helps? | Saving a bit more or moving the deadline* | Giving up | Spending everything
check: tf | Falling behind is normal. | true
home: Check your Saving goal in Chorecast. Are you on track, ahead or behind?
skill: persistence

@lesson 3.1 | What is a unit price? | ⚖️ | stall
hook: A small bag of chips is $2. A big bag is $5. Which is the better deal?
learn: A unit price is what something costs per 100 g, per litre or per item.
learn: It lets you compare packs of different sizes fairly.
learn: Many supermarkets show the unit price in small print on the shelf label.
try: $3 for 300 g is $1 per 100 g :: gap | $4 for 200 g is $_ per 100 g | 2
play: price | Which bag of chips is better value? | Small bag:2:150:g; Big bag:5:450:g
check: mcq | What does a unit price help you do? | Compare different-sized packs fairly* | Find the prettiest pack | Pay faster at the till
check: gap | $6 for 3 litres is $_ per litre | 2
check: tf | The bigger pack is always the better deal. | false | Often it is, but not always. Check the unit price.
home: Find the unit price on three shelf labels at the supermarket.
skill: unitprice

@lesson 3.4 | Cost per use | 👟 | stall
hook: Cheap shoes cost $30 and last 3 months. Good shoes cost $90 and last a year. Which costs less?
learn: Cost per use is the price divided by how many times you use it.
learn: Something dearer can be cheaper in the long run if it lasts much longer.
learn: But only if you really use it.
try: $30 shoes for 3 months is $10 a month :: gap | $90 shoes for 12 months is $_ a month | 7.5
play: price | Which shoes cost less per month? | Cheap shoes:30:3:months; Good shoes:90:12:months
check: mcq | A $20 game you play 100 times, or a $10 game you play twice. Which costs less per play? | The $20 game* | The $10 game | The same
check: gap | A $40 jacket worn 20 times costs $_ per wear | 2
check: tf | The lowest price always means the best value. | false | How long it lasts and how much you use it matter too.
home: Before your next Spend jar buy, work out its cost per use.
skill: value

learn: Ads use tricks to make you want things fast.

learn: Famous faces, catchy songs and free gifts make products seem better than they are.

skill: advertising

learn: Games use timers, gems instead of dollars, and "limited" items to make spending feel small.
learn: Work out the real money cost before you buy, and agree on a limit with your grown-up.

@lesson 4.4 | Your ad shield | 🛡️ | home
hook: You see an ad and suddenly you really want the thing. What's your plan?
learn: Name it: "This is an ad. They want me to buy."
learn: Ask: Do I need it? What does it really cost? Do other reviews agree?
learn: Wait a day. If you still want it, add it to your plan.
play: order | Use your ad shield | Notice it's an ad; Ask who paid for it and why; Check what it really costs; Wait a day; Decide if it fits your plan
check: mcq | You really want something after an ad. What's a smart first step? | Notice it's an ad and wait a day* | Buy it now | Buy two
check: tf | Waiting a day helps you see if you really want it. | true
home: Next time an ad makes you want something, wait a day. If you still want it, make it your Saving goal.
skill: advertising
