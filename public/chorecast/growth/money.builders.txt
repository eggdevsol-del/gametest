@lesson 1.1 | What is a budget? | 📋 | bank
hook: Two kids get the same pocket money. One always runs out. One never does. What's the difference?
learn: A budget is a plan for your money before you spend it.
learn: It splits money into needs, wants and savings.
learn: A plan beats hoping there is some left at the end of the week.
play: sort | Need, Want, Save | Lunch at school=Need; New game skin=Want; Bus fare=Need; Lollies=Want; Money for a bike=Save; Rain jacket=Need; Cinema ticket=Want; Birthday present fund=Save | Sort each spend
check: mcq | What is a budget? | A plan for money before you spend it* | A list of things you bought | A type of bank account
check: tf | A budget only matters if you have lots of money. | false | Budgets help most when money is tight.
check: mcq | Which is usually a need? | Food* | A new phone case | Concert tickets
home: Write a three-line budget for your next pocket money: needs, wants, save.
skill: budget

@lesson 1.2 | Pay yourself first | 🥇 | bank

learn: Then you spend what's left, without touching the savings.

@lesson 1.3 | Split it three ways | 🍕 | bank
hook: If you got $20, how much should you save?
learn: One simple rule: half for needs, almost a third for wants, and a fifth to save.
learn: That's 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings.
learn: It's a starting point, not a law. Change it to fit your goals.
try: $10 × 20% = $2 :: gap | 20% of $20 = $_ | 4
play: gen | split | 4
check: gap | 50% of $40 = $_ | 20
check: mcq | In the 50/30/20 idea, what is the 20% for? | Savings* | Wants | Needs
check: slider | You earn $30. About how much is 30%? | 0 | 30 | 9 | $ | 1 | 1
home: Try the 50/30/20 split on your next pocket money.
skill: percent

@lesson 2.1 | What is a unit price? | ⚖️ | stall
hook: A small bag of chips is $2. A big bag is $5. Which is the better deal?
learn: A unit price is what something costs per 100 g, per litre or per item.
learn: It lets you compare packs of different sizes fairly.
learn: Big supermarkets in New Zealand and Australia must show unit prices on their shelf labels.
try: $3 for 300 g is $1 per 100 g :: gap | $4 for 200 g is $_ per 100 g | 2
play: price | Which bag of chips is better value? | Small bag:2:150:g; Big bag:5:450:g
check: mcq | What does a unit price help you do? | Compare different-sized packs fairly* | Find the prettiest pack | Pay faster at the till
check: gap | $6 for 3 litres is $_ per litre | 2
check: tf | The bigger pack is always the better deal. | false | Often it is, but not always. Check the unit price.
home: Find the unit price on three shelf labels at the supermarket.
skill: unitprice

@lesson 2.4 | Cost per use | 👟 | stall
hook: Cheap shoes cost $30 and last 3 months. Good shoes cost $90 and last a year. Which costs less?
learn: Cost per use is the price divided by how many times you use it.
learn: Something dearer can be cheaper in the long run if it lasts much longer.
learn: But only if you really use it.
try: $30 shoes for 3 months is $10 a month :: gap | $90 shoes for 12 months is $_ a month | 7.5
play: price | Which shoes cost less per month? | Cheap shoes:30:3:months; Good shoes:90:12:months
check: mcq | A $20 game you play 100 times, or a $10 game you play twice. Which costs less per play? | The $20 game* | The $10 game | The same
check: gap | A $40 jacket worn 20 times costs $_ per wear | 2
check: tf | The lowest price always means the best value. | false | How long it lasts and how much you use it matter too.
home: Pick something you use a lot and work out its cost per use.
skill: value
