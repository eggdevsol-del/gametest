@lesson 1.1 | Food chains | 🌿 | garden
hook: A hawk ate a bird, which ate a caterpillar, which ate a leaf. Where did the energy start?
learn: A food chain shows who eats what: leaf → caterpillar → bird → hawk.
learn: Plants are producers. They make their own food using sunlight.
learn: Animals are consumers. They get energy by eating plants or other animals.
try: grass → rabbit → fox :: mcq | In "grass → rabbit → fox", which is the producer? | Grass* | Rabbit | Fox
play: order | Build the food chain, starting with the producer | Grass; Grasshopper; Frog; Heron
check: mcq | Where does the energy in a food chain start? | The sun* | The biggest animal | The soil
check: tf | Animals can make their own food from sunlight. | false | Plants can. Animals have to eat.
check: mcq | An animal that eats only plants is a… | herbivore* | carnivore | producer
home: Write a food chain for something you ate today, starting with the sun.
skill: food-chains

@lesson 1.2 | Food webs | 🕸️ | garden
hook: What would happen to a forest if all its insects disappeared?
learn: Most animals eat more than one thing, so food chains join up into a food web.
learn: If one living thing disappears, others in the web are affected too.
learn: Carnivores eat animals. Herbivores eat plants. Omnivores eat both.
try: A bird that eats seeds and insects is an omnivore :: mcq | A cat that eats mice is a… | carnivore* | herbivore | producer
play: sort | Herbivore, Carnivore, Omnivore | Sheep=Herbivore; Shark=Carnivore; Human=Omnivore; Rabbit=Herbivore; Hawk=Carnivore; Pig=Omnivore | Which is it?
check: mcq | If insects disappeared, birds that eat them would… | have less food* | have more food | not notice
check: tf | A food web is made of many food chains joined together. | true
check: mcq | Which animal is an omnivore? | A bear* | A cow | A lion
home: Draw a food web for the animals and plants on your island.
skill: food-webs

@lesson 1.3 | Habitats | 🏞️ | island
hook: Why doesn't a fish live in a tree?
learn: A habitat is the place where a living thing lives. It gives it food, water, shelter and space.
learn: Forests, wetlands, rocky shores, deserts and oceans are all habitats.
learn: When habitats are damaged, the animals that live there struggle to survive.
try: A rock pool is a habitat for crabs :: mcq | What does a habitat give an animal? | Food, water and shelter* | Toys | Money
play: match | Match each animal to its habitat | Fish=Ocean; Duck=Wetland; Camel=Desert; Monkey=Rainforest
check: mcq | Which is a habitat? | A rock pool* | A bicycle | A sandwich
check: tf | Damaging a habitat can harm the animals that live there. | true
check: mcq | Why do animals need shelter? | To stay safe and out of bad weather* | To watch TV | To grow leaves
home: Explore a small habitat with a grown-up, like under a log or in a rock pool. Who lives there?
skill: habitats

@lesson 1.4 | Adaptations | 🐧 | island
hook: Why do penguins have flippers instead of wings like a sparrow's?
learn: Adaptations are features that help a living thing survive where it lives.
learn: A penguin's flippers help it swim fast to catch fish, and its thick layer of feathers keeps it warm.
learn: A kiwi has nostrils at the tip of its long beak, which help it sniff out worms in the soil.
try: A duck's webbed feet help it swim :: mcq | Why does a kiwi have nostrils at the tip of its beak? | To smell food in the soil* | To breathe underwater | To sing

check: mcq | What is an adaptation? | A feature that helps a living thing survive* | A type of food | A kind of weather
check: tf | A polar bear's thick fur is an adaptation for the cold. | true
check: mcq | Webbed feet are most useful for… | swimming* | climbing trees | digging
home: Pick an animal and find three adaptations that help it live where it does.
skill: adaptations

@lesson 2.1 | Pushes and pulls | 🛒 | stall
hook: Opening a door, kicking a ball, pulling a wagon. What do they have in common?
learn: A force is a push or a pull.
learn: Forces can make things start moving, stop, speed up, slow down or change direction.
learn: Forces can also change an object's shape, like squashing playdough.

@lesson 2.2 | Gravity | 🍎 | garden
hook: Why does a dropped apple fall down and not up?
learn: Gravity is a pulling force. The Earth pulls everything towards its centre.
learn: Weight is how hard gravity pulls on something.
learn: Gravity also keeps the Moon travelling around the Earth.
try: A ball thrown up comes back down because of gravity :: mcq | Gravity is a… | pull* | push | type of light

home: Drop a flat sheet of paper and a scrunched paper ball from the same height. Which lands first, and why?
skill: gravity

@lesson 2.3 | Friction | 🛝 | island
hook: Why is it hard to slide on carpet but easy on ice?
learn: Friction is a force that slows things down when two surfaces rub together.
learn: Rough surfaces make more friction. Smooth or slippery surfaces make less.
learn: Friction is useful. It lets our shoes grip, and it lets bike brakes stop the wheels.
try: Ice has very little friction :: mcq | Which surface has the most friction? | Sandpaper* | Ice | Glass
play: sort | More friction, Less friction | Gravel path=More friction; Wet slide=Less friction; Rubber soles=More friction; Ice rink=Less friction; Carpet=More friction | More friction or less?
check: mcq | Why do shoes have bumpy soles? | To grip with more friction* | To look nice | To go faster on ice
check: tf | Friction can turn movement into heat. | true
check: mcq | Air resistance is a kind of friction. Which falls slowest? | An open parachute* | A stone | A marble
home: Rub your hands together fast. Feel the heat? That's friction.
skill: friction

@lesson 2.4 | Magnets | 🧲 | workshop
hook: Why does a magnet stick to the fridge but not to a wooden table?
learn: Magnets pull on some metals, like iron and steel. They don't pull on wood, plastic or paper.
learn: A magnet has a north pole and a south pole.
learn: Opposite poles pull together (attract). The same poles push apart (repel).
try: A north pole and a south pole attract :: mcq | What happens when two north poles meet? | They push apart* | They pull together | They glow
play: sort | Magnetic, Not magnetic | Steel paperclip=Magnetic; Wooden spoon=Not magnetic; Iron nail=Magnetic; Plastic cup=Not magnetic; Glass marble=Not magnetic; Rubber band=Not magnetic | Will a magnet pull it?
check: mcq | Which will a magnet pick up? | An iron nail* | A paper cup | A wooden block
check: tf | A north pole and a south pole attract each other. | true
check: mcq | Which does a magnet NOT pull? | Plastic* | Iron | Steel
home: Test what a fridge magnet sticks to in your kitchen. Keep it away from phones and screens.
skill: magnets

learn: The Moon orbits the Earth about once a month. The Earth spins once a day.

@lesson 4.1 | Circuits | 🔋 | workshop
hook: Why does a torch only light up when you switch it on?
learn: Electricity flows around a complete loop called a circuit.
learn: A simple circuit has a battery (the energy source), wires, and something to power, like a bulb.
learn: If there's a gap anywhere in the loop, the electricity stops and the bulb goes out.
try: Battery, wire, bulb, wire, and back to the battery :: mcq | Why won't a bulb light if one wire is loose? | The circuit isn't complete* | The bulb is tired | The battery is cold
play: order | Build a circuit | Take a battery; Connect a wire to one end; Connect that wire to the bulb; Connect a second wire from the bulb back to the battery
check: tf | Electricity needs a complete loop to flow. | true
check: mcq | What gives a torch its energy? | The battery* | The bulb | The case
check: mcq | A gap in a circuit makes the bulb… | go out* | brighter | change colour
home: Look inside a torch with a grown-up. Can you trace the circuit?
skill: circuits

@lesson 4.2 | Conductors and insulators | 🔌 | workshop
hook: Why are wires made of copper covered in plastic?
learn: Conductors let electricity flow through them easily. Most metals, like copper, are good conductors.
learn: Insulators stop electricity flowing. Plastic, rubber, wood and glass are insulators.
learn: Wires have metal inside to carry the electricity and plastic outside to keep us safe.
try: Copper is a conductor :: mcq | Which is an insulator? | Rubber* | Copper | Steel
play: sort | Conductor, Insulator | Copper=Conductor; Plastic=Insulator; Aluminium foil=Conductor; Wood=Insulator; Steel spoon=Conductor; Rubber=Insulator | Conductor or insulator?
check: mcq | Why do wires have a plastic coating? | Plastic is an insulator, so it keeps us safe* | It looks nice | It makes electricity faster
check: tf | Most metals are good conductors. | true
check: mcq | Which would let electricity through? | A metal paperclip* | A plastic ruler | A rubber band
home: Find things at home made of conductors and insulators. Look only: never test plugs or sockets.
skill: conductors

@lesson 4.3 | Switches | 🎚️ | workshop
hook: How does a light switch turn a light on and off?
learn: A switch closes or opens a gap in a circuit.
learn: Switch on, and the gap closes, so electricity flows. Switch off, and the gap opens, so it stops.
learn: Switches let us control things without unplugging them.
try: Turning a switch on closes the gap :: mcq | When a switch is off, the circuit has… | a gap* | no gaps | extra power

check: tf | A switch works by opening and closing a gap in a circuit. | true
check: mcq | Which uses a switch? | A torch* | A spoon | A pillow
check: mcq | When you switch a light on, the circuit… | becomes complete* | gets a gap | melts
home: Count the switches in one room of your home.
skill: switches

@lesson 5.2 | Earthquakes | 🫨 | townhall
hook: Why does the ground sometimes shake?
learn: The Earth's crust is broken into huge pieces called plates, which move very slowly.
learn: When plates push or slide past each other, rocks can suddenly break and shake the ground. That's an earthquake.
learn: New Zealand sits where two plates meet, so it has lots of earthquakes.
try: Moving plates cause earthquakes :: mcq | What causes most earthquakes? | Plates moving in the crust* | Thunder | Heavy rain

check: mcq | The Earth's crust is broken into pieces called… | plates* | slices | tiles
home: Practise drop, cover and hold with your family, and plan where you'd meet afterwards.
skill: earthquakes

learn: When lava cools, it hardens into new rock. Many islands were made this way.
