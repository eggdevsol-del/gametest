@lesson 1.1 | Burnout | 🔥 | workshop
hook: School, a job, sport and friends. When is it too much?
learn: Burnout is exhaustion from long-term stress. Signs include tiredness, cynicism and dropping performance.
learn: Boundaries help: saying no, protecting sleep and taking real breaks.
play: sort | Burnout sign, Healthy sign | Always tired even after sleep=Burnout sign; Enjoying time off=Healthy sign; Not caring about things you used to love=Burnout sign; Saying no to extra shifts sometimes=Healthy sign; Getting sick often=Burnout sign | Which is which?
check: mcq | What is burnout? | Exhaustion from long-term stress* | A cooking term | A short bad day
check: tf | Setting boundaries can prevent burnout. | true
check: mcq | A healthy boundary at work? | Saying no to some extra shifts* | Answering messages at 2am | Never taking breaks
home: List your commitments and spot one to cut back.
skill: wellbeing

@lesson 1.3 | Big decisions | 🧭 | townhall
hook: Gap year, uni or job? How do you choose without regret?
learn: List options, think about pros and cons, and imagine yourself in a year.
learn: Talk to people who've done each path. Reversible decisions can be made faster.
play: debate | Should big decisions be made quickly or slowly? | Slowly for hard-to-reverse ones, faster for reversible ones* | Always instantly, go with your gut | Gut feelings carry real information, especially when you've thought about a topic before. They work best alongside facts.
check: mcq | What helps with big decisions? | Pros and cons and advice from people who've done it* | A coin flip only | Asking a random stranger
check: tf | Reversible decisions can be made faster. | true
check: mcq | A useful question? | How will I feel about this in a year?* | What will be fun tonight? | What do my enemies want?
home: Use a pros and cons list on a decision this week.
skill: decisions

@lesson 1.4 | Respect and red flags | 🚩 | garden
hook: Your partner checks your phone and gets angry when you see friends. Is that love?
learn: Healthy relationships have respect, trust and freedom. Consent is ongoing and can be withdrawn.
learn: Red flags include control, jealousy, put-downs and isolating you from friends.
play: sort | Healthy, Red flag | Respecting your time with friends=Healthy; Demanding your passwords=Red flag; Listening when you say no=Healthy; Constant put-downs=Red flag; Supporting your goals=Healthy; Getting angry when you see family=Red flag | Healthy or red flag?
check: mcq | Which is a red flag? | Controlling who you see* | Supporting your goals | Respecting your no
check: tf | Consent can be withdrawn at any time. | true
check: mcq | {nz:Where can you get help about unhealthy relationships in NZ?~au:Where can you get help about unhealthy relationships in Australia?} | {nz:Youthline or 1737~au:1800RESPECT}* | Nowhere | An ad
home: Talk with a trusted adult about what respect looks like.
skill: relationships

@lesson 1.5 | Gambling | 🎲 | stage
hook: Sports betting ads are everywhere. Why does the house always win?
learn: Betting odds are set so the operator profits over time. Most people lose money over time.
learn: Loot boxes in games use similar tricks. {nz:Gambling Helpline: 0800 654 655.~au:Gambling Help Online: 1800 858 858.}
play: slider | You bet $10 a week on games with a 5% house edge. Roughly how much would you expect to lose over a year? | 0 | 100 | 26 | $ | 8 | 1
check: mcq | Who wins over time in gambling? | The operator* | Most gamblers | Everyone
check: tf | Loot boxes can work like gambling. | true
check: mcq | Signs gambling is a problem? | Chasing losses and hiding it* | Playing a board game | Saving money
home: Count the gambling ads you see in one day.
skill: gambling
