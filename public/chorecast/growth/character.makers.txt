@lesson 1.1 | Honest when it's hard | 🧭 | home
hook: You find $20 on the floor at school. No one saw. What now?
learn: Honesty means telling the truth and not taking what isn't yours, even when no one would know.
learn: Small lies make the next lie easier, and trust is slow to rebuild.
learn: Being honest when it costs you is how people learn they can rely on you.
play: path | You find $20 near the school office. No one saw. | Hand it in at the office => good: It may be someone's lunch money, and now people know you can be trusted. | Keep it, finders keepers => bad: It isn't yours, and you'd know. | Ask around for who lost it => ok: Kind, but the office can find the owner more fairly.
check: mcq | What builds trust fastest? | Being honest when it costs you* | Telling people what they want to hear | Never admitting a mistake
check: tf | Small lies don't matter if no one finds out. | false | They make the next lie easier and wear down trust.
home: Talk with your family about a time when being honest was hard but worth it.
skill: honesty

@lesson 1.2 | Keeping your word | 🤝 | home
hook: You promised to help your gran on Saturday. Then you get a better invite. What do you do?
learn: Keeping your word means doing what you said, even when it's no longer convenient.
learn: Before you promise, check you can really do it.
learn: If you truly can't, tell the person early, say sorry and offer another time.
play: path | You promised to help your gran in the garden on Saturday. Then friends invite you to the beach. | Help your gran as promised, and see your friends another day => good: People learn your word means something. | Text her that you're sick => bad: Breaking a promise with a lie does double damage. | Ask her early whether another day works => ok: Better than cancelling late, if she really doesn't mind.
check: mcq | Before you make a promise, you should… | Check you can really keep it* | Say yes to everything | Promise now and decide later
check: tf | If you can't keep a promise, it's better to tell the person early. | true
home: This week, keep track of every promise you make, and whether you kept it.
skill: honesty

@lesson 1.3 | Owning your mistakes | 🙋 | home

learn: Owning a mistake means saying what you did, without blaming or making excuses.
learn: A good apology has three parts: what I did, I'm sorry, and how I'll make it right.
learn: People respect someone who owns up far more than someone who pretends they never slip up.
play: order | Put a good apology in order | Say what you did; Say sorry and mean it; Offer to make it right; Do better next time

home: Next time you make a mistake at home, try the three-part apology.

@lesson 3.1 | Group chats aren't private | 💬 | online
hook: The group chat is roasting a teacher. It's just your friends. What's the harm?
learn: Anything in a group chat can be screenshotted and shared in seconds.
learn: "Private" chats have ended up in front of schools, parents and the person being talked about.
learn: A good rule: only type what you'd be OK with the person seeing.

check: mcq | Can a group chat message be shared? | Yes, in seconds* | No, never | Only after a year
check: tf | Private chats can end up being seen by others. | true
check: mcq | A good rule for group chats is… | Only type what you'd be OK with the person seeing* | Type whatever you want | Roast everyone

@lesson 4.1 | Your brain is still building | 🧠 | online
hook: Why is it so hard to stop scrolling, even when you want to?
learn: The part of your brain that plans and puts on the brakes keeps developing into your twenties.
learn: The part that wants rewards right now is already strong in the teen years.
learn: That's why quick rewards from games and feeds can beat your long-term plans.
learn: The habits you practise now help shape the brain you'll have as an adult.
play: match | Match each brain idea to what it means | Brakes=Planning and self-control, still developing; Reward=Wanting good feelings right now; Habit=Something you do so often it's automatic; Sleep=When your brain sorts and stores the day
check: mcq | Which part of the brain is still developing in the teen years? | The part that plans and puts on the brakes* | The part that makes you hungry | None of it
check: tf | The habits you build as a teen can shape your adult brain. | true
home: Pick one screen habit you want to keep and one you want to change. Tell your parents.

@lesson 4.3 | The teen body clock | 🕰️ | home
hook: Why do teens feel wide awake at 11pm but like zombies at 7am?
learn: In the teen years, the body clock shifts later. Sleepiness comes later at night.
learn: But school still starts early, so many teens run short on sleep.
learn: A regular wake-up time, even on weekends, helps your body clock adjust.
play: tf | Teens' body clocks naturally shift later. | true | That's why teens often feel awake late at night.
check: mcq | What helps your body clock most? | Waking at the same time each day* | Sleeping until 2pm on weekends | Drinking energy drinks
check: tf | Big weekend sleep-ins can make Monday mornings harder. | true
home: Try waking at the same time every day for a week.
skill: sleep
