@lesson 1.1 | Saying thank you | 🙏 | home
hook: Nana gives you a jumper she knitted. It's not your favourite colour. What do you say?
learn: Saying thank you shows people you notice what they did for you.
learn: You can say thank you for the effort, even if the present isn't your favourite.
learn: Look at the person, smile, and say what you're thanking them for.
play: chat | Nana gives you a present | them: Here's something I made for you! | me: Thank you, Nana! Did you knit it yourself?*/I don't like this colour/Is that all? | them: I did! It took me three weeks. | me: Wow, that's so much work. Thank you for making it for me*/Three weeks? That's slow/Can I have money instead?
check: mcq | What's a kind thing to say about a present? | Thank you for making it for me* | I don't like it | Is that all?
check: tf | You can say thank you for someone's effort, even if a present isn't your favourite. | true
check: mcq | A good thank you includes… | What you're thanking them for* | Nothing at all | A frown
home: Say a thank you today that tells someone exactly what you're thankful for.
skill: gratitude

@lesson 2.1 | Try, try again | 🚲 | garden
hook: You try to ride a bike and fall off. Do you give up?
learn: New things are hard at first. Nearly everyone falls off a bike when they start!
learn: Each try teaches your body and brain a little more.
learn: Saying "I can't do it yet" helps you keep trying.
play: path | You've tried to tie your shoelaces five times. They keep coming undone. | Take a breath and try again slowly, or ask for help => good: On the next try, you get it! Practice paid off. | Throw your shoes across the room => bad: Now you're cross, and your shoes are still untied. | Say "I'll never be able to do it" => bad: You can, you just can't yet. Keep practising.
check: mcq | You fall off your bike when learning. You… | Try again* | Give up forever | Blame the bike
check: tf | "I can't do it yet" helps you keep trying. | true
check: mcq | Why do new things get easier? | Each try teaches you a little more* | Magic | They don't
home: Pick something you can't do yet and practise it this week.
skill: grit

@lesson 2.3 | Oops, fix it | 🧽 | home
hook: You knocked over your drink at dinner. What now?
learn: When something goes wrong, stop and take a breath.
learn: Say "Oops, I made a mistake," then fix what you can.
learn: If someone was upset, say sorry. Then think about what you'll do next time.
play: order | Make a fix-it plan. Put the steps in order. | Stop and take a breath; Say "Oops, I made a mistake"; Fix what you can; Say sorry if someone was upset; Think about what you'll do next time
check: mcq | You spill your drink. What's a good first step? | Stop and take a breath* | Run away | Pretend it didn't happen
check: tf | Thinking about next time helps you learn from a mistake. | true
check: mcq | What's part of a fix-it plan? | Fixing what you can* | Blaming someone else | Hiding
home: Use the fix-it plan the next time something goes oops.
skill: mistakes

@lesson 2.4 | Owning up | 🙋 | home
hook: You broke Mum's favourite mug. Nobody saw. Do you tell?
learn: Owning up means telling the truth about a mistake, even when it's hard.
learn: Hiding a mistake usually makes things worse, and you might feel bad inside.
learn: Grown-ups are usually proud of kids who tell the truth.
play: path | You accidentally break Mum's favourite mug. Nobody saw. | Tell Mum straight away and say sorry => good: Mum is sad about the mug, but proud that you told the truth. | Hide the pieces under the couch => bad: When Mum finds them, she's upset about the mug and the hiding. | Say the cat did it => bad: Blaming someone else isn't fair, and it's not the truth.
check: mcq | What does owning up mean? | Telling the truth about a mistake* | Hiding it | Blaming the cat
check: tf | Hiding a mistake usually makes things worse. | true
check: mcq | You broke something by accident. You… | Tell a grown-up and say sorry* | Hide it | Blame someone else
home: Talk with a grown-up about why telling the truth matters, even when it's hard.
skill: honesty

@lesson 3.1 | My body is private | 🩱 | home
hook: Some parts of your body are private. Do you know which ones?
learn: Your private parts are the parts your swimsuit covers.
learn: Nobody should touch them, look at them or take pictures of them.
learn: Nobody should ask you to touch or look at theirs.
learn: Mum or Dad helping you wash, or a doctor with them there, is different.
play: sort | OK, Not OK | Mum helps you wash your hair=OK; The doctor checks your ear while Dad watches=OK; Someone wants a photo of you in the bath=Not OK; Someone asks to see under your swimsuit=Not OK; A friend gives you a high five=OK | Is it OK?
check: mcq | Which parts of your body are private? | The parts your swimsuit covers* | Your hands | Your hair
check: tf | You should tell Mum or Dad if someone breaks a body rule. | true | Always tell, even if they said not to.
check: mcq | Someone wants a photo of you with no clothes on. It is… | Not OK, so tell Mum or Dad* | OK if they're nice | A fun game
home: Talk with Mum or Dad about your family's body rules.
skill: boundaries

@lesson 3.2 | Saying no | ✋ | home
hook: What can you do if something doesn't feel right?
learn: You can say "No!" in a loud, strong voice.
learn: Then get away and go to a grown-up you trust.
learn: Tell Mum or Dad what happened, even if it's hard to say.
learn: It's never your fault, and you won't be in trouble for telling.
play: order | Something feels wrong. What do you do? | Say "No!" loudly; Get away; Tell Mum or Dad; Keep telling until someone helps
check: mcq | Someone asks you to keep a body secret. You… | Tell Mum or Dad* | Keep it forever | Forget about it
check: tf | You might get in trouble for telling. | false | Telling is always the right thing to do.
check: mcq | Who should you tell? | Mum, Dad or another grown-up you trust* | Nobody | Only a friend
home: Practise a strong "No!" voice with Mum or Dad.
skill: boundaries

@lesson 3.3 | Surprises and secrets | 🎁 | home

learn: A surprise is fun and ends soon, like a present or a party. Everyone finds out.
learn: A bad secret makes you feel worried, scared or yucky, and someone wants you to keep it forever.
learn: You should always tell a trusted grown-up about a bad secret.

check: mcq | Someone online says "Don't tell your mum." You… | Tell a trusted grown-up* | Keep the secret | Send them a photo
check: tf | A surprise party is a fun surprise, not a bad secret. | true
check: mcq | A bad secret makes you feel… | Worried, scared or yucky* | Happy and excited | Hungry
home: Talk with a grown-up about the difference between surprises and secrets.
skill: telling
