@lesson 1.1 | Tulip mania | 🌷 | museum
hook: In the 1630s, some tulip bulbs sold for about the price of a house. Then the price crashed. Why?
learn: In the Netherlands, rare tulips became fashionable, and people bought them hoping to sell for more.
learn: Prices rose on hype, not real value. In February 1637, buyers vanished and prices collapsed.
learn: Historians argue about how wild it really was, but it's become the classic story of a bubble.
play: market | Ride a hype bubble and decide when to hold | 10
check: mcq | What is a bubble? | Prices rising on hype above real value* | A soap game | A type of bank
check: tf | Bubbles usually end with sharp price falls. | true
check: mcq | What drove tulip prices up? | People hoping to resell for more* | Tulips curing illness | Government rules
home: Think of something that's popular now. Is it real value or hype?
skill: bubbles

@lesson 1.4 | The Medici | 🏰 | museum
hook: A family in Florence became powerful through banking. How did banking work 600 years ago?
learn: The Medici bank had branches across Europe in the 1400s.
learn: Merchants used bills of exchange, so they didn't need to carry chests of gold.
learn: Careful record-keeping, like double-entry bookkeeping, helped banks grow.

@lesson 1.5 | The 2008 crisis | 🏚️ | museum
hook: In 2008, a housing collapse in the US shook banks worldwide. What went wrong?
learn: Lenders gave risky home loans to people who couldn't afford them.
learn: Those loans were bundled and sold around the world. When people couldn't pay, the losses spread.
learn: Governments rescued banks and tightened rules afterwards.
