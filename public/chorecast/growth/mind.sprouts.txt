@lesson 1.1 | What's a habit? | 🪥 | home
hook: You brush your teeth without even thinking about it. Why?
learn: A habit is something you do so often that it happens almost by itself.
learn: Good habits help you every day: brushing your teeth, packing your bag, saying thank you.
learn: Habits grow with practice. The more you do something, the easier it gets.
try: Brushing your teeth every morning is a habit :: mcq | Which is a good habit? | Putting your shoes away* | Leaving toys on the stairs | Skipping breakfast
play: sort | Good habit, Not helpful | Washing hands before eating=Good habit; Going to bed late=Not helpful; Packing your bag the night before=Good habit; Leaving wet towels on the floor=Not helpful; Drinking water=Good habit | Good habit, or not helpful?
check: mcq | How does a habit get easier? | By doing it again and again* | By doing it once | By only thinking about it
check: tf | A habit is something you do without much thinking. | true
check: mcq | Which habit helps at school? | Packing your bag the night before* | Losing your hat | Forgetting your lunch
home: Choose one good habit to practise every day this week, and tick it off each day.
skill: habits

@lesson 1.2 | Tiny steps | 👣 | home
hook: How do you learn to ride a bike? All at once, or a little at a time?
learn: Big things are easier when you break them into tiny steps.
learn: Want to read more? Start with one page a day.
learn: Small steps every day add up to big changes.
try: One page a day is a tiny step :: mcq | Ari wants to get better at skipping. What's a tiny step? | Practise for 2 minutes a day* | Skip 1,000 times today | Never try
play: order | Make your bed in tiny steps | Pull up the sheet; Straighten the duvet; Fluff the pillow; Put your soft toy on top
check: mcq | Why are tiny steps good? | They're easy to start, and they add up* | They're boring | They take years
check: tf | Doing a little every day can add up to a lot. | true
check: mcq | What's a tiny step towards a tidy room? | Put away 5 things* | Clean the whole house | Get a new room
home: Pick something you'd like to get better at, and do a tiny step of it each day.
skill: tiny-steps

@lesson 1.3 | Routines | 🌅 | home
hook: What do you do every morning, in the same order?
learn: A routine is a set of things you do in the same order, like a morning or bedtime routine.
learn: Routines help you remember things and feel calm, because you know what comes next.
learn: A picture chart can help you follow a routine.
try: Wake up, get dressed, eat breakfast, brush teeth :: mcq | What's a routine? | Things you do in the same order each time* | Something you do once | A kind of sandwich
play: order | Put a bedtime routine in order | Have a bath or shower; Put on pyjamas; Brush your teeth; Read a story; Lights out
check: mcq | How do routines help? | You know what comes next* | They make you late | They change every day
check: tf | A routine can help you feel calm. | true
check: mcq | Which belongs in a good morning routine? | Eating breakfast* | Watching TV until you're late | Going back to sleep
home: Make a picture chart of your morning routine with a grown-up.
skill: routines

@lesson 1.4 | Practice makes progress | 🎹 | stage
hook: Can anyone play a song perfectly the very first time?
learn: Getting good at something takes practice.
learn: Mistakes are part of learning. Every try teaches your brain something.
learn: A little practice often is better than a lot just once.
try: 10 minutes of piano every day helps more than 1 hour once a week :: mcq | What helps you get better at something? | Practising often* | Giving up | Waiting
play: match | Match each skill to a way to practise | Reading=A page every night; Kicking a ball=Kicking against a wall; Times tables=Quick quizzes; Drawing=Sketching every day
check: tf | Mistakes can help you learn. | true
check: mcq | Which is better? | A little practice often* | Lots of practice once | No practice
check: mcq | Mia keeps missing the ball. What should she do? | Keep practising* | Stop forever | Blame the ball
home: Practise something for 5 minutes every day this week. Notice how it gets easier.
skill: practice

@lesson 2.1 | Naming feelings | 😊 | home
hook: How many feelings can you name?
learn: Everyone has feelings: happy, sad, angry, scared, excited, worried.
learn: All feelings are OK to have. What matters is what we do with them.
learn: Naming a feeling ("I feel cross") helps you understand it and calm down.
try: When I lose a game, I might feel disappointed :: mcq | Your friend can't come to play. You might feel… | disappointed* | excited | hungry
play: match | Match each moment to a feeling | Getting a hug=Happy; Hearing thunder at night=Scared; Your tower gets knocked over=Cross; Waiting for your birthday=Excited
check: tf | All feelings are OK to have. | true
check: mcq | Why name your feelings? | It helps you understand them* | It makes them bigger | It's rude
check: mcq | Which one is a feeling? | Worried* | Table | Blue
home: At dinner, everyone names one feeling they had today, and why.
skill: naming-feelings

@lesson 2.2 | Calm-down tools | 🌬️ | garden
hook: When you're really cross, your body gets hot and your heart beats fast. What can help?
learn: Big feelings can make our bodies feel wobbly. Calm-down tools help.
learn: Slow breathing: breathe in while you count to 4, then out slowly while you count to 4. It's like smelling a flower, then blowing out a candle.
learn: Other tools: a drink of water, a walk, squeezing a soft toy, or a quiet spot.
try: Smell the flower, blow out the candle :: mcq | Which is a calm-down tool? | Slow breathing* | Shouting louder | Throwing toys
play: sort | Helps me calm down, Makes it worse | Slow breaths=Helps me calm down; Hitting=Makes it worse; A drink of water=Helps me calm down; Slamming doors=Makes it worse; A hug from a grown-up=Helps me calm down | Does it help, or make it worse?
check: mcq | Smelling a flower and blowing out a candle helps you… | breathe slowly* | run faster | eat
check: tf | Slow breaths can help your body calm down. | true
check: mcq | When you're very angry, a good first step is to… | stop and breathe* | throw something | yell at someone
home: Practise "smell the flower, blow out the candle" five times with a grown-up.
skill: calming

@lesson 2.3 | Worries | 💭 | home
hook: What do you do when a worry keeps going round and round in your head?
learn: Everyone worries sometimes. A worry is a thought about something that might go wrong.
learn: Talking about a worry with Mum, Dad or another trusted grown-up makes it smaller.
learn: You can ask: "What's one thing I can do about it?"
try: Telling Mum about a worry helps :: mcq | What can make a worry smaller? | Telling a trusted grown-up* | Keeping it secret forever | Thinking about it all night
play: sort | Helps with worries, Doesn't help | Talking to Dad=Helps with worries; Making a plan=Helps with worries; Hiding it=Doesn't help; Slow breathing=Helps with worries; Thinking about it over and over=Doesn't help | Does it help?
check: tf | Everyone worries sometimes. | true
check: mcq | Ari is worried about a spelling test. What could help? | Practising the words and telling Mum* | Never thinking about spelling | Staying home
check: mcq | Who can you share a worry with? | A trusted grown-up* | Nobody, ever | A stranger online
home: Tell a grown-up about one small worry, and think of one thing you could do about it together.
skill: worries

@lesson 2.4 | Asking for help | 🙋 | stage
hook: Does asking for help mean you're not clever?
learn: Asking for help is smart and brave. Even grown-ups ask for help.
learn: Have a go first, then ask: "Can you show me how to start?"
learn: Ask the right person: a teacher for schoolwork, Mum or Dad for big problems.
try: "Can you help me tie this knot?" :: mcq | You're stuck on a puzzle after trying. What could you do? | Ask for help* | Cry and give up | Throw it away
play: match | Match each problem to who could help | Stuck on homework=Your teacher; Hurt your knee=A grown-up nearby; Can't reach the top shelf=Someone taller; Lost at the shops=A shop worker
check: tf | Asking for help shows you're weak. | false | Asking for help is smart and brave.
check: mcq | What's a good way to ask? | "Could you please show me how?"* | "Do it for me now!" | Say nothing and sulk
check: mcq | Who should you tell about a big problem? | Mum, Dad or another trusted grown-up* | A stranger | Nobody
home: Ask someone in your family for help with something this week, and say thank you.
skill: asking-help
