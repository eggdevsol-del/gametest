hook: Two people apply for a job. One is brilliant but late. One is reliable and keen. Who gets it?
learn: Employers value reliability, attitude, teamwork and willingness to learn.
learn: These are called soft skills. They matter in every job.

check: tf | Soft skills only matter in some jobs. | false
check: mcq | What's a soft skill? | Teamwork* | Height | A big house

@lesson 1.3 | Being reliable | ⏰ | home
hook: You promised to walk the neighbour's dog at 4pm. It's 3:55 and your show is on. What now?
learn: Reliable people do what they say, when they say.
learn: If you can't, you tell people early and make a plan.
play: path | You promised to walk the neighbour's dog at 4pm, but a friend invites you to the park. | Walk the dog first, then go to the park => good: You kept your promise and still had fun. That's reliable. | Go to the park and say nothing => bad: The dog waited. The neighbour may not trust you again. | Message the neighbour early to swap the time => ok: Telling people early is much better than not turning up.
check: mcq | What does reliable mean? | Doing what you say you'll do* | Doing what you feel like | Being fast
check: tf | If you can't do something, telling people early is better than not showing up. | true
check: mcq | Why does reliability matter for paid jobs? | People keep hiring you* | It doesn't | It makes jobs shorter
home: Make one promise this week and keep it exactly.
skill: reliability

@lesson 1.5 | Taking feedback | 🗣️ | workshop
hook: Your boss says your work "could be better". Are you in trouble?
learn: Feedback helps you improve. It's about the work, not about you.
learn: Say thanks, ask how to fix it, then try again.
play: order | Put the steps for handling feedback in order | Listen without interrupting; Say thanks for the feedback; Ask how to do it better; Try again; Check if it's better now
check: mcq | Someone says your work needs fixing. Best response? | "Thanks. How can I make it better?"* | "You're wrong" | Walk away
check: tf | Feedback is about your work, not who you are. | true
check: mcq | What should you do after feedback? | Try again using it* | Forget it | Stop working
home: Ask a grown-up for one piece of feedback on a chore, and use it.
skill: feedback

@lesson 2.2 | Be on time | 🕐 | home
hook: Soccer starts at 9:00. It takes 20 minutes to get there and 10 minutes to get ready. When do you start?
learn: Work backwards from when you need to be there.
learn: Add a little spare time for surprises, like traffic or lost shoes.
learn: Being early feels calm. Being late is stressful for everyone.
try: Starts 9:00, 20 minutes travel, 10 minutes to get ready, so start at 8:30 :: gap | Starts 4:00, 15 minutes travel, 15 minutes to get ready. Start getting ready at _ | 3:30 | 3:30; 3:45; 4:15
play: gen | time | 4
check: gap | Your job starts at 10:00. It's a 25-minute walk. Leave by _ | 9:35 | 9:35; 9:25; 10:25
check: tf | Adding spare time helps with surprises. | true
check: mcq | You need to be there at 3:00 and it takes 30 minutes. A smart time to leave is… | 2:20, with some spare time* | 2:59 | 3:00
home: Work backwards to plan what time you need to leave for something tomorrow.
skill: punctuality

@lesson 2.3 | Keeping promises | 🤝 | stall
hook: You said you'd help your aunt at her market stall on Saturday morning. Then a friend invites you to a movie at the same time. What do you do?
learn: A promise is a promise, even when something more fun comes up.
learn: Only say yes when you know you can do it.
learn: If you truly can't, tell the person early and help find another way.
play: path | You promised to help at your aunt's market stall on Saturday morning. A friend invites you to a movie. | Help at the stall and ask your friend about an afternoon movie => good: You kept your word and still saw your friend. Your aunt will ask you again. | Tell your aunt a few days early and find someone to cover => ok: Not perfect, but honest. She can make a plan. | Go to the movie and don't tell her => bad: She's setting up alone. She won't trust you next time.
check: mcq | When should you say yes to a job? | When you know you can do it* | Always, to be nice | Never
check: tf | If you can't keep a promise, telling the person early is better than saying nothing. | true
check: mcq | What happens when you often break promises? | People stop trusting you* | People pay you more | Nothing
home: Make one small promise to someone this week and keep it.
skill: reliability

@lesson 2.4 | Checklists | ✅ | workshop
hook: Pilots use checklists before every flight. Why would someone who flies every day need a list?
learn: Checklists stop you forgetting steps, even when you're busy or tired.
learn: Pilots, nurses and chefs use them to get things right every time.
learn: Make a checklist for any job with lots of steps.
play: build | Build a checklist for washing the car | Get a bucket, sponge and car soap; Rinse the car with the hose; Wash from the top down; Rinse off the soap; Dry it with a towel; Put everything away
check: mcq | Why do pilots use checklists? | So they don't miss a step* | Because they can't remember anything | For fun
check: tf | Checklists are only for people who forget a lot. | false | Experts use them to get it right every time.
check: mcq | When is a checklist most useful? | For jobs with lots of steps* | For blinking | For sleeping
home: Make a checklist for getting ready for school and use it all week.
skill: planning

@lesson 2.5 | Running late | 📱 | online

learn: A quick message before you're late beats a big excuse after.

I'll be there by 4:15*/Yeah, maybe/I'll come whenever | them: OK, thanks for letting me know. | me: Thanks for waiting. I'll still stay the full hour*/Whatever/I'll leave early to make up for it
check: mcq | When should you tell someone you'll be late? | As soon as you know* | After you arrive | Never
check: tf | A clear message with a new arrival time is helpful. | true
check: mcq | Which message is best? | Sorry, running 10 minutes late. There by 3:40* | Late | Something happened, idk
home: Practise writing a polite "running late" message with a grown-up.

@lesson 2.6 | Your reputation | 🏆 | workshop

learn: It's built slowly, job by job, and it can be damaged quickly.
learn: Reliable workers get asked back and recommended to others.

check: mcq | What do happy customers often do? | Ask you back and tell others* | Forget you | Ask for a refund
home: Ask a grown-up who they'd recommend for a job, and why.
