@lesson 1.1 | Nouns and pronouns | 🏷️ | stage
hook: "Ari fed Ari's dog because Ari loves Ari's dog." What's wrong with this sentence?
learn: Nouns name people, places, things and ideas: Ari, Rotorua, dog, kindness.
learn: Proper nouns name a particular one and start with a capital letter: Ari, Rotorua, Saturday.
learn: Pronouns stand in for nouns so we don't repeat them: he, she, it, they, his, her.
try: "Ari fed his dog because he loves it." :: mcq | Which is a proper noun? | Wellington* | city | river
play: sort | Common noun, Proper noun, Pronoun | ocean=Common noun; Pacific=Proper noun; they=Pronoun; teacher=Common noun; Mrs Lee=Proper noun; she=Pronoun | What kind of word is it?
check: mcq | Which word is a pronoun? | them* | bike | Tuesday
check: mcq | Which noun names an idea? | courage* | chair | dog
check: tf | Proper nouns start with a capital letter. | true
home: Write a sentence about someone in your family, then write it again using a pronoun.
skill: nouns-pronouns

@lesson 1.2 | Verbs and tense | ⏳ | stage
hook: "Yesterday I walk to school." What's wrong?
learn: Verbs show actions or being: run, think, is.
learn: Tense shows when: I walk (present), I walked (past), I will walk (future).
learn: Some verbs change in the past: run becomes ran, eat becomes ate, go becomes went.
try: Yesterday I walked to school :: mcq | Which is past tense? | swam* | swim | will swim
play: match | Match each verb to its past tense | go=went; eat=ate; buy=bought; catch=caught
check: mcq | "Tomorrow we … to the beach." | will go* | went | goes
check: mcq | Which sentence is in the past tense? | We cleaned the car.* | We clean the car. | We will clean the car.
check: tf | "thinked" is the past tense of "think". | false | It's thought.
home: Tell a grown-up about last weekend, and listen for your past tense verbs.
skill: verb-tense

@lesson 1.3 | Adjectives | 🎨 | stage
hook: "A dog", or "a scruffy, muddy, happy dog". Which paints a better picture?
learn: Adjectives describe nouns: big, red, fluffy, brave.
learn: They can tell what kind, how many or which one: three hens, that bike.
learn: Good writers choose exact adjectives: "freezing" says more than "cold".
try: "The tiny kitten" uses the adjective tiny :: mcq | Which word is an adjective? | shiny* | shine | shined
play: sort | Adjective, Not an adjective | soft=Adjective; jump=Not an adjective; enormous=Adjective; table=Not an adjective; gloomy=Adjective; quickly=Not an adjective | Is it an adjective?
check: mcq | Which adjective tells you the most about how hot it was? | scorching* | warm | nice
check: mcq | Find the adjective: "The brave firefighter climbed the ladder." | brave* | climbed | ladder
check: tf | Adjectives describe verbs. | false | Adjectives describe nouns. Adverbs describe verbs.
home: Describe your favourite food to a grown-up using five adjectives.
skill: adjectives

@lesson 1.4 | Adverbs | 🐢 | stage
hook: She ran. She ran quickly. She ran slowly. How does one word change the picture?
learn: Adverbs tell us more about verbs: how, when or where something happens.
learn: Many end in -ly: quickly, quietly, carefully.
learn: Others tell when or where: yesterday, soon, outside, here.
try: In "He whispered softly", softly is the adverb :: mcq | Which word is an adverb? | carefully* | careful | care
play: match | Match each adverb to what it tells you | quietly=How; tomorrow=When; outside=Where
check: mcq | Find the adverb: "The hen clucked loudly." | loudly* | hen | clucked
check: mcq | Which adverb tells you when? | soon* | slowly | there
check: tf | All adverbs end in -ly. | false | Some don't, like soon, here and often.
home: Play a game: a grown-up says an adverb and you act something out in that way.
skill: adverbs

@lesson 2.1 | Commas in lists | 🧺 | stage
hook: "For the picnic we need bread cheese apples and juice." Where do the commas go?
learn: Commas separate the items in a list: bread, cheese, apples and juice.
learn: Most New Zealand writing leaves out the comma before the last "and".
learn: Commas help readers pause in the right places.
try: I fed the hens, the ducks and the goat :: mcq | Which list is punctuated correctly? | red, blue, green and yellow* | red blue green, and yellow | red, blue, green, and, yellow
play: order | Build the sentence in order | I packed; a hat,; a towel,; sunscreen; and a drink.
check: mcq | How many commas does this need? "We saw cows sheep pigs and goats." | 2* | 3 | 1
check: tf | Commas separate items in a list. | true
check: mcq | Which sentence is punctuated correctly? | I like maths, art and music.* | I like maths art, and, music. | I, like maths art and music.
home: Write your family's shopping list as one sentence with commas.
skill: commas-lists

@lesson 2.2 | Apostrophes in short forms | ✂️ | stage
hook: "do not" can squash into "don't". Where does the missing letter go?
learn: An apostrophe (') can show where letters have been left out.
learn: do not becomes don't. I am becomes I'm. it is becomes it's. we will becomes we'll.
learn: The apostrophe goes where the letters were, not in the gap between the words.
try: can not becomes can't :: mcq | What's the short form of "they are"? | they're* | the'yre | theyr'e
play: match | Match the long and short forms | I have=I've; you are=you're; she will=she'll; does not=doesn't
check: mcq | Which is written correctly? | we'll* | wel'l | well'
check: mcq | "it's" is short for… | it is* | belonging to it | its
check: tf | In "don't", the apostrophe takes the place of the o in "not". | true
home: Find five short forms with apostrophes in a book or on a food packet.
skill: contractions

@lesson 2.3 | Apostrophes for owning | 🦴 | home
hook: "The dogs bone" or "the dog's bone"?
learn: An apostrophe and s show that something belongs to someone: the dog's bone, Mia's bike.
learn: If the owners are more than one and already end in s, just add the apostrophe: the girls' team.
learn: "its" with no apostrophe means belonging to it: The tree lost its leaves.
try: The hen's eggs are the eggs of one hen :: mcq | Which shows the hat belongs to Tom? | Tom's hat* | Toms hat | Toms' hat
play: sort | Owning, Short form | Sam's ball=Owning; can't=Short form; the cat's tail=Owning; I'm=Short form; Mum's car=Owning; we've=Short form | Is the apostrophe for owning, or a short form?
check: mcq | The bikes belong to two boys. Which is right? | the boys' bikes* | the boy's bikes | the boys bikes'
check: mcq | Fill the gap: "The dog wagged … tail." | its* | it's | its'
check: tf | "its" with no apostrophe means belonging to it. | true
home: Label three things at home with who owns them, like "Dad's mug".
skill: possessive-apostrophes

@lesson 2.4 | Speech marks | 💬 | stage
hook: How do readers know when a character is talking?
learn: Speech marks go around the words someone says: “Let's go to the beach,” said Mum.
learn: Start the spoken words with a capital letter.
learn: Put the punctuation inside the speech marks: “Are you coming?” asked Leo.
try: “I'm hungry,” said Sam. :: mcq | Which is punctuated correctly? | “Wait for me!” shouted Ana.* | “Wait for me” ! shouted Ana. | Wait for me! “shouted Ana.”
play: match | Match each speaker to what they said | “Woof!”=The dog; “Time for bed,”=Dad; “Two tickets, please,”=The shopper
check: tf | The comma or question mark goes inside the speech marks. | true
check: mcq | Which words are spoken? “I found it!” yelled Kai. | I found it!* | yelled Kai | Kai
check: mcq | Spoken words begin with… | a capital letter* | a full stop | a comma
home: Write a short chat between you and a pet using speech marks.
skill: speech-marks

@lesson 3.1 | Reading between the lines | 🕵️ | stage
hook: "Jai slammed the door and stomped up the stairs." How is Jai feeling? The writer didn't say!
learn: Inference means working out what the writer hasn't said, using clues.
learn: Slamming doors and stomping are clues that Jai is angry or upset.
learn: Use clues from the text and what you already know.
try: "Her hands shook as she stepped onto the stage" tells you she's nervous :: mcq | "Tama grinned and punched the air." How does Tama feel? | Happy and proud* | Sad | Bored
play: match | Match each clue to the feeling | Yawned and rubbed her eyes=Tired; Hid behind the couch=Scared; Couldn't stop giggling=Amused; Stared out of the window, sighing=Bored
check: mcq | "The streets were wet and people shook their umbrellas." What can you infer? | It had been raining* | It was very hot | It was night-time
check: mcq | Inference uses… | clues from the text and what you already know* | only the pictures | guesses with no clues
check: tf | Writers always tell you exactly how characters feel. | false | Often they show you with clues instead.
home: Watch a short scene with the sound off and work out how people feel from the clues.
skill: inference

@lesson 3.2 | Fact or opinion? | 🥝 | stage
hook: "Kiwi are the best birds in the world." Is that a fact?
learn: A fact can be checked and shown to be true: Kiwi can't fly.
learn: An opinion is what someone thinks or feels: Kiwi are the cutest birds.
learn: Words like best, worst, should, beautiful and boring often signal an opinion.
try: "A week has seven days" is a fact :: mcq | Which is an opinion? | Netball is more fun than football* | A week has seven days | Kiwi lay eggs
play: sort | Fact, Opinion | A week has 7 days=Fact; Pizza is the best dinner=Opinion; The sun is a star=Fact; Winter is boring=Opinion; Kiwi lay eggs=Fact | Fact or opinion?
check: mcq | Which word often signals an opinion? | best* | measured | counted
check: tf | An opinion can be proven true or false. | false | Facts can be checked. Opinions are what people think.
check: mcq | "Our school should have a longer lunch." This is… | an opinion* | a fact | a question
home: Find one fact and one opinion in an ad or on a food packet.
skill: fact-opinion

@lesson 3.3 | Why did they write it? | 📰 | stage
hook: A recipe, a joke and an ad all use words. Do they want the same thing from you?
learn: Writers write to inform (teach facts), to persuade (change your mind) or to entertain.
learn: An ad persuades. A science page informs. A funny story entertains.
learn: Knowing the purpose helps you read carefully, especially ads.
try: A toy ad wants you to buy the toy :: mcq | What's the purpose of a weather report? | To inform* | To entertain | To persuade
play: sort | Inform, Persuade, Entertain | A recipe=Inform; Join our sports club today!=Persuade; A joke book=Entertain; A dictionary=Inform; An ad for sneakers=Persuade; A comic=Entertain | Why was it written?
check: mcq | "Buy now! Only $10! You'll love it!" The writer wants to… | persuade you* | inform you | entertain you
check: tf | A story can entertain and teach something at the same time. | true
check: mcq | Which is most likely written to inform? | A guide to looking after chickens* | A cartoon | A cereal ad
home: Find three pieces of writing at home and say why each one was written.
skill: authors-purpose

@lesson 3.4 | Summing it up | 🏊 | stage
hook: Can you tell a whole story in one sentence?
learn: A summary gives the main points in a few words, in your own words.
learn: Leave out small details and examples.
learn: Read this: "The town pool cracked, and fixing it would take a year. Kids missed swimming, so they held a cake stall. Their money helped the pool open two months early."
try: A summary keeps the main points :: mcq | Which is the best summary of the pool story? | Kids raised money that helped the pool reopen early* | The pool was old | There was a cake stall with chocolate cake
play: sort | Keep, Leave out | The pool cracked=Keep; The cakes had sprinkles=Leave out; Kids raised money=Keep; The pool opened early=Keep; One girl wore a red hat=Leave out | Keep it in the summary, or leave it out?
check: mcq | A summary should be… | short, with the main points* | longer than the story | full of small details
check: tf | A summary uses your own words. | true
check: mcq | What should you leave out of a summary? | Small details* | The main idea | The ending
home: Sum up a show or a book chapter for a grown-up in under 30 words.
skill: summarising
