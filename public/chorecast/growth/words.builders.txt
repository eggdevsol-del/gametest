@lesson 1.1 | Asking so people say yes | 🙏 | stage
hook: You want a later bedtime on Friday. How do you ask?
learn: Good requests are specific, give a reason, and offer something.
learn: "Could I stay up till 9 on Friday to watch the game? I'll be ready on time Saturday."
play: chat | Asking for a later bedtime | them: What's up? | me: Could I stay up till 9 on Friday for the game? I'll still get up on time Saturday*/I want to stay up/Everyone else gets to | them: Hmm, what if you're tired? | me: If I'm grumpy Saturday, I'll go back to 8:30 next week*/I won't be/That's not fair
check: mcq | What makes a request stronger? | A clear ask with a reason* | Whining | Asking five times
check: tf | Offering something in return can help. | true
check: mcq | Which is the clearest request? | Can I have 30 minutes of screen time after homework?* | Can I have screens? | I want stuff
home: Make a clear request at home with a reason and an offer.
skill: asking

@lesson 1.2 | Persuade with reasons | 📣 | stage
hook: How do you convince your class to choose your idea for the trip?
learn: Strong persuasion uses reasons, examples and thinking about what others care about.
learn: Weak persuasion uses only feelings or "because I said so".
play: debate | Which argument better persuades the class to visit the zoo? | We'll see animals we're learning about, and it's free for schools* | The zoo is my favourite place | Sharing why you love something can be persuasive too. People like passion, especially when it comes with reasons.
check: mcq | Which is a strong reason? | It links to what we're learning* | Because I said so | I just want to
check: tf | Thinking about what others care about helps persuade them. | true
check: mcq | Which word often adds a reason? | Because* | Never | Whatever
home: Persuade someone at home to try a new game, using two reasons.
skill: persuasion

@lesson 1.3 | Tone in messages | 💬 | online
hook: "OK." Is that message happy, cross or fine? Hard to tell!
learn: Messages lose your voice, so words matter more.
learn: Add a few warm words. Re-read before you send.
play: mcq | Which reply sounds friendly? | Sounds good! See you at 4 😊* | k | Whatever
check: tf | Texts can sound ruder than you meant. | true
check: mcq | Before sending a message you should… | Re-read it* | Send it fast | Add 50 emojis
check: mcq | Which is clearest for meeting up? | Let's meet at the library at 3:30* | Meet somewhere sometime | soon?
home: Re-read your next three messages before you send them.
skill: tone

@lesson 1.4 | Presenting | 🎤 | stage
hook: Everyone's looking at you. Your mind goes blank. Now what?
learn: Plan three main points. Practise out loud.
learn: Breathe, slow down and look at a few friendly faces.
play: order | Put presentation prep in order | Pick your three main points; Find an example for each; Practise out loud; Breathe and slow down; Present and smile
check: mcq | How many main points is easy to remember? | About three* | Twenty | Zero
check: tf | Practising out loud helps. | true
check: mcq | Nervous before talking? Try… | Slow breaths* | Talking super fast | Hiding
home: Give a 1-minute talk to your family about your favourite animal.
skill: presenting

@lesson 1.5 | Disagree kindly | 🤝 | garden
hook: Your friend wants to play tag. You want to play ball. Argument incoming?
learn: Disagreeing is fine. Be kind about it.
learn: Listen, say what you think, then find a fair plan.
play: path | You and a friend want different games at lunch. | Suggest tag first, then ball => good: Fair plan! Everyone gets a turn. | Say "Your game is boring" => bad: Ouch. Now nobody wants to play. | Go off and play alone => ok: You avoided a fight, but you missed out on playing together.
check: mcq | A kind way to disagree? | I see why you like it. Could we take turns?* | You're wrong | Whatever
check: tf | It's OK to disagree with friends. | true
check: mcq | A fair plan might be… | Taking turns* | Only doing what you want | Not playing
home: Next time you disagree, try "I see your point, and…".
skill: disagreeing

@lesson 1.6 | Clear instructions | 📋 | kitchen
hook: Could a robot follow your instructions to make toast?
learn: Good instructions are in order, one step at a time, with exact words.
play: build | Build the toast instructions in order | Put bread in the toaster; Push the lever down; Wait for it to pop up; Take the toast out carefully; Spread the butter
check: mcq | Good instructions are… | In order and exact* | Mixed up | Very long sentences
check: tf | Each step should be one action. | true
check: mcq | Which instruction is clearest? | Push the lever down* | Do the toaster thing | Make it work
home: Write instructions for a simple job and have someone follow them exactly.
skill: instructions
