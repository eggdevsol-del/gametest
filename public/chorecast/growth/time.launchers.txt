@lesson 1.1 | Fiat money | 💵 | bank
hook: Your $20 note isn't backed by gold. So why is it worth anything?

learn: Most money is created when banks make loans, as deposits in accounts.

@lesson 1.2 | Enron | 📊 | townhall
hook: A giant energy company looked super profitable. Then it collapsed in weeks. How?
learn: Enron hid debts and invented profits with accounting tricks.
learn: When the truth came out in 2001, the company went bankrupt and its auditor collapsed too.
learn: New laws made company leaders and auditors more responsible for honest accounts.

@lesson 2.1 | Measuring inflation | 🧺 | townhall
hook: How does anyone know prices rose 3% last year? Who checks?
learn: {nz:Stats NZ~au:The Australian Bureau of Statistics} tracks the prices of a "basket" of things households buy, like food, rent, petrol and power.
learn: This is the {nz:Consumers Price Index~au:Consumer Price Index} (CPI). Its change over a year is the inflation rate.
learn: Your own inflation can differ. A renter who drives a lot feels rent and petrol rises more.

@lesson 2.2 | Inflation targets | 🎯 | bank
hook: Why don't central banks aim for 0% inflation?
learn: {nz:In 1989, New Zealand became the first country to give its central bank an inflation target in law.~au:The Reserve Bank of Australia began targeting inflation in the early 1990s.}
learn: {nz:The Reserve Bank of New Zealand aims to keep inflation between 1% and 3%, near 2%.~au:The Reserve Bank of Australia aims to keep inflation between 2% and 3%, near 2.5%.}
learn: A little inflation is seen as healthy. It guards against deflation spirals and leaves room to cut rates.

@lesson 2.3 | The cash rate | 🏦 | bank
hook: The central bank raises interest rates. Why would that slow price rises?
learn: {nz:The Reserve Bank sets the Official Cash Rate (OCR).~au:The Reserve Bank sets the cash rate.} It flows through to the rates banks charge and pay.
learn: Higher rates make borrowing dearer and saving more attractive, so people and businesses spend less, and price rises ease.
learn: Lower rates do the opposite when the economy is weak. Changes take a year or two to fully work.
play: sort | Raise rates, Cut rates | Inflation is well above target=Raise rates; The economy is slumping and inflation is low=Cut rates; Everyone is borrowing and prices are racing up=Raise rates; Jobs are disappearing and prices are flat=Cut rates | What would the central bank likely do?
check: mcq | How do higher interest rates slow inflation? | People and businesses borrow and spend less* | Shops are banned from raising prices | More money is printed
check: tf | Interest rate changes take time to work through the economy. | true
check: mcq | Inflation is well below target and the economy is weak. The central bank would likely… | Cut rates* | Raise rates a lot | Close the banks
home: Find the current {nz:OCR~au:cash rate} and the date of the next decision.
skill: centralbank

@lesson 2.4 | Hyperinflation | 💸 | museum
hook: In Germany in 1923, some workers were paid twice a day and rushed to spend before prices rose again. How did it get so bad?
learn: Hyperinflation is when prices rise extremely fast, sometimes doubling within days.
learn: Germany in 1923 and Zimbabwe around 2008 printed huge amounts of money to pay their bills.
learn: Savings became worthless. People turned to foreign money or swapping goods instead.
