@lesson 1.1 | Gross and net pay | 🧾 | bank
hook: Your job pays $20 an hour. You work 10 hours. Why isn't $200 in your account?
learn: Gross pay is what you earn before anything comes out.
learn: Net pay, or take-home pay, is what lands in your account.
learn: Tax and other deductions sit in between.
try: 10 hours × $20 = $200 gross :: gap | 15 hours × $22 = $_ gross | 330
play: gen | percent | 4
check: mcq | What is net pay? | What you take home after deductions* | What you earn before tax | Your hourly rate
check: gap | Gross $300, deductions $45. Net = $_ | 255
check: tf | Gross pay is always bigger than or equal to net pay. | true
home: Ask a grown-up to show you the gross and net lines on a payslip.

@lesson 1.2 | How income tax works | 🏛️ | townhall
hook: If you earn more, do you lose more of every dollar to tax?
learn: Income tax is taken from your pay by your employer and sent to {nz:Inland Revenue (IRD)~au:the Australian Taxation Office (ATO)}.
learn: Rates go up in steps called brackets. A higher rate only applies to the part of your income above each step.
learn: So a pay rise never leaves you worse off after tax.
learn: Rates change from time to time. Check {nz:ird.govt.nz~au:ato.gov.au} for this year's numbers.
play: debate | Is it true that a pay rise can push you into a higher bracket and leave you with less? | No. Only the extra dollars are taxed at the higher rate* | Yes. All your income gets taxed at the new rate | Some benefits and student loan repayments can change with income, so it's worth checking the whole picture.
check: mcq | Who usually takes tax from your pay? | Your employer* | You, at the end of the year only | Your bank
check: tf | Moving into a higher tax bracket makes all your income taxed at that rate. | false
check: mcq | Where do you find this year's official tax rates? | {nz:ird.govt.nz~au:ato.gov.au}* | A social media post | Your friend's guess
home: Look up this year's tax rates on {nz:ird.govt.nz~au:ato.gov.au} with a grown-up.
skill: tax

@lesson 2.2 | The bond | 💰 | bank
hook: The landlord asks for $2,400 bond. Where does that money go?
learn: Bond is money held in case you damage the place or don't pay rent.
learn: {nz:In NZ a landlord can ask for up to 4 weeks' rent as bond. They must lodge it with Tenancy Services within 23 working days.~au:In Australia the landlord or agent must lodge your bond with your state or territory's bond authority. In most places the limit is about 4 weeks' rent.}
learn: You get it back at the end if the place is in good shape, apart from fair wear and tear.
play: order | Getting your bond back. Put the steps in order. | Do a condition report with photos when you move in; Look after the place while you live there; Clean well before you leave; Do a final check with the landlord; Apply for the bond refund; Get the bond paid back to you

check: tf | You can lose part of your bond for normal wear and tear. | false | Fair wear and tear, like slightly faded paint, isn't damage.

@lesson 2.3 | Your tenancy agreement | 📝 | home
hook: You're handed a 10-page agreement. Do you sign straight away?
learn: Get your tenancy agreement in writing and keep a copy. It lists the rent, bond, start date and rules.
learn: Check if it's fixed-term, with a set end date, or periodic, which rolls on until someone gives notice.
learn: {nz:In NZ a landlord can't ask for more than 2 weeks' rent in advance.~au:The law limits how much rent in advance you can be asked for, often 2 weeks.}
learn: Tenancy law still applies even if the agreement says otherwise.
play: diff | The ad and the agreement. Tap what's different. | Rent $280 a week; Bond 4 weeks; Pets OK; Power not included; Starts 1 March | Rent $300 a week; Bond 4 weeks; No pets; Power not included; Starts 1 March
check: mcq | What's a fixed-term tenancy? | One with a set end date* | One that can never end | One with no rent
check: tf | A clause that signs away your legal rights as a tenant doesn't count. | true | The law applies whatever the agreement says.
check: mcq | Before you sign, you should… | Read it all and ask about anything unclear* | Sign fast before someone else does | Only read the rent
home: Read a sample tenancy agreement on {nz:tenancy.govt.nz~au:your state's fair trading website} and find the rent increase rules.

@lesson 2.4 | Rent rises and repairs | 🔧 | home
hook: Your rent goes up after 4 months. Can they do that?
learn: {nz:In NZ rent can only go up once every 12 months, with at least 60 days' written notice.~au:In most states rent can only go up once every 12 months, with written notice, often 60 days.}
learn: Landlords must keep the place in reasonable repair. Report problems in writing and keep a copy.
learn: {nz:Rentals must meet the Healthy Homes Standards for heating, insulation, ventilation, moisture and draughts.~au:Many states now set minimum standards for rentals, covering things like locks, heating, water and power.}

check: tf | Stopping your rent is a legal way to force repairs. | false | Keep paying rent and use the proper steps, like a tribunal.

@lesson 2.5 | When it goes wrong | ⚖️ | townhall
hook: The landlord keeps $800 of your bond for "cleaning". The place was spotless. Now what?
learn: Talk first. Many problems get sorted with a calm message and good evidence.
learn: {nz:If not, you can apply to the Tenancy Tribunal. Tenancy Services and Community Law give free advice.~au:If not, you can apply to your state or territory's tribunal. Tenants' advice services can help for free.}
learn: Your dated photos from moving in and out are your best evidence.
play: path | Your landlord wants to keep $800 of bond for cleaning. You cleaned well and took photos. | Send a polite message with your photos and ask for the full bond => good: Strong start. With evidence, many landlords back down. If not, the tribunal can decide. | Post an angry review naming the landlord => bad: It might feel good, but it won't get your bond back and could cause new trouble. | Give up and let them keep it => bad: That's $800 gone, even though you had evidence and rights.
check: mcq | What's your best evidence in a bond dispute? | Dated photos from moving in and out* | Your memory | A friend's opinion
check: mcq | Who can decide a tenancy dispute you can't sort out? | {nz:The Tenancy Tribunal~au:Your state or territory's tribunal}* | The landlord | Your bank
