@lesson 1.1 | Writing an email | 📧 | online
hook: You email a coach about missing training: "hey cant come". Will that land well?
learn: Good emails have a clear subject, a greeting, a short message, and a sign-off.
learn: Say what you need and by when.
play: order | Build the email in order | Subject: Missing training on Thursday; Hi Coach Sam,; I have a dentist appointment and can't make training on Thursday.; I'll do the drills at home and see you Saturday.; Thanks, Ava
check: mcq | What should a subject line do? | Say what the email is about* | Be blank | Say "hi"
check: tf | A greeting and sign-off make emails more professional. | true
check: mcq | Best way to end an email to a coach? | Thanks, Ava* | bye lol | Nothing
home: Write a practice email to a teacher asking a question.
skill: email

@lesson 1.2 | Negotiating | 🤝 | stage
hook: You want $30 for mowing. Your neighbour offers $20. Do you take it, walk away, or talk?
learn: Find what both sides need. Explain your value. Look for options in between.
learn: Know your walk-away point before you start.
play: chat | Negotiating a mowing job | them: I can pay $20 to mow the lawns. | me: Thanks! It's a big section and takes me 90 minutes. Could we do $28?*/$20 is a rip-off/OK fine | them: That's more than I wanted. | me: How about $25, and I'll do the edges too?*/Then forget it/I'll do it for $10
check: mcq | What should you know before negotiating? | Your walk-away point* | Your neighbour's password | Nothing
check: tf | Offering extra value can help close a deal. | true
check: mcq | A good negotiation ends with… | Both sides feeling it's fair* | One side angry | Nobody agreeing
home: Practise a negotiation with a grown-up for a chore price.
skill: negotiate

@lesson 1.3 | Spotting bias | 🔎 | online
hook: Two articles about the same event say opposite things. Who's right?
learn: Bias is leaning one way. Check who wrote it, who paid, and what evidence they use.
learn: Compare several reliable sources.
play: sort | Fact, Opinion | The concert started at 7pm=Fact; The concert was amazing=Opinion; 2,000 people attended=Fact; The band is the best ever=Opinion; Tickets cost $40=Fact; It was worth every cent=Opinion | Fact or opinion?
check: mcq | How can you check a claim? | Compare reliable sources* | Believe the first post | Ask the author only
check: tf | Opinions can be useful but aren't the same as facts. | true
check: mcq | What's a warning sign of bias? | Only one side is shown* | Facts with sources | Dates and names
home: Find one fact and one opinion in a news article.
skill: critical

@lesson 1.4 | Speaking with stories | 🎙️ | stage
hook: Why do people remember stories better than lists?
learn: A short story with a problem, an action and a result makes your point stick.
learn: Start with a hook, tell the story, then make your point.
play: order | Put the talk in order | Hook: Last year our canteen threw out 300 sandwiches; Story: We started a pre-order system; Result: Waste dropped by half; Point: Small changes can cut waste
check: mcq | What makes a story stick? | A problem, action and result* | Lots of numbers only | Reading off a page
check: tf | Starting with a hook grabs attention. | true
check: mcq | Where does your main point usually go? | After the story* | Never | First word
home: Tell a 60-second story with a hook and a point.
skill: presenting

@lesson 1.5 | Giving feedback | 🗣️ | workshop
hook: Your friend's song needs work. How do you say so without crushing them?
learn: Be specific and kind. Say what works, what could improve, and why.
learn: Focus on the work, not the person.
play: mcq | Which feedback is most helpful? | The chorus is catchy. The verses feel long. Could you cut one?* | It's bad | It's fine, I guess
check: tf | Good feedback is specific. | true
check: mcq | Feedback should focus on… | The work* | The person's looks | Their friends
check: mcq | Which starts feedback kindly? | Here's what I liked…* | You always mess up | Ugh
home: Give someone specific, kind feedback today.
skill: feedback

@lesson 1.6 | Your online voice | 🌐 | online
hook: A comment you post at 14 could be found at 24. Does that matter?
learn: Posts can last forever, be screenshotted and shared.
learn: Before posting, ask: is it true, kind and would I be OK with anyone seeing it?
play: scam | Post it or rethink it? | Congrats to our team on winning the final!=real; Screenshot of a friend's private message to laugh at=scam; A photo of your art project=real; An angry rant about a teacher by name=scam | "Real" means fine to post, "scam" means rethink it.
check: mcq | Before posting, ask… | Is it true, kind and OK for anyone to see?* | Will it go viral? | Who can I annoy?
check: tf | Deleted posts can still be screenshotted. | true
check: mcq | A friend's private message should be… | Kept private* | Posted for laughs | Sent to everyone
home: Check the privacy settings on one account with a grown-up.
skill: reputation
