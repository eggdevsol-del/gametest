@lesson 1.1 | Making a claim | 📣 | stage
hook: "Our school should start 30 minutes later." How would you convince the principal?
learn: A persuasive piece starts with a clear claim: what you want the reader to agree with.
learn: A claim must be specific and arguable. "School exists" isn't a claim. "Phones should stay in lockers during class" is.
learn: Everything in the piece should support that one claim.
try: "Every student should learn to cook" is a claim :: mcq | Which is the strongest claim? | Our town needs a covered bike park at the station* | Bikes exist | I like bikes
play: sort | Claim, Not a claim | Homework should be limited to 30 minutes a night=Claim; Homework is given at school=Not a claim; Every school should have a vegetable garden=Claim; Vegetables are plants=Not a claim | Is it an arguable claim?
check: mcq | A good claim is… | clear and arguable* | a fact nobody disputes | a question with no answer
check: tf | A persuasive piece should support one clear claim. | true
check: mcq | Which one is arguable? | Teenagers should be paid for doing chores* | Chores are jobs around the house | Some families have chore charts
home: Write one claim about something you'd like to change at home, then talk it over with a grown-up.
skill: persuasive-claim

@lesson 1.2 | Reasons and evidence | 🧾 | stage
hook: "Because I said so" isn't a great reason. What is?
learn: Back your claim with reasons, and back each reason with evidence: facts, examples or what experts say.
learn: Claim: schools should have gardens. Reason: kids learn where food comes from. Evidence: a class that grew and cooked its own vegetables.
learn: Strong evidence is specific and comes from a source the reader can trust.
try: A reason plus evidence makes a claim believable :: mcq | Which is the best evidence that the station needs a bike park? | A count showing 40 bikes chained to the fence each morning* | Bikes are cool | My friend said so
play: match | Match each part of an argument to its example | Claim=Libraries should open on Sundays; Reason=Many families are only free at weekends; Evidence=A survey of 100 parents at our school
check: mcq | Which reason best supports "Kids should learn to budget"? | It helps them manage money as adults* | Budgets are written on paper | Some people like spreadsheets
check: tf | A friend's opinion is the strongest kind of evidence. | false | Facts, data and trusted experts are stronger.
check: mcq | Good evidence is… | specific and trustworthy* | vague and exciting | as long as possible
home: Find one fact that supports something you believe, and say where it came from.
skill: evidence

@lesson 1.3 | The other side | ⚖️ | stage
hook: Why would a persuasive writer mention the opposite view?
learn: A counterargument is the strongest point against your claim.
learn: Mention it, then answer it (that's a rebuttal): "Some say gardens take too much work. But a weekly roster shares it out."
learn: Taking on the other side shows you're fair, which makes you more convincing.
try: A counterargument is followed by your rebuttal :: mcq | What's a rebuttal? | Your answer to the other side's point* | Your first claim | The title
play: order | Put the paragraph in order | Some people think phones help with schoolwork.; That's true for some tasks.; But phones also distract, so they should stay in bags unless a teacher asks for them.
check: mcq | Why include a counterargument? | It shows you've thought fairly about the issue* | It makes the piece longer | It confuses readers
check: tf | A counterargument supports your own claim. | false | It's the other side's point, which you then answer.
check: mcq | Which is a rebuttal to "School gardens cost too much"? | Seeds and compost are cheap, and families can lend tools* | Gardens are nice | Costs are numbers
home: Pick a family debate, like bedtime, and write the best argument for the side you disagree with.
skill: counterargument

@lesson 1.4 | Words that persuade | 🎯 | stage
hook: Ads say "Don't miss out!" and "Everyone's talking about it!" Why?
learn: Persuasive techniques include strong verbs, rhetorical questions ("Who wants dirty rivers?"), repetition and appeals to feelings.
learn: "Everyone's doing it" (the bandwagon) and false urgency ("Today only!") are tricks to watch for.
learn: Use techniques honestly in your own writing, and spot them when they're used on you.
try: "Don't you deserve the best?" is a rhetorical question :: mcq | "Only 2 left! Buy now!" uses… | urgency* | evidence | a counterargument
play: match | Match each technique to an example | Rhetorical question=Who wants to breathe dirty air?; Bandwagon=Everyone at school has one!; Urgency=Sale ends tonight!; Repetition=Clean streets, clean parks, clean town.
check: mcq | Which is a rhetorical question? | Isn't it time we fixed the playground?* | The playground has a slide. | Fix the playground.
check: tf | "Everyone has one" is good evidence. | false | It's the bandwagon trick. Being popular doesn't prove something is good.
check: mcq | Why do ads use urgency? | To make you decide before you think* | To give you facts | To be fair
home: Spot three persuasive techniques in ads this week and point them out to a grown-up.
skill: persuasive-techniques

@lesson 2.1 | Clauses | 🧱 | stage
hook: Is "when the bell rang" a full sentence?
learn: A clause has a subject and a verb. A main clause makes sense alone: "We ran outside."
learn: A subordinate clause doesn't make sense alone: "when the bell rang".
learn: Join them to make a complex sentence: "When the bell rang, we ran outside."
try: "Because it rained, the game was cancelled" has two clauses :: mcq | Which is a main clause? | The dog barked* | Although the dog barked | Because the dog barked
play: match | Join each subordinate clause to a main clause | When the sun set=the beach went quiet; Because she practised=she won the race; Although it was cold=we went for a swim
check: mcq | Which is a complex sentence? | After we ate, we washed the dishes.* | We ate. | We ate dinner.
check: tf | "Although it was late" is a complete sentence. | false | It's a subordinate clause, so it needs a main clause.
check: mcq | Which word often starts a subordinate clause? | because* | the | it
home: Write three sentences that start with when, because and although.
skill: clauses

@lesson 2.2 | Subject and verb agree | 🤝 | stage
hook: "The box of apples are heavy." Is that right?
learn: A singular subject takes a singular verb: The dog runs.
learn: A plural subject takes a plural verb: The dogs run.
learn: Watch out for words in between: "The box of apples is heavy." The subject is the box, not the apples.
try: The list of chores is long :: mcq | Which is correct? | The basket of eggs is full.* | The basket of eggs are full. | The basket of eggs be full.
play: sort | is, are | The cat=is; My friends=are; One of the girls=is; The kids in the team=are; Everybody=is | Which verb fits: is or are?
check: mcq | "Each of the players … a number on their shirt." | has* | have | having
check: mcq | Which is correct? | The dogs bark at night.* | The dogs barks at night. | The dogs is barking at night.
check: tf | In "The box of apples is heavy", the subject is "apples". | false | The subject is "box", so the verb is "is".
home: Read something you wrote and check every subject agrees with its verb.
skill: subject-verb

@lesson 2.3 | Run-on sentences | 🏃 | stage
hook: "I got home I fed the dog it was hungry I gave it a bone." Phew! What's wrong?
learn: A run-on joins sentences with no proper punctuation or joining word.
learn: Fix it with a full stop, a joining word (and, but, so, because) or a semicolon.
learn: A comma alone can't join two full sentences. "I got home, I fed the dog" is a comma splice.
try: I got home. I fed the dog because it was hungry. :: mcq | Which fixes "It rained we stayed inside"? | It rained, so we stayed inside.* | It rained, we stayed inside. | It rained we, stayed inside.
play: sort | Correct, Run-on | I was tired, so I slept.=Correct; I was tired I slept.=Run-on; She ran fast and she won.=Correct; She ran, she won.=Run-on; He laughed because it was funny.=Correct | Correct, or a run-on?
check: mcq | "The bus was late, we missed the movie." This is a… | comma splice* | correct sentence | question
check: tf | A semicolon can join two closely linked sentences. | true
check: mcq | What's the best fix for "I love maths it's my favourite"? | I love maths. It's my favourite.* | I love maths, it's my favourite. | I love, maths it's my favourite.
home: Find a run-on in something you wrote or a message you sent, and fix it.
skill: run-ons

@lesson 2.4 | Active and passive | 🔁 | stage
hook: "The dog chased the cat" or "The cat was chased by the dog". Which sounds stronger?
learn: In the active voice, the subject does the action: The dog chased the cat.
learn: In the passive voice, the action is done to the subject: The cat was chased by the dog.
learn: Active is usually clearer and stronger. Passive helps when the doer is unknown or doesn't matter: "The window was broken."
try: Active: Mia painted the fence. Passive: The fence was painted by Mia. :: mcq | Which is active? | The chef cooked the meal.* | The meal was cooked by the chef. | The meal was cooked.
play: sort | Active, Passive | Kai kicked the ball.=Active; The ball was kicked by Kai.=Passive; The cake was eaten.=Passive; Hens lay eggs.=Active; The eggs were collected.=Passive | Active or passive?
check: mcq | Make it active: "The test was marked by Mrs Ng." | Mrs Ng marked the test.* | The test marked Mrs Ng. | Mrs Ng was marked by the test.
check: tf | The passive voice is useful when we don't know who did something. | true
check: mcq | Which is usually clearer? | The active voice* | The passive voice | Neither
home: Rewrite three passive sentences from an article or a sign in the active voice.
skill: active-passive

@lesson 3.1 | Similes and metaphors | 🦁 | stage
hook: "The classroom was a zoo." Was it full of animals?
learn: Figurative language says something in a way that isn't literal, to create a picture.
learn: A simile compares using "like" or "as": as brave as a lion, ran like the wind.
learn: A metaphor says one thing is another: The classroom was a zoo.
try: "Her smile was sunshine" is a metaphor :: mcq | Which is a simile? | He was as quiet as a mouse* | He was a mouse | He saw a mouse
play: sort | Simile, Metaphor | as cold as ice=Simile; The road was a ribbon=Metaphor; ran like a cheetah=Simile; My brother is a night owl=Metaphor; as light as a feather=Simile | Simile or metaphor?
check: mcq | "The stars were diamonds" is a… | metaphor* | simile | fact
check: tf | A simile uses "like" or "as". | true
check: mcq | What does "The classroom was a zoo" mean? | It was noisy and wild* | It had animals in it | It was empty
home: Describe your morning using one simile and one metaphor.
skill: figurative-language

@lesson 3.2 | Personification and imagery | 🌬️ | garden
hook: "The wind howled and the trees shivered." Can wind really howl?
learn: Personification gives human actions or feelings to things that aren't human.
learn: Imagery is description that appeals to the senses: sight, sound, smell, taste and touch.
learn: Writers use both to make readers feel something.
try: "The sun smiled down" is personification :: mcq | Which is personification? | The old car groaned up the hill* | The car was red | The car was as fast as a jet
play: match | Match each line to the sense it appeals to | The bread's warm, buttery smell=Smell; Waves crashed and hissed=Sound; Icy water stung my toes=Touch; The sour lemon made my lips pucker=Taste
check: tf | "The leaves danced in the wind" is personification. | true
check: mcq | Imagery appeals to… | the senses* | numbers | rules
check: mcq | Why do writers use personification? | To bring things to life for the reader* | To give facts | To make writing shorter
home: Write two sentences about your house at night using personification.
skill: personification-imagery

@lesson 3.3 | Tone | 🎭 | stage
hook: "Nice job." Said with a smile or an eye-roll, it means different things. How does writing show tone?
learn: Tone is the writer's attitude: cheerful, serious, angry, sarcastic, worried.
learn: Word choice shows tone: "a tiny hiccup" and "a disaster" could describe the same thing.
learn: Spotting tone helps you understand what the writer really means.
try: "Disaster struck when the cake collapsed" has a dramatic tone :: mcq | "We're thrilled to welcome you!" The tone is… | enthusiastic* | angry | worried
play: match | Match each sentence to its tone | Please remember to wipe your feet.=Polite; I can't believe you did this again!=Angry; What a glorious morning!=Joyful; I hope the storm passes soon.=Worried
check: mcq | Which word gives the most serious tone? | catastrophe* | oops | hiccup
check: tf | The same event can be written with different tones. | true
check: mcq | Tone is shown mostly by… | word choice* | font size | page numbers
home: Rewrite "The bus was late" in a cheerful tone, then an angry one.
skill: tone

@lesson 3.4 | Spotting bias | 🛹 | stage
hook: Two articles about the same skate park. One says "noisy eyesore", the other "buzzing hangout". Who's right?
learn: Bias is when writing leans to one side, often by leaving things out or using loaded words.
learn: Loaded words carry strong feelings: eyesore, heroic, disaster, amazing.
learn: To check for bias, ask: Who wrote this? What might they want? What's missing?
try: "Noisy eyesore" uses loaded words :: mcq | Which is the most neutral description? | The skate park opened on Saturday* | The fantastic skate park finally opened | The terrible skate park opened
play: sort | Loaded, Neutral | Ridiculous plan=Loaded; New plan=Neutral; Heroic effort=Loaded; Large crowd=Neutral; Shocking choice=Loaded | Loaded or neutral?
check: mcq | A skateboard shop writes "Skate parks are the best thing for any town". Why might this be biased? | The shop sells skateboards* | The shop is in a town | The shop uses full stops
check: tf | Leaving out the other side can make writing biased. | true
check: mcq | Which question helps you spot bias? | What might the writer want me to think?* | How many pages is it? | What font is it in?
home: Compare two reviews or ads for the same thing and find the loaded words.
skill: bias
