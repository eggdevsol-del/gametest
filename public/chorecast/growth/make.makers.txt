@lesson 1.1 | The one-page plan | 🗒️ | workshop
hook: You've got a business idea. What should you write down before spending a cent?
learn: A one-page plan covers the problem, your solution, your customers, costs, price and how you'll reach people.
learn: Writing it down shows the gaps before they cost you money.
play: match | Match each part of the plan to an example | Problem=Students forget their lunches; Solution=A lunch subscription; Customers=Busy parents; Price=$6 a lunch; Getting the word out=Flyers at school pick-up
check: mcq | What should a plan start with? | The problem you're solving* | Your logo | Your profit
check: tf | A plan can show problems before you spend money. | true
check: mcq | Who are "customers" in a plan? | The people who'll pay* | Your friends | Your competitors
home: Write a one-page plan for a business idea you like.
skill: planning

@lesson 1.2 | Margins | 📐 | stall
hook: Two businesses each sell $1,000 of stuff. Why does one keep $600 and the other $100?
learn: Margin is the share of the price you keep after the cost of making it.
learn: Margin % = (price − cost) ÷ price × 100.
try: Price $10, cost $6. Margin = $4, or 40% :: gap | Price $20, cost $15. Margin = _% | 25
play: price | Which supplier gives you cheaper stock per candle? | Supplier A:48:24:candles; Supplier B:30:12:candles
check: gap | Price $50, cost $30. Margin = _% | 40
check: tf | A bigger margin means you keep more of each sale. | true
check: mcq | What lowers your margin? | Higher costs* | Higher prices | More customers
home: Find a product and estimate its margin.
skill: profit

@lesson 1.3 | Build a brand | ✨ | stage
hook: Why would someone pay more for a brand they know?
learn: A brand is what people think and feel about your business.
learn: It's built by being consistent, keeping promises and treating customers well.
play: debate | What builds a strong brand most? | Keeping promises to customers, every time* | A really cool logo | A great logo and name help people remember you, but trust is what keeps them coming back.
check: mcq | A brand is mostly… | What people think and feel about you* | Just your logo | Your bank balance
check: tf | Breaking promises hurts a brand. | true
check: mcq | What helps people recognise a brand? | Being consistent* | Changing your name every week | Hiding your name
home: Pick a brand you trust and say why you trust it.
skill: marketing

@lesson 1.4 | Selling online safely | 🛒 | online
hook: You're selling sneakers online. A buyer wants to pay later with a weird link. Hmm.
learn: Use trusted marketplaces and their payment systems.
learn: Never send items before payment clears, and never click links buyers send you.
play: scam | Real buyer or scammer? | I'll pay through the marketplace now and pick up tomorrow=real; I've overpaid by $200, please refund the difference to my cousin=scam; Click this link to receive your payment=scam; Can you post it today? Paid through the site just now=real; I'm overseas. My courier will collect it. Send it first, I'll pay later=scam | Overpayment refunds, payment links and "send first" are classic scams.
check: mcq | A buyer overpays and asks for a refund to someone else. That's… | A common scam* | Generous | Normal
check: tf | You should send items before the payment clears. | false
check: mcq | Safest way to get paid online? | Through the marketplace's own system* | A link from the buyer | Gift cards
home: Check the safety tips page on a marketplace you know.
skill: scams

@lesson 1.5 | Cash flow | 🌊 | bank
hook: A business is making a profit but can't pay its bills. How?
learn: Cash flow is the timing of money in and out.
learn: If you pay suppliers now but customers pay in 30 days, you can run out of cash.
play: diff | Plan and reality. Tap what changed. | Week 1: Buy stock −$200; Week 2: Sales +$300; Week 3: Rent −$100; Week 4: Sales +$300 | Week 1: Buy stock −$200; Week 2: Sales +$50; Week 3: Rent −$100; Week 4: Sales +$550
check: mcq | What is cash flow? | When money comes in and goes out* | Your logo | Total profit only
check: tf | A profitable business can still run out of cash. | true
check: mcq | What helps cash flow? | Getting customers to pay sooner* | Paying bills early when you have no money | Ignoring it
home: Ask a grown-up when their bills are due compared with when they get paid.
skill: cashflow

@lesson 1.6 | Canva's start | 🎨 | stage
hook: An Australian student kept getting told "no". Then she built one of the world's biggest design tools.
learn: As a university student in Perth, Melanie Perkins taught other students to use design software, and saw how hard it was.
learn: Her first business made school yearbooks easier to design. Then came Canva.
learn: Many investors said no before some said yes. She kept improving her pitch.
play: mystery | She grew up in Perth, Australia; She noticed design software was too hard; Her first company made yearbook design easier; She co-founded Canva | Melanie Perkins* | Sara Blakely | Mikaila Ulmer
check: mcq | What problem did Canva solve? | Design tools were too hard to use* | People needed more phones | Cars were too slow
check: tf | Hearing "no" from investors meant the idea was bad. | false | Many great ideas get lots of no's first.
check: mcq | What did Melanie do after rejections? | Improved her pitch and kept going* | Gave up | Copied someone else
home: Think of an everyday problem and an idea that could make it easier.
skill: grit
