@lesson 2.1 | Straight line or curve? | 📈 | shares
hook: Two charts. One is a straight line. One starts flat, then shoots up. Which shows compound growth?
learn: Adding $1,000 a year with no interest makes a straight line.
learn: Compound growth makes a curve that gets steeper, because growth earns more growth.
learn: In the early years the two look almost the same. The gap shows up later.

check: mcq | Which shape shows compound growth? | A curve that gets steeper* | A flat line | A straight line
check: tf | Compound growth looks most impressive in the later years. | true
check: mcq | Why does the curve get steeper? | Growth earns more growth* | The bank adds a bonus | Charts are drawn that way for fun
home: Sketch a straight line and a compound curve, and explain the difference to someone at home.
skill: compound

@lesson 2.2 | The rule of 72 | ✌️ | shares
hook: How long does money take to double? There's a shortcut.
learn: Divide 72 by the yearly growth rate. The answer is roughly how many years it takes to double.
learn: At 6% a year, money doubles in about 12 years. At 8%, about 9 years.
learn: It works for debt too. At 24% interest, a debt doubles in about 3 years.
try: 72 ÷ 6 = 12 years to double :: gap | At 9% a year, money doubles in about _ years | 8
play: gen | compound | 3
check: gap | At 4% a year, money doubles in about _ years | 18
check: mcq | At 12% a year, roughly how long does money take to double? | 6 years* | 12 years | 72 years
check: tf | The rule of 72 works for debt too. | true
home: Use the rule of 72 on a savings rate you find online.
skill: compound

@lesson 2.5 | Real growth | 🎈 | shares
hook: Your money grew 5% this year. Prices rose 3%. How much better off are you really?
learn: Inflation means prices rise over time, so each dollar buys a bit less.
learn: Real growth is roughly your return minus inflation.
learn: Long-term charts can look huge. Allowing for inflation shows what the money can really buy.
try: 6% return − 2% inflation ≈ 4% real growth :: gap | 7% return − 3% inflation ≈ _% real growth | 4
play: slider | Your savings earn 2% a year. Inflation is 3%. Roughly what's your real growth, in %? | -5 | 5 | -1 | % | 0 | 1
check: gap | 8% return − 3% inflation ≈ _% real growth | 5
check: tf | If inflation is higher than your interest rate, your money buys less over time. | true
check: mcq | Real growth means… | Growth after taking off inflation* | Growth with bonuses added | Growth before fees
