@lesson 1.1 | Capital letters and full stops | ✍️ | stage
hook: How do you know where a sentence starts and stops?
learn: A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop.
learn: Names of people, places and days start with a capital letter too: Ari, Napier, Monday.
learn: A sentence makes sense on its own: "The hen laid an egg."
try: "my dog is big." needs a capital M :: mcq | Which sentence is written correctly? | We fed the ducks.* | we fed the ducks. | We fed the ducks
play: sort | Needs a capital, Fine as it is | ari=Needs a capital; tuesday=Needs a capital; apple=Fine as it is; wellington=Needs a capital; chair=Fine as it is | Does it need a capital letter?
check: mcq | Which word needs a capital letter? | sydney* | river | house
check: tf | A sentence can end with a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark. | true
check: mcq | Where does the full stop go? | At the end of the sentence* | At the start | After every word
home: Write two sentences about your day. Check each one has a capital letter and a full stop.
skill: capitals-full-stops

@lesson 1.2 | Asking questions | ❓ | stage
hook: "Where is my hat?" How can you tell this is a question?
learn: A question asks something. It ends with a question mark (?).
learn: Questions often start with who, what, where, when, why or how.
learn: An exclamation mark (!) shows a strong feeling: "Watch out!"
try: "Can I help?" ends with a question mark :: mcq | Which one needs a question mark? | Where are the eggs* | The eggs are here | Here are the eggs
play: sort | Question mark, Full stop | What time is it=Question mark; I like apples=Full stop; How old are you=Question mark; The sky is blue=Full stop; Who is at the door=Question mark | Which mark goes at the end?
check: mcq | Which word often starts a question? | Why* | Because | And
check: mcq | Which mark fits best at the end of "Look out, a wave"? | !* | ? | ,
check: tf | "How many eggs are there." is punctuated correctly. | false | It asks something, so it needs a question mark.
home: Ask a grown-up three questions about when they were your age. Write one down with its question mark.
skill: question-marks

@lesson 1.3 | Joining words | 🔗 | stage
hook: "I was hungry. I ate an apple." Can you join these into one sentence?
learn: Joining words link ideas: and, but, because and so.
learn: "and" adds more: I fed the hens and collected the eggs.
learn: "but" shows a change: I wanted to play, but it rained. "because" gives a reason: I ate an apple because I was hungry.
try: I was tired, so I went to bed :: mcq | Pick the best joining word: I wanted a swim … the pool was closed. | but* | and | because
play: match | Match the two halves | I like apples=and I like pears; I was cold=so I put on a jumper; I stayed inside=because it was raining; I tried hard=but I didn't win
check: mcq | I fed the dog … it was hungry. | because* | but | or
check: mcq | Which joining word gives a reason? | because* | and | but
check: tf | "but" can show that something changed. | true
home: Tell a grown-up about your day using and, but, because and so.
skill: conjunctions

@lesson 1.4 | Nouns and verbs | 🐈 | stage
hook: In "The cat jumps", which word is the thing and which is the action?
learn: A noun names a person, place or thing: cat, school, Mum, apple.
learn: A verb is a doing word: jump, run, eat, sing.
learn: Most sentences need a noun and a verb: "Birds sing."
try: In "Dogs bark", dogs is the noun and bark is the verb :: mcq | Which word is a verb? | swim* | pool | towel
play: sort | Noun, Verb | apple=Noun; run=Verb; teacher=Noun; sleep=Verb; beach=Noun; kick=Verb | Noun or verb?
check: mcq | Which word is a noun? | garden* | dig | quickly
check: mcq | In "The hen pecks the corn", which word is the verb? | pecks* | hen | corn
check: tf | "happy" is a verb. | false | Happy describes a feeling. It isn't a doing word.
home: Find five nouns in your kitchen, then act out five verbs.
skill: nouns-verbs

@lesson 2.1 | Who, what and where | 📖 | stage
hook: Good readers ask questions while they read. Let's try it.
learn: Read this: "Mia ran to the hen house after school. Inside, she found three brown eggs in the straw."
learn: Who is it about? Mia. Where did she go? The hen house. What did she find? Three brown eggs.
learn: The answers are right there in the words.
try: Who ran to the hen house? Mia :: mcq | Where were the eggs? | In the straw* | On the roof | In the kitchen
play: match | Match each question to its answer | Who?=Mia; Where?=The hen house; What did she find?=Three brown eggs; When?=After school
check: mcq | How many eggs did Mia find? | Three* | Two | Ten
check: mcq | What colour were the eggs? | Brown* | White | Blue
check: tf | Mia went to the hen house before school. | false | The story says after school.
home: Read a page of any book with a grown-up and ask each other who, what and where.
skill: reading-literal

@lesson 2.2 | What happened first? | 🌻 | garden
hook: Stories happen in order. What came first?
learn: Read this: "Leo planted a seed. Every day he watered it. A green shoot poked up. Soon there was a tall sunflower."
learn: Words like first, then, next and finally help us follow the order.
learn: Putting events in order helps you understand a story and tell it again.
try: First, Leo planted a seed :: mcq | What happened after he watered it? | A green shoot poked up* | He planted the seed | He picked the flower
play: order | Put Leo's story in order | Leo planted a seed; He watered it every day; A green shoot poked up; A tall sunflower grew
check: mcq | What grew in the end? | A sunflower* | A tree | A carrot
check: mcq | Which word tells you something comes last? | Finally* | First | Then
check: tf | Leo watered the seed only once. | false | He watered it every day.
home: Tell a story you know to a grown-up using first, then, next and finally.
skill: sequencing

@lesson 2.3 | Clues and guesses | 🔍 | stage
hook: Dark clouds, a cold wind and people grabbing umbrellas. What do you think will happen?
learn: Good readers guess what might happen next, using clues.
learn: Read this: "Sam's kite climbed higher and higher. Then the wind dropped."
learn: The clue is that the wind dropped. A good guess is that the kite will come down.
try: Clouds and wind are clues that it might rain :: mcq | "The ice block sat in the hot sun." What will probably happen? | It will melt* | It will grow | It will freeze
play: match | Match each clue to what might happen | The puppy saw a ball=It will chase it; The baby yawned=She will go to sleep; The pot boiled over=A grown-up will turn the heat down; The bell rang=School is out
check: mcq | "Tom's tummy rumbled. He looked at the clock: lunchtime!" What will Tom do next? | Eat lunch* | Go to bed | Brush his teeth
check: mcq | What helps you guess what happens next? | Clues in the story* | The page number | The size of the letters
check: tf | A good guess uses clues from the story. | true
home: Stop halfway through a story and guess what will happen. Were you right?
skill: predicting

@lesson 2.4 | The main idea | 🐝 | garden
hook: If you told a friend what a story was mostly about in one sentence, what would you say?
learn: The main idea is what the writing is mostly about.
learn: Read this: "Bees fly from flower to flower. They drink nectar and carry pollen. They take nectar back to the hive to make honey."
learn: The main idea is that bees collect nectar from flowers to make honey. The other bits are details that tell us how.
try: The main idea is what it's mostly about :: mcq | What are the bee sentences mostly about? | How bees make honey* | Flowers are pretty | Hives are big
play: sort | Main idea, Detail | Bees make honey from flowers=Main idea; Bees drink nectar=Detail; Bees carry pollen=Detail; They take nectar to the hive=Detail | Main idea or detail?
check: mcq | "Hedgehogs sleep in the day. They hunt for bugs at night. Their spikes keep them safe." What is it mostly about? | How hedgehogs live* | Bugs at night | Spiky plants
check: tf | The main idea is the smallest detail in the story. | false | The main idea is what it's mostly about. Details tell us more.
check: mcq | What do details do? | Tell us more about the main idea* | Change the topic | End the story
home: Tell a grown-up the main idea of a favourite book or show in one sentence.
skill: main-idea

@lesson 3.1 | Magic e | 🪄 | stage
hook: Why does "hop" turn into "hope" with just one more letter?
learn: The vowels are a, e, i, o and u.
learn: In hop, cap and kit, the vowel makes a short sound.
learn: Add an e to the end and the vowel says its name: hope, cape, kite. The e stays silent.
try: tap and an e make tape :: mcq | Add an e to "pin". What word do you get? | pine* | pinn | pein
play: match | Add an e and match the new word | cub=cube; not=note; man=mane; rid=ride
check: mcq | Which word has a long a sound? | cake* | cat | can
check: mcq | Add an e to "hop". What do you get? | hope* | hopp | hoep
check: tf | In "kite", the e at the end is silent. | true
home: Find three magic e words in a book.
skill: magic-e

@lesson 3.2 | ck or k? | 🦆 | island
hook: Is it "duk" or "duck"?
learn: At the end of a short word with a short vowel, we usually write ck: duck, sock, back.
learn: After a long vowel sound or another consonant, we write k: cake, milk, park.
learn: Say the word slowly and listen to the vowel.
try: "sack" has a short a, so it ends in ck :: mcq | Which is spelt correctly? | clock* | clok | clokc
play: sort | Ends in ck, Ends in k | duck=Ends in ck; milk=Ends in k; sock=Ends in ck; park=Ends in k; truck=Ends in ck; book=Ends in k | How does it end?
check: mcq | Which word is spelt right? | black* | blak | blac
check: mcq | Which word ends in k, not ck? | milk* | duck | sock
check: tf | "rocket" has ck in it. | true
home: Write a list of five ck words with a grown-up.
skill: ck-k

@lesson 3.3 | More than one | 🦊 | island
hook: One fox, two... foxs or foxes?
learn: Most words add s for more than one: cat, cats. tree, trees.
learn: Words ending in s, x, ch or sh add es: bus, buses. fox, foxes. dish, dishes.
learn: Some words change: one child, two children. One mouse, two mice.
try: box becomes boxes :: mcq | One brush, two… | brushes* | brushs | brushies
play: sort | Add s, Add es | hen=Add s; fox=Add es; egg=Add s; lunch=Add es; dish=Add es; cow=Add s | How do you make more than one?
check: mcq | One child, two… | children* | childs | childes
check: mcq | One match, two… | matches* | matchs | matchies
check: tf | More than one mouse is "mouses". | false | It's mice.
home: Count things at home and say their plurals: plates, cups, boxes.
skill: plurals

@lesson 3.4 | Compound words | 🌈 | garden
hook: Which two words are hiding in "sunflower"?
learn: A compound word is two words joined together: sun and flower make sunflower.
learn: Knowing the two parts helps you spell it and understand it.
learn: Here are more: rainbow, football, toothbrush, cupcake.
try: rain and coat make raincoat :: mcq | Which is a compound word? | goldfish* | golden | gold
play: match | Join the words | tooth=brush; cup=cake; foot=ball; rain=bow
check: mcq | Which two words make "bedroom"? | bed and room* | be and droom | bedr and oom
check: mcq | snow and man make… | snowman* | snowmen | manysnow
check: tf | "butterfly" is a compound word. | true
home: Find three compound words around your house, like doorbell or teapot.
skill: compound-words
