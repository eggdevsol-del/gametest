@lesson 1.1 | Ratios | 🧃 | kitchen
hook: Cordial says 1 part syrup to 4 parts water. How much water for 50 ml of syrup?
learn: A ratio compares amounts. 1:4 means for every 1 of this, 4 of that.
learn: Multiply both sides by the same number to scale up.
try: 1:4 with 50 ml syrup means 200 ml water :: gap | 1:5 with 40 ml syrup needs _ ml water | 200
play: match | Match the ratio to the mix | 1:1=Equal amounts; 1:3=One part to three parts; 2:1=Twice as much of the first; 1:10=A tiny bit of the first
check: gap | Paint mixes 2:3 blue to white. 4 cups of blue needs _ cups of white | 6
check: tf | 1:4 and 2:8 are the same ratio. | true
check: mcq | 3 boys to 2 girls in a group of 10. How many girls? | 4* | 2 | 6
home: Find a ratio on a packet at home.
skill: ratio

@lesson 1.4 | Percent change | 📈 | shares
hook: Prices went up from $4 to $5. Is that a 1% rise?
learn: Percent change = change ÷ original × 100.
learn: $4 to $5 is a $1 change. $1 ÷ $4 = 0.25, so 25%.
try: $10 to $12 is $2 ÷ $10 = 20% :: gap | $20 to $25 = _% increase | 25
play: gen | percent | 3
check: gap | $50 down to $40 = _% decrease | 20
check: mcq | A price rises 10% then falls 10%. It ends… | A bit lower than it started* | Back where it started | Higher
check: tf | Percent change uses the original amount. | true
home: Find an old price for something and work out how much it's risen.
skill: percent

@lesson 2.1 | Probability as a fraction | 🎯 | stage
hook: A bag has 3 red marbles and 7 blue. You grab one without looking. What's the chance it's red?
learn: Probability = the number of ways it can happen ÷ the total number of outcomes.
learn: 3 red out of 10 marbles is 3/10, or 30%.
learn: The probabilities of all the outcomes add up to 1, or 100%.

@lesson 2.2 | Coins have no memory | 🪙 | stage
hook: A coin lands heads 5 times in a row. Is tails "due" next?
learn: Each fair coin flip is independent. It doesn't remember what happened before.
learn: The chance of tails is still 1 in 2, every single time.
learn: Believing a result is "due" is called the gambler's fallacy. It costs gamblers a lot.
play: debate | After 5 heads in a row, should you bet big on tails? | No. Each flip is still 50/50* | Yes. Tails is due | Streaks feel meaningful, and if a coin came up heads hundreds of times you might suspect it wasn't fair. For a fair coin, each flip is still 50/50.
check: mcq | A fair coin lands heads 5 times. The chance of tails next is… | 1 in 2* | Almost certain | 1 in 6
check: tf | A pokie machine is more likely to pay out after a long losing streak. | false | Each spin is random. Machines don't owe you a win.
check: mcq | What's the gambler's fallacy? | Thinking a result is "due" after a streak* | A gambling law | A lucky charm
home: Flip a coin 30 times and record the longest streak. Streaks happen by chance!
skill: probability

@lesson 2.3 | Two dice | 🎲 | stage
hook: Roll two dice and add them. Which total comes up most often?
learn: Two dice have 6 × 6 = 36 possible outcomes.
learn: There are 6 ways to make 7 (1+6, 2+5, 3+4, 4+3, 5+2, 6+1), so 7 is the most likely total: 6 in 36, or 1 in 6.
learn: 2 and 12 have only one way each: 1 in 36.
try: Ways to make 3: 1+2 and 2+1, so 2 out of 36 :: gap | Ways to make 4 with two dice: _ | 3
play: match | Match each total to the number of ways to roll it | 2=1 way; 3=2 ways; 5=4 ways; 7=6 ways
check: mcq | What's the most likely total with two dice? | 7* | 12 | 2
check: gap | Two dice have _ possible outcomes | 36
check: tf | Rolling 12 is as likely as rolling 7. | false | 12 has 1 way. 7 has 6 ways.
home: Roll two dice 36 times and tally the totals. Does 7 win?
skill: probability

@lesson 2.4 | Odds and probability | ⚖️ | stage
hook: A betting ad says "5 to 1". What's the actual chance?
learn: Odds against of 5 to 1 mean 5 ways to lose for every 1 way to win.
learn: That's 1 win out of 6 in total: a probability of 1/6, about 17%.
learn: Betting odds also include the bookmaker's cut, so they're stacked against you.
try: Odds of 3 to 1 against = 1 out of 4 = 25% :: gap | Odds of 4 to 1 against = 1 out of _ | 5
play: match | Match the odds against to the probability | 1 to 1=50%; 3 to 1=25%; 4 to 1=20%; 9 to 1=10%
check: mcq | Odds of 9 to 1 against mean the chance is… | 10%* | 90% | 9%
check: tf | Betting odds include a cut for the bookmaker. | true
check: gap | Odds of 19 to 1 against = _% chance | 5
home: Next time you see odds in an ad, turn them into a percentage.
skill: probability

@lesson 2.6 | Why the house wins | 🏠 | stage
hook: Casinos have bright lights, music and free drinks. Who pays for it all?
learn: Every casino game has a house edge: a small advantage built into the rules.
learn: A roulette wheel with one zero has 37 numbers, but a win pays as if there were 36. The house keeps about 2.7% of every bet on average.
learn: Pokies are set to pay back less than goes in. The longer you play, the more you lose on average.
try: 2.7% of $100 = $2.70 :: gap | 2.7% of $1,000 bet over a night = $_ expected loss | 27
play: scam | Honest fact or gambling hook? | Pokies pay back less than goes in=real; You're due for a win, keep spinning!=scam; Free bonus bet when you deposit $50!=scam; Each spin is random=real; A near miss means you're getting close=scam | Gambling marketing uses near misses, "due" wins and bonuses to keep you playing.
check: mcq | What's the house edge? | An advantage built into the game for the casino* | The edge of the table | A lucky seat
check: tf | Over many bets, the house edge means players lose money on average. | true
check: mcq | A near miss on a pokie means… | Nothing. It's designed to keep you playing* | You'll win soon | The machine is broken
home: Spot a gambling ad and work out which trick it uses.
skill: probability
