@lesson 1.1 | Below zero | 🌡️ | island
hook: It's −3 °C at night and 8 °C at noon. How much warmer is noon?
learn: Negative numbers are less than zero. On a number line they sit to the left of 0.
learn: −3 is bigger than −8, because it's closer to zero.
learn: From −3 up to 8 is 3 up to zero, then 8 more: 11 degrees.
try: From −2 to 5 is 7 :: mcq | Which is bigger? | −4* | −9 | −12
play: order | Smallest first | −10; −4; 0; 3; 7
check: mcq | From −6 °C to 4 °C is a rise of… | 10 degrees* | 2 degrees | −2 degrees
check: tf | −1 is less than −5. | false | −1 is closer to zero, so it's bigger.
check: mcq | −3 + 5 = ? | 2* | −8 | 8
home: Look at the forecast for a cold place. Work out the gap between its high and its low.
skill: negatives

@lesson 1.2 | Adding and subtracting negatives | ↔️ | bank
hook: You owe $5 and then earn $12. Where are you now?
learn: Adding a negative is like taking away: 7 + (−3) = 4.
learn: Taking away a negative is like adding: 7 − (−3) = 10.
learn: On a number line, adding moves right and taking away moves left.
try: −5 + 12 = 7 :: gap | −2 + 9 = _ | 7
play: gen | integers | 5
check: mcq | 6 − (−4) = ? | 10* | 2 | −10
check: mcq | −8 + 3 = ? | −5* | −11 | 5
check: tf | Taking away a negative number makes the answer bigger. | true
home: Keep a pretend bank balance for a week that dips below zero and climbs back.
skill: integer-add

@lesson 1.3 | Multiplying and dividing negatives | ✖️ | bank
hook: What's −4 × 3? And −4 × −3?
learn: A negative times a positive is negative: −4 × 3 = −12.
learn: A negative times a negative is positive: −4 × −3 = 12.
learn: Dividing works the same way: −12 ÷ 3 = −4, and −12 ÷ −3 = 4.
try: −5 × 2 = −10 :: mcq | −6 × −2 = ? | 12* | −12 | −8
play: sort | Positive answer, Negative answer | −3 × 4=Negative answer; −5 × −2=Positive answer; 8 ÷ −2=Negative answer; −9 ÷ −3=Positive answer; 6 × 7=Positive answer | Will the answer be positive or negative?
check: mcq | −20 ÷ 5 = ? | −4* | 4 | −15
check: mcq | −7 × −3 = ? | 21* | −21 | −10
check: tf | Two negatives multiplied together make a positive. | true
home: Explain to a grown-up, in your own words, why a negative times a negative is positive.
skill: integer-multiply

@lesson 2.3 | Simplifying | 🧩 | workshop
hook: x + x + x. Is there a shorter way to write it?
learn: Like terms have the same letter: 3x and 5x are like terms.
learn: Add or take away like terms: 3x + 5x = 8x.
learn: Unlike terms stay apart: 3x + 2y can't be simplified.
try: 4a + 2a = 6a :: mcq | Simplify 7n − 2n | 5n* | 9n | 5
play: match | Match each to its simplest form | x + x + x=3x; 2y + 4y=6y; 5a − a=4a; 3b + 2 + b=4b + 2
check: mcq | Simplify 2x + 3y + 4x | 6x + 3y* | 9xy | 6x + 3
check: mcq | Which pair are like terms? | 4m and 9m* | 4m and 4n | 4m and 4
check: tf | 3x + 2y is the same as 5xy. | false | x and y are unlike terms, so 3x + 2y stays as it is.
home: Explain like terms to someone at home using apples and bananas.
skill: simplify-algebra

@lesson 2.4 | Solving equations | ⚖️ | stall

learn: An equation says two things are equal: 3x + 2 = 17.
learn: Undo each step, doing the same to both sides. Take away 2 from both: 3x = 15.
learn: Divide both by 3: x = 5. Check it: 3 × 5 + 2 is 17.
try: x + 6 = 10, so x is 4 :: gap | Solve 2x = 18. x is _ | 9
play: gen | equation | 5
check: gap | Solve x − 7 = 12. x is _ | 19
check: gap | Solve 4x + 3 = 23. x is _ | 5

home: Make up an equation about something you're saving for, and solve it.
skill: equations

@lesson 3.1 | Percent of an amount | 🏷️ | stall
hook: A shop says 20% off a $60 hoodie. How much do you save?
learn: Percent means "out of 100". 20% is 20 out of 100.
learn: 10% is easy: divide by 10. 10% of $60 is $6.
learn: 20% is double 10%, so $12. 5% is half of 10%, so $3.

@lesson 3.2 | Discounts and increases | 📉 | stall
hook: A $40 game goes up 10%, then goes on sale at 10% off. Is it back to $40?
learn: For a discount, take the percentage off: 25% off $80 is $80 − $20 = $60.
learn: For an increase, add it on: 10% more than $40 is $44.
learn: 10% off $44 is $4.40 off, which makes $39.60. The same percentage of a bigger amount is bigger.
try: 30% off $50 is $35 :: gap | 20% off $90 is $_ | 72
play: gen | discount | 4
check: gap | 10% more than $70 is $_ | 77
check: mcq | Which is cheaper: $100 with 30% off, or $90 with 20% off? | $100 with 30% off* | $90 with 20% off | They're the same
check: tf | Going up 10% and then down 10% gets you back where you started. | false | 10% of the bigger number is more, so you end up a little lower.
home: Find a sale ad and work out the sale price yourself before you read it.
skill: percent-change

learn: Simplify a ratio by dividing both parts by the same number: 10:4 is 5:2.

@lesson 4.2 | Angles in a triangle | 🔺 | workshop
hook: Can a triangle have two right angles?
learn: The three angles in any triangle add up to 180°.
learn: If two angles are 50° and 60°, the third is 180 − 50 − 60 = 70°.
learn: So a triangle can have at most one right angle.
try: 90° + 45° + 45° is 180° :: gap | A triangle has angles of 30° and 80°. The third angle is _° | 70
play: sort | Could be a triangle, Can't be a triangle | 60°, 60°, 60°=Could be a triangle; 90°, 90°, 10°=Can't be a triangle; 100°, 40°, 40°=Could be a triangle; 50°, 50°, 50°=Can't be a triangle; 30°, 60°, 90°=Could be a triangle | Do the angles add up to 180°?
check: gap | 180 − 90 − 35 = _ | 55
check: mcq | An equilateral triangle has three equal angles. Each one is… | 60°* | 90° | 45°
check: tf | A triangle can have two right angles. | false | Two right angles already make 180°, which leaves nothing for the third.
home: Draw a triangle, measure its angles with a protractor and check they add up to 180°.
skill: triangle-angles

@lesson 4.3 | Pythagoras | 📐 | workshop

learn: In a right-angled triangle, the longest side is the hypotenuse. It's opposite the right angle.
learn: Pythagoras: a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse.
learn: For sides of 3 and 4: 9 + 16 = 25, and the square root of 25 is 5.
try: Sides of 6 and 8 give 36 + 64 = 100, so the hypotenuse is 10 :: gap | Sides of 5 and 12 make a hypotenuse of _ | 13
play: gen | pythag | 4
check: gap | 9 + 16 = _ | 25
check: mcq | Which side is the hypotenuse? | The longest side, opposite the right angle* | The shortest side | Any side
check: gap | Sides of 8 and 15 make a hypotenuse of _ | 17
home: Check a corner is square with a tape measure: 30 cm one way, 40 cm the other, and 50 cm across.
skill: pythagoras

@lesson 4.4 | Using Pythagoras | 📺 | home

learn: Screen sizes measure the diagonal, from corner to corner.
learn: A rectangle's diagonal makes two right-angled triangles, so Pythagoras finds it.
learn: A 9 by 12 rectangle has a diagonal of √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15.

check: tf | Pythagoras only works in right-angled triangles. | true
home: Measure the diagonal of a book or a screen, then check it with Pythagoras.
