@lesson 1.1 | The cover letter | ✉️ | workshop
hook: A recruiter spends about 30 seconds on your letter. What will they notice?
learn: Open with the role and why you want it. Then give two or three examples that match what they asked for.
learn: Keep it under a page, and end with a clear next step.
play: order | Build the cover letter in order | Dear Ms Taufa,; I'm applying for the weekend barista role advertised on your website.; Last summer I made about 200 coffees a day at a busy beach kiosk.; I'm reliable, quick to learn and great with regulars.; I'd love to chat. I'm available any time after 3pm. Kind regards, Ava Lee
check: mcq | What should a cover letter match? | What the job ad asks for* | Your favourite movies | Your friends' jobs
check: tf | Specific examples beat vague claims. | true
check: mcq | A cover letter should usually be… | Under a page* | Five pages | One word
home: Draft a cover letter for a real part-time job ad.
skill: applying

learn: If it's not written down, it's hard to prove later.

check: mcq | Best time to read the terms? | Before signing* | After the first bill | Never
home: Read the terms of one subscription your family has.

check: tf | Suggesting a solution helps. | true

@lesson 2.1 | Contract words | 📖 | bank
hook: "Either party may terminate on 4 weeks' notice. The tenant is liable for any breach." Clear?
learn: Contracts use special words. Look them up before you sign.
learn: Terminate means end. A breach is breaking one of the terms. Liable means legally responsible.
learn: A notice period is how much warning you must give before ending the contract.
play: match | Match each contract word to its meaning | Terminate=End the contract; Breach=Breaking one of the terms; Liable=Legally responsible; Notice period=Warning you must give before ending it; Clause=One numbered part of the contract
check: mcq | What does "breach" mean? | Breaking a term of the contract* | A kind of beach | Signing twice
check: tf | It's fine to sign a contract with words you don't understand. | false | Look them up or ask first.
check: mcq | A notice period of 4 weeks means… | You must give 4 weeks' warning before ending it* | It only lasts 4 weeks | You get 4 free weeks
home: Find three of these words in a real contract or set of terms and conditions.
skill: contracts

@lesson 2.2 | Cooling-off periods | ❄️ | townhall
hook: A salesperson knocks on your door and signs you up for a $3,000 water filter. Next day you regret it. Too late?
learn: {nz:In NZ, if a seller comes to your door uninvited or calls you, you usually have 5 working days to cancel under the Fair Trading Act.~au:In Australia, if a seller comes to your door uninvited or calls you, you usually have 10 business days to cancel under the Australian Consumer Law.}
learn: Most other buys, like in a shop, have no cooling-off period. Changing your mind isn't a legal right.
learn: Some contracts include their own cooling-off terms, so read them.
play: sort | Cooling-off applies, Usually no cooling-off | A door-to-door seller signs you up=Cooling-off applies; You buy shoes in a shop and change your mind=Usually no cooling-off; A cold caller sells you a phone plan=Cooling-off applies; You order a pizza online=Usually no cooling-off; A seller knocks to sell a vacuum cleaner=Cooling-off applies; You buy a jacket and later dislike the colour=Usually no cooling-off | Does a cooling-off period apply?
check: mcq | {nz:How long do you usually have to cancel an uninvited door-to-door sale in NZ?~au:How long do you usually have to cancel an unsolicited door-to-door sale in Australia?} | {nz:5 working days~au:10 business days}* | 1 hour | 1 year
check: tf | You can always return something to a shop just because you changed your mind. | false | Change-of-mind returns depend on the shop's own policy.
check: mcq | Who do the door-to-door sales rules cover? | A seller who turns up uninvited* | A shop you walk into | A friend selling raffle tickets
home: Look up the uninvited sales rules on {nz:consumerprotection.govt.nz~au:accc.gov.au}.
skill: consumer

@lesson 2.4 | Jointly and severally | 🏠 | home
hook: You sign a flat lease with two friends. One moves out and stops paying. Who covers their rent?
learn: "Jointly and severally liable" means each of you can be held responsible for the whole amount, not just your share.
learn: If one flatmate stops paying, the landlord can ask the others for all of it.
learn: A guarantor promises to pay if someone else doesn't. Only agree if you could afford to.
play: path | A friend asks you to be guarantor on their car loan. | Say no kindly, or ask for time to think through the risk => good: Smart. If they can't pay, you could owe the whole loan. | Sign straight away to be nice => bad: If they stop paying, the lender can chase you for the lot. | Sign without reading it => bad: You've promised to pay a debt you haven't even read.
check: mcq | What does "jointly and severally liable" mean? | Each person can owe the whole amount* | Everyone only ever pays their share | Nobody owes anything
check: tf | A guarantor may have to pay someone else's debt. | true
check: mcq | Before becoming a guarantor, ask yourself… | Could I afford to pay all of it?* | Will they like me more? | Is the pen nice?
home: Read the liability section of a sample tenancy agreement.
skill: contracts

@lesson 2.5 | Unfair terms | ⚖️ | townhall
hook: A contract says the company can change the price whenever it likes, but you can never cancel. Allowed?
learn: {nz:Under the Fair Trading Act, courts can strike out unfair terms in standard form contracts.~au:Under the Australian Consumer Law, unfair terms in standard form contracts are void, and businesses can be fined for using them.}
learn: A standard form contract is a take-it-or-leave-it one, like a phone plan or a gym membership.
learn: One-sided terms, like only the business being able to cancel or change the deal, can be unfair.
play: scam | Fair term or red flag? | You can cancel with 30 days' notice=real; We can change the price at any time, and you can't cancel=scam; We'll give 30 days' notice of price rises, and you can leave without a fee=real; You must keep paying even if we never provide the service=scam; All fees are listed on page 1=real | One-sided terms can be unfair and may not hold up.
check: mcq | What's a standard form contract? | A take-it-or-leave-it contract written by the business* | One you write yourself | A handshake deal
check: tf | You're always bound by every term a business puts in its contract. | false | Unfair terms in standard form contracts can be struck out.
check: mcq | Which term might be unfair? | Only the business can cancel or change the deal* | You can cancel with notice | Prices are shown clearly
home: Read a phone plan's terms and find what they say about price changes.
skill: contracts

@lesson 2.6 | Before you sign | ✍️ | workshop
hook: The salesperson says, "Just sign here. It's standard." What do you do?
learn: Take your time. Pressure to sign fast is a warning sign.
learn: Read it all, ask about anything unclear, and get promises written in.
learn: Never sign a blank or half-filled form, and keep a copy of what you sign.
play: build | Build your before-you-sign checklist | Take your time, or take it home; Read every page; Look up words you don't know; Ask questions and get the answers in writing; Check the total cost and how to cancel; Sign only when you're happy, and keep a copy
check: mcq | "It's standard, just sign." What should you do? | Read it anyway* | Sign fast | Sign a blank copy
check: tf | Spoken promises are as easy to prove as written ones. | false | Get important promises in writing.
check: mcq | After you sign, you should… | Keep a copy* | Throw it away | Post it online
home: Make a "before I sign" checklist in your phone's notes.
