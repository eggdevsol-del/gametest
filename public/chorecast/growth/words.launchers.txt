@lesson 1.1 | The cover letter | ✉️ | workshop
hook: A recruiter spends about 30 seconds on your letter. What will they notice?
learn: Open with the role and why you want it. Then give two or three examples that match what they asked for.
learn: Keep it under a page, and end with a clear next step.
play: order | Build the cover letter in order | Dear Ms Taufa,; I'm applying for the weekend barista role advertised on your website.; Last summer I made about 200 coffees a day at a busy beach kiosk.; I'm reliable, quick to learn and great with regulars.; I'd love to chat. I'm available any time after 3pm. Kind regards, Ava Lee
check: mcq | What should a cover letter match? | What the job ad asks for* | Your favourite movies | Your friends' jobs
check: tf | Specific examples beat vague claims. | true
check: mcq | A cover letter should usually be… | Under a page* | Five pages | One word
home: Draft a cover letter for a real part-time job ad.
skill: applying

@lesson 1.2 | Reading the fine print | 🔍 | bank
hook: A gym deal says "$1 for the first month". What's the catch?
learn: Read terms for minimum contracts, cancellation fees, automatic renewals and price rises.
learn: If it's not written down, it's hard to prove later.
play: diff | The ad and the contract. Tap what the ad didn't mention. | $1 first month; Cancel any time; Full gym access | $1 first month; 12-month minimum term; $99 cancellation fee; Price rises to $25 a week after month one
check: mcq | Which is a common catch? | A long minimum term* | Free towels | Friendly staff
check: tf | Automatic renewals can keep charging you. | true
check: mcq | Best time to read the terms? | Before signing* | After the first bill | Never
home: Read the terms of one subscription your family has.
skill: contracts

@lesson 1.3 | Tough conversations | 🗝️ | home
hook: Your flatmate hasn't paid their share of power for two months. How do you raise it?
learn: Pick a calm time. Stick to facts. Say how it affects you. Suggest a fix.
learn: "The power bill is $180 and we agreed to split it. I've paid both months. Can we sort a plan?"
play: chat | Raising unpaid bills with a flatmate | them: Hey, what's up? | me: Can we chat about the power bill? I've covered the last two, about $180. Could we set up auto-payments?*/You're a freeloader/Never mind | them: Oh, sorry, I lost track. | me: No stress. Want to pay me back over the next two paydays?*/You always do this/Forget it then
check: mcq | A good way to start a hard talk? | Calmly, with facts* | Yelling | Through a public post
check: tf | Suggesting a solution helps. | true
check: mcq | Which statement sticks to facts? | The bill was $180 and I paid it* | You're useless | Everyone hates you
home: Practise a tricky conversation with a grown-up playing the other side.
skill: communication

@lesson 1.4 | Make a submission | 📝 | townhall
hook: Your council wants to close a skate park. Can you have a say?
learn: Councils and governments ask for public submissions on plans and laws.
learn: A strong submission is short, specific and backed by evidence or experience.
play: order | Build a submission in order | Who you are and why you care; Your main point in one sentence; Evidence or your own experience; What you want the council to do; Thank them for considering it
check: mcq | What makes a submission strong? | Specific points with evidence* | Just being angry | Being very long
check: tf | Young people can make submissions. | true
check: mcq | Where can you often find open consultations? | Your council's website* | A gaming chat | Nowhere
home: Find one open consultation on your council's website.
skill: civics

@lesson 1.5 | Networking | 👋 | stage
hook: You meet someone working your dream job. What do you say?
learn: Introduce yourself in one sentence, ask a good question, then follow up with a thank-you.
learn: Networking is about helping each other, not just asking for jobs.
play: chat | Meeting an engineer at a careers day | them: Hi there! | me: Hi, I'm Ava. I'm in Year 12 and I'm keen on civil engineering. What do you enjoy about it?*/Can I have a job?/Hi | them: I love seeing bridges I designed get built. Want to know how I started? | me: Yes please! And could I email you a question later?*/Nah I'm good/Just give me a job
check: mcq | A good networking opener is… | Your name and a genuine question* | Asking for a job straight away | Silence
check: tf | Following up with a thank-you helps. | true
check: mcq | Networking is mainly… | Building helpful relationships* | Begging | Showing off
home: Write a one-sentence introduction about yourself and your goals.
skill: networking

@lesson 1.6 | Checking claims | 🧪 | online
hook: A viral post says "Scientists prove chocolate cures colds". Share it?
learn: Check the source, look for the original study, and see if trusted outlets report it.
learn: Headlines often exaggerate. Read past them.
play: scam | Trustworthy or misleading? | A health site citing a published study with a link=real; Screenshot of a headline with no source=scam; Official stats from a government agency=real; "Doctors hate this one trick!"=scam | Sources and evidence beat screenshots and hype.
check: mcq | First step with a surprising claim? | Check the source* | Share it | Like it
check: tf | Headlines can exaggerate what a study found. | true
check: mcq | Which is usually more reliable? | An official statistics agency* | An anonymous meme | A comment section
home: Fact-check one viral claim with a grown-up.
skill: critical
