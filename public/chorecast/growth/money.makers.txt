@lesson 1.1 | Why use a bank? | 🏦 | bank
hook: Your gran keeps her savings in a tin. What could go wrong?
learn: Banks keep money safe, let you pay without cash, and can pay you interest.
learn: Cash at home can be lost, stolen or burned, and it never earns interest.
play: debate | Cash in a tin or money in a bank? | A bank keeps it safer and can pay interest* | A tin is better because you can always see it | The tin feels safe because you can touch it. Some people also like that cash stops them overspending.
check: mcq | Which is NOT a usual reason to use a bank? | To get free money for nothing* | To keep money safe | To pay without cash
check: tf | Money in a tin at home earns interest. | false
check: mcq | What can happen to cash kept at home? | It can be lost or stolen* | It grows by itself | The government doubles it
home: Ask a grown-up which bank they use and why they chose it.

@lesson 1.2 | Everyday or savings? | 💳 | bank
hook: Why do most people have more than one bank account?
learn: An everyday account is for spending. It usually comes with a card.
learn: A savings account pays more interest but can be harder to spend from.
learn: Keeping them apart stops savings leaking into daily spending.
play: match | Match each account to what it's good for | Everyday account=Paying for lunch with a card; Savings account=Money for a car in two years; Term deposit=Locking money away for a set time at a set rate; Kids' or youth account=Low or no fees while you're young
check: mcq | Which account usually pays the most interest? | Savings or term deposit* | Everyday account | They all pay the same
check: tf | Keeping savings in a separate account can help you not spend them. | true
check: mcq | What usually comes with an everyday account? | A bank card* | A loan | A house
home: Look at your Chorecast Spend jar and Vault. Which works like an everyday account, and which like a savings account?
skill: banking

@lesson 1.3 | Fees and interest | 💸 | bank
hook: One account pays 3% interest but charges $5 a month. Another pays 1% with no fees. Which wins?
learn: Interest is what the bank pays you. Fees are what you pay the bank.
learn: Always compare both. A high rate can be eaten by fees.
learn: Interest rates are yearly. 3% on $500 is about $15 a year.
try: 2% of $1,000 is $20 a year :: gap | 3% of $500 a year = $_ | 15
play: slider | You save $600 at 3% a year, with a $5 monthly fee. Roughly how much do you end the year with? | 500 | 700 | 558 | $ | 12 | 1
check: mcq | What eats into interest? | Fees* | Deposits | Your card colour
check: gap | 2% of $1,000 for a year = $_ | 20
check: tf | The account with the highest interest rate is always best. | false | Fees can make it worse.
home: Find the monthly fee on one of your family's accounts.
skill: interest

@lesson 1.4 | Keeping your account safe | 🛡️ | online
hook: A text says your bank account is locked. Click to fix it! Do you?
learn: Banks never ask for your password or PIN by text or email.
learn: Scammers copy bank logos and make you rush.
learn: If unsure, call the number on the back of your card.
play: scam | Real or scam? | Your bank: Click here within 1 hour or your account closes=scam; Your card ending 4421 was used at City Bakery for $6.50=real; We need your PIN to verify you. Reply now=scam; Your monthly statement is ready. Log in through our app to view it=real; You have won $500! Pay a $20 fee to claim=scam | Rushing you, asking for a PIN, or asking you to pay to get money are big warning signs.
check: tf | A bank might text you asking for your PIN. | false
check: mcq | A message says you must act in 1 hour. That's… | A classic scam pressure trick* | Normal bank behaviour | A good deal
check: mcq | The safest way to check a weird message from your bank? | Call the number on your card* | Click the link | Reply to the text
home: Set up a strong password or PIN with a grown-up, and never share it.
skill: scams

learn: Several at once can quietly cost hundreds of dollars a year.
learn: Check them every few months. Cancel the ones you don't use.

@lesson 2.2 | Free trials | 🎁 | online
hook: "Free for 30 days!" Then what?
learn: Most free trials need a card. When the trial ends, the paid plan starts by itself.
learn: Set a reminder a few days before the trial ends.
learn: If you won't use it, cancel early. Many keep working until the trial ends anyway.
play: order | Use a free trial without getting stung | Read what it costs after the trial; Note the end date; Set a reminder 3 days before; Decide if you really use it; Cancel in the account settings if you don't
check: mcq | What usually happens when a free trial ends? | The paid plan starts and your card is charged* | It stops and nothing happens | You get another free month
check: tf | Setting a reminder before a trial ends can save you money. | true
home: With a grown-up, find where to cancel a subscription in one app you use.
skill: deals

@lesson 2.3 | The fine print | 🔍 | online
hook: The big print says $39. The small print says something else.
learn: Fine print holds the real rules: the contract length, exit fees, price after the deal, and data limits.
learn: "$39 for the first 6 months" might become $55 after that.
learn: Before you agree, find the total cost for the whole contract, not just the first month.
try: $39 for 6 months, then $55 for 6 months, is $564 a year :: gap | $30 for 6 months, then $50 for 6 months, is $_ a year | 480
play: diff | Read the ad and its fine print. Tap what the fine print adds. | Unlimited data; $39 a month; Cancel any time | Unlimited data, slowed after 50 GB; $39 a month for 6 months, then $55; Cancel any time, $99 exit fee in year one
check: mcq | What should you work out before you sign? | The total cost of the whole contract* | Only the first month's price | Only the colour of the phone
check: tf | "Unlimited" plans can have limits in the fine print. | true
home: Find a phone or internet ad and read its fine print with a grown-up.
skill: deals

@lesson 2.4 | Bundles and deals | 📦 | stall
hook: "Bundle and save!" Do you save if you didn't need half the bundle?
learn: A bundle is a good deal only if you'd buy every part anyway.
learn: Shops make bundles to sell more, not always to save you money.
learn: Compare the bundle with buying just what you need.
play: price | You need a phone case and a charger. Which is the better deal for you? | Case and charger, bought separately:45:2:items; Bundle with case, charger, earbuds and a stand:60:2:items
check: mcq | When is a bundle a good deal? | When you'd buy every part anyway* | Whenever it says "save" | When it has the most items
check: tf | Shops use bundles to get you to spend more. | true
home: Spot a bundle deal and work out with a grown-up whether it really saves money.
skill: deals

@lesson 3.1 | What's a big purchase? | 💻 | stall

check: mcq | Which is usually cheaper overall? | Saving up first* | Borrowing | They always cost the same
check: tf | Borrowing usually costs extra. | true
home: Ask a grown-up about the last big purchase they made. Did they save or borrow?

@lesson 3.2 | How long to save? | 📅 | bank
hook: You want an $800 e-bike. You can save $120 a month. When can you buy it?
learn: Divide the price by what you can save each month. $800 ÷ $120 is about 6.7, so 7 months.
learn: Already have some saved? Take that off first.
learn: To get there sooner, save more, earn more or look for a cheaper option.
try: $600 ÷ $100 a month = 6 months :: gap | $900 ÷ $150 a month = _ months | 6
play: slider | An $800 e-bike. You have $200 saved and can save $100 a month. How many months to go? | 0 | 12 | 6 | months | 0 | 1
check: gap | $500 ÷ $50 a month = _ months | 10
check: mcq | How can you reach a big goal sooner? | Save more, earn more or find a cheaper option* | Wait and hope | Borrow from a stranger
home: Set a big Saving goal in Chorecast, and work out how many paydays it will take.
skill: goals

@lesson 3.3 | New or second-hand? | 🔄 | stall
hook: A new bike is $900. A two-year-old one is $400. Which is smarter?
learn: Second-hand can save a lot, because things lose value fast when they're new.
learn: Check it carefully: does it work, is it damaged, could it be stolen?
learn: Buying from a shop usually gives you more protection if it breaks. Private sales usually don't.
play: debate | Should a teen buy a second-hand phone? | Often yes, if it's checked carefully. It can save a lot of money* | Never. Second-hand is always junk | New often comes with a warranty, which can be worth paying for.
check: mcq | What should you check on a second-hand item? | That it works and isn't stolen* | Only the colour | Nothing
check: tf | Buying privately usually gives you less protection than buying from a shop. | true
home: Compare the new and second-hand price of something you'd like, with a grown-up.
skill: bigbuys

@lesson 4.1 | How scams work | 🎣 | online
hook: Scams come in many shapes. Most follow the same recipe. What is it?
learn: First they contact you: a text, call, email, DM or ad.
learn: Then the hook: a prize, a scare, a deal or a new friend. Then pressure: act now!
learn: Finally they ask for money or details, often by gift cards, crypto or a bank transfer.
play: build | Build the scam recipe | Contact out of the blue; A hook, like a prize or a scare; Pressure to act fast; A request for money or details; A hard-to-trace payment, like gift cards or crypto
check: mcq | What do most scams try to make you do? | Act fast without thinking* | Take your time | Ask a grown-up
check: tf | Scams often ask for payment by gift card or crypto. | true
check: mcq | A message offers a prize but wants a fee first. That's… | A classic scam* | A lucky day | A normal prize
home: Show a grown-up the scam recipe and ask if they've seen one.
skill: scams

@lesson 4.2 | Gift card payments | 🎁 | stall
hook: A caller says you owe tax and must pay today with gift cards. Real?
learn: No real government department, bank or company asks to be paid in gift cards.
learn: Gift cards are like cash. Once you share the code, the money is gone.
learn: If anyone asks you to buy gift cards to pay a bill, it's a scam.
play: scam | Real or scam? | The tax office says pay your tax debt today with gift cards=scam; Your aunt gives you a gift card for your birthday=real; Your "boss" emails asking you to buy gift cards for clients, urgently=scam; A shop sells you a gift card at the counter=real; A friend you met online asks for a gift card to fix their phone=scam | Gift cards are for gifts, never for paying bills or fees.
check: mcq | Someone wants a bill paid with gift cards. That's… | A scam* | Normal | A special deal
check: tf | Once you share a gift card code, the money is usually gone. | true
check: mcq | Who might really ask you to pay with gift cards? | Nobody legitimate* | The tax office | Your bank
home: Next time you're at the shops, find the scam warning near the gift cards.
skill: scams

@lesson 4.3 | Easy money offers | 💰 | online
hook: "Earn $300 a day just liking videos!" They even pay you $10 at first. What's the catch?
learn: Task scams pay a little at first, then ask you to deposit money to "unlock" bigger earnings.
learn: Once you pay in, the money vanishes, and they ask for more.
learn: Real jobs pay you. They never ask you to pay to work.
play: path | An app paid you $10 for liking videos. Now it says deposit $100 to unlock $500. | Stop, don't pay, and tell a grown-up => good: You spotted the trap. The first $10 was bait. | Deposit $100 to unlock the $500 => bad: The money vanishes, and they ask for more to "release" it. | Get friends to join for a bonus => bad: Now your friends lose money too.
check: mcq | A "job" asks you to pay money to start earning. That's… | A scam* | A great offer | Normal
check: tf | Real jobs pay you. They don't ask you to pay to work. | true
check: mcq | Why do task scams pay a little at first? | To make you trust them before they take more* | Because they're generous | By mistake
home: Talk with a grown-up about a too-good-to-be-true offer you've seen online.
skill: scams

@lesson 4.4 | Card fraud | 💳 | bank
