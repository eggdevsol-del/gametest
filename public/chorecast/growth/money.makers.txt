@lesson 1.1 | Why use a bank? | 🏦 | bank
hook: Your gran keeps her savings in a tin. What could go wrong?
learn: Banks keep money safe, let you pay without cash, and can pay you interest.

@lesson 2.3 | The minimum payment trap | 🪤 | bank
hook: Your card bill says "Minimum payment $25". You owe $1,000. Pay $25 and you're fine, right?
learn: The minimum payment keeps you in good standing with the bank, but it barely dents the debt.
learn: Much of a small payment goes on interest, so the balance shrinks very slowly.
learn: Pay as much as you can. The full balance is best.
play: path | You owe $1,000 on a card at 20% a year. The bill shows a $25 minimum. | Pay the full $1,000 => good: No interest, and the debt is gone. | Pay $200 now and keep paying big chunks => ok: You'll pay a bit of interest, but you'll be clear in a few months. | Pay only the $25 minimum each month => bad: About $17 of each payment goes on interest. It would take over five years and cost more than $600 extra.
check: mcq | Why is paying only the minimum risky? | The debt shrinks slowly and costs lots of interest* | The bank closes your account | It's against the law
check: tf | The minimum payment is the amount you should aim to pay. | false | It's the least you can pay. Aim for the full balance.
check: slider | You owe $1,000 at 20% a year. Roughly how much interest is that in one month? | 0 | 50 | 17 | $ | 3 | 1
home: Look at a credit card statement with a grown-up and find the minimum payment line.
skill: debt
