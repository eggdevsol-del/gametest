@lesson 1.1 | Business structures | 🏢 | townhall
hook: Sole trader, partnership or company. Which should a 17-year-old start with?
learn: A sole trader is just you. It's simple, but you're personally responsible for debts.
learn: A company is a separate legal entity. It costs more to run but can protect your personal assets.
learn: {nz:In New Zealand you register companies with the Companies Office.~au:In Australia you'll need an ABN, and companies register with ASIC.}
play: match | Match each structure to its description | Sole trader=Just you, simple, you're responsible for debts; Partnership=Two or more people sharing a business; Company=A separate legal entity with shareholders; Trust=Assets held by trustees for beneficiaries
check: mcq | Simplest way to start a small business? | Sole trader* | A listed company | A trust
check: tf | A sole trader is personally responsible for business debts. | true
check: mcq | {nz:Where are NZ companies registered?~au:What number does an Australian business usually need?} | {nz:The Companies Office~au:An ABN}* | {nz:The post office~au:A phone number} | {nz:Nowhere~au:None}
home: Look up the {nz:Companies Office~au:ABN} website and find how to register.
skill: business

@lesson 1.2 | GST and invoices | 🧾 | bank
hook: Your business charges GST. Whose money is it?
learn: GST is a tax on most goods and services: {nz:15% in New Zealand~au:10% in Australia}.
learn: Registered businesses collect it from customers and pass it on to {nz:IRD~au:the ATO}. It's not your money to spend.
learn: A tax invoice shows the GST and your {nz:GST number~au:ABN}.
try: {nz:$100 + 15% GST = $115~au:$100 + 10% GST = $110} :: gap | {nz:$200 + 15% GST = $_~au:$300 + 10% GST = $_} | {nz:230~au:330}
play: gen | gst | 4
check: mcq | GST you collect belongs to… | {nz:IRD~au:The ATO}, once you pass it on* | You to spend | Your customers
check: tf | A tax invoice shows the GST amount. | true
check: mcq | {nz:What's the GST rate in New Zealand?~au:What's the GST rate in Australia?} | {nz:15%~au:10%}* | {nz:10%~au:15%} | 0%
home: Find the GST line on a receipt from the shop.
skill: tax

@lesson 1.3 | Funding a start-up | 💵 | bank
hook: You need $5,000 to start. Where could it come from?
learn: Options include savings, family, loans, grants and investors.
learn: Loans must be repaid with interest. Investors take a share of your business.
learn: Start small with your own money when you can, and prove the idea first.
play: match | Match the funding to what you give up | Your savings=Money you could spend on other things; Bank loan=Repayments plus interest; Investor=A share of the business; Grant=Time to apply, and rules about how you spend it
check: mcq | What do investors usually get? | A share of the business* | Nothing | Free products only
check: tf | Loans must be repaid with interest. | true
check: mcq | A smart way to start? | Small, with your own money, proving the idea* | Borrow as much as possible | Spend before you have customers
home: Research one grant or programme for young entrepreneurs in your area.
skill: funding

@lesson 1.4 | Consumer law | ⚖️ | townhall
hook: A customer says your product broke after a week. Do you have to fix it?
learn: {nz:The Consumer Guarantees Act says products must be of acceptable quality and match their description.~au:Australian Consumer Law gives customers guarantees that products are of acceptable quality.}
learn: "No refunds" signs don't remove these rights.
play: tf | A "no refunds" sign means customers have no rights. | false | Consumer law guarantees still apply.

home: Read the {nz:Consumer Protection~au:ACCC} page on refunds.

@lesson 1.6 | Businesses that do good | 🌍 | garden
